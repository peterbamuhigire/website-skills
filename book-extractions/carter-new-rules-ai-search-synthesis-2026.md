## What survives critical reading

1. **Plan for the answer journey, not a model brand.** The book separates
   remembered/foundation knowledge, retrieved/current information, and deeper
   reasoning as a useful planning lens. Treat these as response modes, not a
   universal platform taxonomy: models route queries differently and change.
2. **Separate outcomes.** Track entity representation, retrieval/indexing,
   citation or answer contribution, referral, and business outcome as different
   observations. A mention is not a citation; a citation is not a visit; a visit
   is not a qualified lead.
3. **Make the entity unambiguous.** Use plain subject–predicate–object sentences,
   consistent names, explicit offer/audience/location relationships, and linked
   topic coverage. This is a writing and information-architecture heuristic,
   not a keyword or token-manipulation guarantee.
4. **Match content to the job.** Maintain evergreen identity and offer facts;
   publish time-stamped updates when facts change; show expert working, evidence,
   limitations, and original experience for complex decisions; maintain accurate
   off-site profiles, reviews, and community context where they are authentic and
   rights-safe.
5. **Baseline before optimisation.** Combine lead self-report, referrer and
   analytics review, search-console/webmaster evidence, prompt observations, and
   server logs. Each source is partial; do not infer total AI visibility from one.
6. **Experiment in small slices.** Test one topic, page, profile, source,
   measurement field, or agent handoff at a time. Keep a hypothesis, primary
   outcome, trust/accessibility guardrail, stop rule, and rollback path.
7. **Prepare for tasks only when the business has a task.** Visible facts,
   accessible content, current feeds/APIs, clear consent, and bounded actions are
   useful agent-readiness work. MCP, `llms.txt`, markdown mirrors, commerce
   protocols, and custom agents are optional experiments—not default visibility
   requirements.

## Corrections applied to engine guidance

- Do not require a 50-word opening, an FAQ on every page, a fixed monthly update
  cadence, a three-second threshold, or a particular citation lift. These may be
  local acceptance choices when justified, not universal GEO facts.
- Do not call AI search “ranking” when the observation is a mention, citation,
  cited page, referral, sentiment, or conversion. Name the exact measure.
- Do not use unverified book statistics or named tool capabilities as market,
  platform, or model facts. Mark absent evidence `NOT_ASSESSED`.
- For Google Search, retain the official position that core SEO, crawlability,
  helpful people-first content, technical eligibility, and Search Console
  measurement remain the foundation; Google says `llms.txt`, artificial
  chunking, special AI markup, and inauthentic mentions are not requirements.
  See the [Google generative AI guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide).
- Treat Bing AI Performance as a documented public-preview measurement surface,
  not a ranking or authority score; see the [Bing announcement](https://blogs.bing.com/webmaster/February-2026/Introducing-AI-Performance-in-Bing-Webmaster-Tools-Public-Preview).
- Keep OpenAI crawler decisions separate: `OAI-SearchBot` search inclusion,
  `GPTBot` potential training, and `ChatGPT-User` user-triggered access are not
  interchangeable controls; see [OpenAI's crawler documentation](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/bots).
