# Message Mining And Proof

Use this reference before writing or revising any high-stakes page:

- home
- service
- pricing
- landing
- campaign page
- email capture or waitlist page

Primary sources:

- Joanna Wiebe, *Where Stellar Messages Come From*
- Ann Handley, *Everybody Writes*

## Core rule

The market writes the best draft first.
Your job is to find it, organise it, and sharpen it.

## Message-mining sources in order of value

1. customer interviews
2. recorded sales calls
3. proposal objections
4. onboarding forms
5. testimonials and case-study interviews
6. support and WhatsApp threads
7. competitor reviews
8. community threads where buyers discuss the problem

## Four message buckets

Sort findings into:

1. problem language
2. outcome language
3. trust language
4. objection language

Each final page should use material from all four.

## The 20-35 per cent rule

Do not try to persuade everyone.
Write for the right-fit segment most likely to act, not the whole market.

Translate that into page decisions:

- qualify the audience early
- state who the offer is for and not for
- avoid neutral headlines that offend nobody and move nobody

## Proof extraction method

When you mine testimonials, case studies, or interviews, pull:

- before-state
- failed alternatives
- why they chose this option
- measurable change
- emotional change
- time-to-result
- who specifically benefited

Weak proof:

- "They were great to work with."

Strong proof:

- "Referrals stopped asking whether we were legitimate, and our average deal size rose within a quarter."

## Objection library

Maintain a running objection list for each offer:

- price
- trust
- speed
- fit
- internal effort required
- risk of switching
- fear of looking foolish
- fear of hidden costs

Handle the objection where it naturally appears on the page.

## Make proof local to the claim

Do not quarantine proof at the bottom of the page.
For each important claim, place one proof element in the same section or immediately after it:

- a number
- a named example
- a short quote
- a process detail
- a comparison
- a risk-reversal line

## Output standard

Every major page should leave behind:

- a message bank
- a proof bank
- an objection bank
- a shortlist of phrases worth testing in headlines, subheads, and CTAs
