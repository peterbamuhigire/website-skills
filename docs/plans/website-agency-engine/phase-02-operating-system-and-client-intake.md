# Phase 2 - Operating System and Client Intake

## Objective

Build the agency operating system that governs how projects enter, qualify, scope, start, and move
through the repository. This is the phase that converts "we have skills" into "we have a machine."

## Why This Phase Matters

Premium work breaks when intake is weak. Most website projects fail long before design or code:

- wrong client fit
- poor discovery
- unclear goals
- missing content
- hidden decision-makers
- unrealistic budgets
- no approval discipline

This phase prevents those failures.

## Core Outcomes

- create a standard intake and qualification process
- define mandatory discovery inputs before a build starts
- establish project stage gates
- standardize proposal, kickoff, and decision capture

## Workstreams

### 1. Client qualification model

Create a scorecard that rejects weak projects early.

Required factors:

- budget suitability
- decision-maker access
- urgency level
- business maturity
- content readiness
- trust and compliance demands
- growth intent

Leads that cannot satisfy the minimum bar should move to:

- a referral
- a lower-tier offer
- a paid strategy session

### 2. Discovery system

Create a repeatable discovery package that collects:

- business model
- target audience
- main conversion goals
- service or offer structure
- competitors
- trust assets
- brand constraints
- existing analytics and SEO condition
- technical constraints

This discovery pack should feed directly into:

- `brand-alignment`
- `design-reference`
- `sector-strategies`
- `content-writing`
- `page-builder`
- `seo`

### 3. Standard project folders and artifacts

For each new client project, define the canonical artifact set:

- discovery summary
- positioning summary
- sitemap and page goals
- offer and CTA hierarchy
- style brief
- content inventory
- SEO brief
- launch checklist

### 4. Stage-gate workflow

Introduce explicit decision gates:

1. Qualified lead
2. Paid discovery or signed proposal
3. Strategy approved
4. Design system approved
5. Content and page structure approved
6. Build approved for QA
7. Launch approved
8. Post-launch measurement active

Each gate needs:

- owner
- required artifacts
- approval method
- rollback rule if incomplete

### 5. Proposal and kickoff templates

Build a premium proposal template that includes:

- business problem
- strategic recommendation
- scope definition
- delivery method
- timeline
- investment
- assumptions
- exclusions
- approval model

Kickoff should then translate the sold scope into internal operating facts.

## Repository Outputs Required

- intake questionnaire templates
- lead qualification scorecard
- premium proposal template
- kickoff template
- discovery-to-build artifact map
- project stage-gate checklist under `docs/` or a dedicated operations area

## Success Criteria

- new projects start with the same minimum information every time
- strategy work is not skipped because a client is in a hurry
- scope, assumptions, and exclusions are visible before build work
- low-quality leads are filtered out without wasting deep delivery time
- handoffs between business and production are explicit

## Risks to Avoid

- beginning production before discovery is complete
- letting WhatsApp or email threads become the source of truth
- accepting fixed-price work without clear exclusions
- letting clients change direction after approval without formal re-scoping
- treating missing content as a minor issue

## Reading Material To Buy And Provide At This Stage

- *Managing the Professional Service Firm* by David Maister: operating discipline and client management
- *Book Yourself Solid* by Michael Port: qualification, trust, and service selling basics
- *SPIN Selling* by Neil Rackham: discovery quality and diagnostic selling
- *The Checklist Manifesto* by Atul Gawande: why stage gates and checklists matter in delivery systems
