# Recommended Books

A consolidated reading list mapped to the 12 phases of this plan. Every book named in
an individual phase document is repeated here, organised so the operator can see the
full reading commitment at a glance and so a new operator entering the certification
programme can plan a syllabus.

The reading rule is the rule already established in `docs/evaluation/2026-04-12/suggested-reading.md`:

> Any future reading should be selected to solve a known enforcement or scaling gap.
> The repository already has enough theory. The next reading should improve system
> behaviour.

Books appear in the section of the phase that needs them. A book that supports more
than one phase appears once in its primary phase and is cross-referenced in later
phases. No book is listed without a reason.

## Phase 1 - Agency Positioning and Offer Architecture

- *Win Without Pitching* by Blair Enns - the operating philosophy of premium-only positioning.
- *Value-Based Fees* by Alan Weiss - the canonical reference for the pricing logic.
- *Built to Sell* by John Warrillow - the founder-independent service business model.
- *The Four Conversations* by Blair Enns - the conversation architecture of premium sales.
- *Flawless Consulting* by Peter Block - the contracting and discovery foundation.
- *Secrets of Business Value* by Tony Anglin - exit-grade discipline applied early.

## Phase 2 - Operating System and Client Intake

- *The Checklist Manifesto* by Atul Gawande - the philosophy of repeatable intake.
- *The E-Myth Revisited* by Michael E. Gerber - systemising service work end-to-end.
- *Getting Things Done* by David Allen - operator-level capture and review discipline.
- *Atomic Habits* by James Clear - rituals and triggers for repeated process adherence.

## Phase 3 - Strategy and Research Engine

- *Hooked* by Nir Eyal - audience and behaviour modelling that supports strategy briefs.
- *Building a StoryBrand* by Donald Miller - audience-and-message structure.
- *Obviously Awesome* by April Dunford - positioning as a strategic, not marketing, exercise.
- *The Mom Test* by Rob Fitzpatrick - the discovery interview technique.
- *Jobs To Be Done* by Anthony Ulwick - structured customer-need research.

## Phase 4 - Design System and Build Standardisation

- *Refactoring UI* by Adam Wathan and Steve Schoger - the visual discipline foundation.
- *Atomic Design* by Brad Frost - the component architecture foundation.
- *Practical Typography* by Matthew Butterick - the typography discipline foundation.
- *Thinking with Type* by Ellen Lupton - the typographic theory the type system rests on.
- *Don't Make Me Think* by Steve Krug - the usability discipline that informs every component.

## Phase 5 - Conversion, Content, and SEO Engine

- *The Copywriter's Handbook* by Robert Bly - the canonical reference for headline, body, and CTA.
- *The Adweek Copywriting Handbook* by Joseph Sugarman - the slippery-slide and persuasion model.
- *Everybody Writes* by Ann Handley - the editorial discipline for content production.
- *Made to Sell* by Christopher Spenn - the conversion-page architecture.
- *Product-Led SEO* by Eli Schwartz - the strategy-first SEO model.

## Phase 6 - Delivery, QA, and Launch Operations

- *Continuous Delivery* by Jez Humble and David Farley - the philosophy that shapes the launch runbook.
- *The Phoenix Project* by Gene Kim, Kevin Behr, and George Spafford - the operational frame for launch reliability.
- *The DevOps Handbook* by Gene Kim, Jez Humble, Patrick Debois, and John Willis - the practical reference for the launch and rollback workflow.
- *Release It!* by Michael Nygard - the failure-mode catalogue that shapes the rollback discipline.

## Phase 7 - Proof, Authority, and Lead Generation

- *Content Inc.* by Joe Pulizzi - the audience-first content business model.
- *Show Your Work!* by Austin Kleon - the public-knowledge philosophy that the case-study system rests on.
- *They Ask, You Answer* by Marcus Sheridan - the publishing-as-trust model.
- *Expert Secrets* by Russell Brunson - the authority frameworks (used selectively for premium B2B).
- *Made to Stick* by Chip Heath and Dan Heath - the case-study writing discipline.
- *Authority* by Nathan Barry - the publishing mechanics that compound into authority.

## Phase 8 - Retainers, Automation, and Revenue Expansion

- *Million Dollar Consulting* by Alan Weiss - the retainer pricing and value framing.
- *The Automatic Customer* by John Warrillow - subscription and retainer business models.
- *Hooked* by Nir Eyal (re-read) - the engagement model that retainers depend on.
- *The Hard Thing About Hard Things* by Ben Horowitz - the discipline of growing a recurring-revenue business.

## Phase 9 - Scale, Team Enablement, and Governance

- *The E-Myth Revisited* by Michael E. Gerber (re-read) - the systemisation foundation.
- *Traction* by Gino Wickman - the operating cadence that governance rests on.
- *Principles* by Ray Dalio - the decision-quality model.
- *High Output Management* by Andrew S. Grove - the management and training reference.
- *Multipliers* by Liz Wiseman - team-design for quality amplification.

## Phase 10 - Hard Enforcement and Visual QA Gates

- *High Performance Browser Networking* by Ilya Grigorik - the canonical reference for performance budgets and network reality.
- *Web Performance in Action* by Jeremy Wagner - practical performance engineering including budget enforcement.
- *Inclusive Components* by Heydon Pickering - pattern-by-pattern accessibility for the exact components a static site uses.
- *Accessibility for Everyone* by Laura Kalbag - organisational and operational view of accessibility as engineering.
- *Form Design Patterns* by Adam Silver - form accessibility in practical depth.
- *Refactoring UI* by Adam Wathan and Steve Schoger (re-read) - the visual rubric foundation.
- *Continuous Delivery* by Jez Humble and David Farley (re-read) - the gated-pipeline philosophy.
- *Accelerate* by Nicole Forsgren, Jez Humble, and Gene Kim - the evidence that gates correlate with both quality and speed.
- *The Tangled Web* by Michal Zalewski - the security model the security gate rests on.
- *Building Secure and Reliable Systems* by Heather Adkins et al. (Google SRE) - security gates and reliability together.

## Phase 11 - World-Class Operating Discipline

- *The E-Myth Revisited* by Michael E. Gerber (re-read) - operator-independence as a property.
- *High Output Management* by Andrew S. Grove (re-read) - training and certification design.
- *Multipliers* by Liz Wiseman (re-read) - quality amplification through team design.
- *An Elegant Puzzle* by Will Larson - organisational architecture for small teams.
- *Trustworthy Online Controlled Experiments* by Ron Kohavi, Diane Tang, and Ya Xu - the canonical experimentation reference.
- *You Should Test That* by Chris Goward - practical CRO programme design.
- *Lean Analytics* by Alistair Croll and Benjamin Yoskovitz - choosing the right metric per stage.
- *Observability Engineering* by Charity Majors, Liz Fong-Jones, and George Miranda - the engineering view of telemetry.
- *Site Reliability Engineering* edited by Betsy Beyer et al. - alerting philosophy and measurable operations.
- *Docs for Developers* by Jared Bhatti et al. - the documentation style guide and drift-eradication reference.
- *Every Page Is Page One* by Mark Baker - drift-resistant reference writing.
- *Practical Typography* by Matthew Butterick (re-read) - the rubric backbone for design-quality scoring.

## Phase 12 - African Authority Engine

- *Show Your Work!* by Austin Kleon (re-read) - the public-knowledge-commons philosophy.
- *Built to Sell* by John Warrillow (re-read) - founder-independent authority, not only delivery.
- *Authority* by Nathan Barry (re-read) - the mechanics of publishing into authority.
- *The Brand Gap* by Marty Neumeier - the strategic frame for category leadership.
- *Made to Stick* by Chip Heath and Dan Heath (re-read) - case-study writing that travels.
- *They Ask, You Answer* by Marcus Sheridan (re-read) - publishing-as-trust at continental scale.
- *Africa's Business Revolution* by Acha Leke, Mutsa Chironga, and Georges Desvaux (McKinsey) - market context for African premium positioning.
- *The Mobile Frontier* by Rachel Hinman - mobile-first thinking grounded in real global constraints.
- *Inclusive Design Patterns* by Heydon Pickering - the accessibility reference re-read in the African multilingual and assistive-tech context.
- *Designing Across Cultures* (collected essays from Smashing Magazine, A List Apart, and equivalents) - cross-cultural design literacy.
- GSMA Mobile Money programme publications - primary-source grounding for the mobile money UX reference; sourced from gsma.com/mobilefordevelopment/.
- Academic papers on M-Pesa UX, USSD adoption, and African digital identity (Mendoza-Denton et al., Suri and Jack, World Bank Findex reports) - sourced via Google Scholar.

## Cross-Cutting and Optional Reading

These books support more than one phase but do not need to be assigned to a single one.

- *Tools and Weapons* by Brad Smith - the policy and trust context that shapes premium engagements.
- *The Design of Everyday Things* by Donald Norman - the foundation of usability that informs every craft skill.
- *Universal Principles of Design* by William Lidwell, Kritina Holden, and Jill Butler - the cross-discipline pattern catalogue.
- *Don't Make Me Think* by Steve Krug (re-read) - the usability discipline applied to all phases.
- *The Practice* by Seth Godin - the cadence of professional creative output that the publishing rhythm depends on.

## How To Use This Reading List

- Operators in the certification programme are assigned the Phase 10 and Phase 11 lists
  as required reading before the practical exam.
- Senior operators (strategists and lead designers) are assigned the Phase 1, 7, and 12
  lists as required reading before they take a discovery or strategy seat.
- The QA and launch operator is assigned the Phase 6, 10, and 11 lists.
- Re-reads are flagged because the same book reads differently at different phases of
  the operator's growth.
- A new book added to the engine must replace or sit beside an existing book with a
  written rationale; the reading list is not allowed to grow without that justification.
