# Diagnostic Entry Offers

Parent skill: [authority-offers](../SKILL.md). Read when choosing the first
offer a new prospect meets, when designing a niche opt-in and the booking step
behind it, or when deciding whether a low-price offer helps or harms the core
programme.

`offer-ladder.md` sets friction and ascension rules, and
`../../agency-positioning/references/premium-audit-offers.md` defines the
agency's paid website audits. This file adds the governing rule for entry
offers, the minimum funnel for a niche agency, and a reputation-and-website
audit for founder-led and professional-services buyers.

## 1. The rule: an entry offer is a diagnostic path, never a cheap version

An entry offer exists to reveal the prospect's problem and lead to the core
programme. It must not be a smaller, cheaper edition of the core service.

| Passes | Fails |
|---|---|
| A paid audit whose findings the core programme fixes | A "starter website" below the market floor |
| A niche checklist that leads to a booked review | A monthly "basic SEO" plan that never ascends |
| A workshop that ends with a diagnostic booking | A discounted build to "get a foot in the door" |

**Why.** Low-price products tend to attract buyers who stay low-price, and
they consume the same sales effort as the core offer. Many agencies stall
selling small items that never ascend.

**Test each entry offer:**

1. What does the buyer learn about their problem?
2. Which core-programme outcome does the finding point to?
3. What is the booked next step, and who owns it?
4. Would a buyer who takes only this offer think they have "had the service"?
   If yes, redesign it.

## 2. The minimum funnel for a niche agency

Build one of each, not many:

1. **Clear target.** One niche (see
   `../../agency-positioning/references/niche-fit-test-and-market-sizing.md`).
2. **Opt-in asset.** A niche **cheat sheet**: high desire, low commitment,
   specific. Title pattern: "The [Year] [Niche] Website and Enquiry Checklist"
   or "[Number] Things Every [Niche] Website Needs Before [Season]".
3. **Appointment step.** The opt-in thank-you page and the delivery message
   both invite a booking: "Most [niche] owners want to understand this but
   would rather have an expert do it. Book 20 minutes and I'll show you how
   we'd apply it to your site."
4. **Hot-lead follow-up.** A short, pre-written sequence for people who opt in
   or book: delivery, one useful follow-up, one client story, a booking reminder.

**Cheat-sheet specification:**

- One page, or two at most; printable and readable on a phone.
- Written for one niche, in its trade language and seasons.
- Each item is checkable by the owner in minutes.
- Ends with one call to action: book the review.

**Consent and data.** Collect only the fields you need to qualify (name,
organisation, role, phone or email). State what you will send and how to stop
it. In Uganda, consent is required to collect personal data and written
objections to direct marketing must be honoured within 14 days (register
PL-01). In Kenya, direct marketing needs consent and a free, simple opt-out,
and objections are absolute (register PL-02). Do not add bought or scraped
contacts to the list.

## 3. Reputation and website audit (founder-led and professional-services buyers)

For law firms, clinics, consultancies, schools and other buyers whose leader's
reputation drives enquiries, pair the website audit with a reputation audit.
It diagnoses whether the founder and firm are findable, credible and distinct
before any build is proposed.

**Instruments:**

| Instrument | Method | Output |
|---|---|---|
| Name search | Search the leader's and the firm's names; review the first three pages, images and news | Share of results the firm controls or that are favourable |
| Differentiation check | List the firm's claims; mark each as common to competitors or distinct (see `../../agency-positioning/references/points-of-difference-and-pitch-lines.md`) | Short list of real points of difference |
| Stakeholder words | Ask 6–10 clients or peers for three to five words describing the firm and one improvement | Repeated words; gap from intended position |
| Competitor scoring | Score the firm and up to three competitors on what buyers value (expertise, speed, responsiveness, local knowledge) | Where the firm stands clear |
| Website credibility | Proof, bios, method, contact path, speed and accessibility | Findings sheet |

**Deliverables:** findings sheet, differentiation statement, three priority
fixes, and a recommended next step into the core website or authority
programme.

**Local adaptation.** In cultures where people avoid direct criticism of
seniors, gather stakeholder words through the agency as a third-party
interviewer or an anonymous form, and ask indirect questions ("What do people
ask you about the firm?"). Regulated professions (advocates, doctors,
accountants) may face advertising and testimonial restrictions from their
professional bodies; check the current rules before recommending public
claims or testimonials.

## 4. Measurement

For each entry offer, track: opt-ins or purchases, bookings from them, shows,
core-programme wins within six months, and revenue from those wins. An entry
offer that produces many opt-ins but few bookings is failing its job.
