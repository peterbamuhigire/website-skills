# Price Presentation and Choice Close

Parent skill: [premium-sales-conversation](../SKILL.md). Read when preparing
the part of a sales meeting where the solution and price are presented, when a
prospect asks you to "send something over", when deciding whether to state a
price range early, or when rewriting a weak close.

`premium-pricing-defence.md` fixes the anchors before the call and
`sales-process-and-takeaway-selling.md` sets out the full six-step sale. This
file covers the presentation order, the early price-range check, the close, and
what happens when a proposal is requested.

## 1. The four-step offer order

Present in this order, in conversation rather than slides:

1. **The problem.** Restate the problem in the prospect's words, confirmed
   earlier in the meeting.
2. **What it costs them.** Show how the problem reaches their revenue, margin,
   workload or reputation, in their numbers where possible.
3. **How we solve it.** The mechanism and the causal chain: "we change
   [cause], so [leading indicator] rises, which brings [outcome]."
4. **The price.** Only now, framed against steps 2 and 3.

Presenting price before value invites comparison on price alone.

## 2. Exception: pre-disqualify with a range

When size signals suggest the prospect probably cannot afford the programme
(very small team, no current marketing spend, "we just rely on word of
mouth"), state the range early, before investing hours in diagnosis:

"Before we go further, it's fair to tell you where we sit. Our work is at the
upper end of the market because of the results it's built for, so programmes
run from [UGX X] to [UGX Y] a month [or: projects from UGX X]. Is that within
range for you right now?"

This is respectful, saves both sides time, and is consistent with anchoring
early in `premium-pricing-defence.md`. If the answer is no, move to
`no-fit-and-referral-scripts.md`.

## 3. Six building blocks of a convincing presentation

Check the presentation against these before the meeting:

| Block | Test | Language cue |
|---|---|---|
| Diagnosis | Did we understand this prospect before prescribing? | "Before I suggest anything, help me understand how [process] works today." |
| Customisation | Is a proven core woven with their data, words, competitors and local context? | "Because most of your parents enquire from a phone at night, we would start with…" |
| Honesty | Have we named our limitation and its mitigation up front? | "One honest limitation: we don't run your advertising. Here is who does, and how we work with them." |
| Value | Does each point add accepted value, logical and emotional? Drop points the prospect does not care about | "Is that important to you, or a nice-to-have?" |
| Proof | Is every claim backed by evidence we trust and can show? | "Here is the before-and-after from [client], with their permission." |
| Pictures | Can they see the outcome? | "Picture this: the front desk stops fielding the same fee question forty times a week." |

Keep a rehearsed core, never a canned deck. Do not present slide by slide;
owners who read their slides aloud tend not to close. Visuals support the
conversation; they do not run it.

## 4. The choice close

Narrow to two or three options, recommend one, and ask the prospect to choose.
Do not build an à-la-carte list in the meeting.

- "There are two sensible ways to start: [Option A] or [Option B]. I
  recommend [A] because [reason tied to their goal]. Which suits you better?"
- **Assumptive next step:** "Here's how we'll get started: we sign the
  engagement letter this week, run the content workshop on [date], and you'll
  see the page plan by [date]."
- **Reassurance for a hesitant buyer:** "It's normal to hesitate before a
  decision like this. Here is what protects you: [stage gate, exit point,
  paid discovery first]."

Options follow `../../agency-positioning/references/pricing-rules.md` (options
differ in scope, not quality, and one is recommended).

**Pre-close checks:**

- "How does this compare with what you had in mind?"
- "On a scale of 1 to 10, how ready are you to go ahead? … What would move that
  to a 10?" The answer names the real objection.

**Weak closes to remove:** "So, what do you think?", "Let me know if you're
interested", "Any thoughts?", "I'll send you a proposal and you can have a
look."

## 5. When they say "send me something"

Many prospects ask for a document instead of deciding. A proposal sent without
a booked follow-up starts a chase. Before the call ends:

1. Ask what the decision depends on and who else decides.
2. Agree a **decide-by date**: "Can we agree to decide one way or the other by
   [date]?"
3. **Book the walk-through meeting** in both calendars, with the other
   decision-makers invited.
4. Send the document the same day. The proposal is a follow-up to a
   conversation, never the close itself.

**Follow-up sequence between the meeting and the walk-through (up to 14 days),
sent with the prospect's agreement:**

| Day | Content |
|---|---|
| 0 | Programme overview, the two options side by side, agreement and payment method |
| 2 | Short founder video: what is included and how we work |
| 4, 7, 10 | One client story each, matched to the prospect's sector, with permission |
| 12 | "Your first 90 days with us": what happens, when, who |
| 14 | The pre-booked decision call |

Commercial wording, terms and the proposal document itself belong to the
proposal engine (proposal-skills). This skill owns the conversation and the
booked next step; hand over the call summary, the agreed options and the
decide-by date.

## 6. Language and habits to avoid

- Tool-first pitches ("we do SEO, UX, hosting…") to owners who want outcomes.
- "Just checking in" follow-ups with no value and no decision point.
- Invented scarcity. Say "we take on [n] builds a quarter" only if it is true
  and enforced.
- Discounting in the room. See `premium-pricing-defence.md`.
- Pressure beyond the facts, or pushing a client into debt to buy.

## 7. Worked rewrite (illustrative)

Weak: "So that's our proposal. What do you think?"

Strong: "There are two sensible ways to start: the full rebuild in ten weeks,
or a four-week launch of the three pages that bring most enquiries, with the
rest in phase two. I recommend the four-week launch because your admissions
window opens in March. Which fits your budget cycle better? … Good. Here's how
we'll get started."
