# Sales Process And Takeaway Selling

Parent skill: [premium-sales-conversation](../SKILL.md). Read when running or scripting a consultative sale end to end: attracting prospects instead of chasing them, gaining permission, presenting the offer, closing, preventing buyer's remorse, handling wavering prospects, or diagnosing why a sales team under-performs. The patterns apply to the agency's own sales and to client businesses whose websites feed a sales team.

## 1. Position, do not prospect

Top sellers are sought out; average sellers chase. Build positioning on four pillars:

1. **Writing:** articles, reports, books, newsletters in the buyer's own media.
2. **Speaking:** conferences, associations, webinars, podcasts.
3. **Publicity:** interviews, media appearances, quoted commentary.
4. **Expert authority:** being known as the specialist in a narrow niche.

Test: if the prospect sees "just another consultant, agent, or rep", positioning has failed.

## 2. The welcome-guest lead process

Replace cold calling with lead generation:

1. Run an advertisement or post that offers useful information, never a product pitch.
2. Offer a guide, report, checklist, recording, or diagnostic.
3. The prospect raises a hand to receive it.
4. Deliver it with a follow-up sequence.
5. The sequence qualifies the prospect and positions the seller as the expert.
6. When ready, the prospect contacts the seller.

Rules:

- The headline carries most of the advertisement's effect; write and test many.
- Ask for enough detail at opt-in (role, organisation, phone) to qualify, while keeping friction proportionate to the value of the asset. Collect only data you need and have consent to use.
- Channels: trade media, association lists, direct mail postcards, search and social advertising leading to a landing page and lead magnet.

## 3. The six-step sale

| Step | What to do | Failure it prevents |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Permission to sell | Obtain explicit agreement to discuss the offer ("Yes, show me"). If refused, withdraw gracefully and reposition. | Being treated as a pest. |
| 2. The offer | State the specific offer: price, terms, timeline, deliverables. Present it; do not hint. | Vague proposals that invite haggling. |
| 3. The presentation | Tailor to what discovery revealed: how it solves their stated problem, the process, the proof, the hidden benefits. | Generic pitches. |
| 4. Emotional logic | Engage the emotional driver (fear of loss, status, relief, hope, pride, legacy), then give the logical justification (return, guarantee, comparison) the buyer will use to defend the decision to others. | Decisions that collapse under scrutiny. |
| 5. The close | Ask for the order with a commitment question. | Conversations that end without a decision. |
| 6. The morning after | Within 24 hours: welcome message, reaffirm the decision (ideally with third-party endorsement), restate benefits, state what happens next, ask for a referral or testimonial when appropriate. | Buyer's remorse, cancellations, refunds, lost referrals. |

### Closing questions

- **Assumptive:** "When would you like us to start?"
- **Alternative choice:** "Would the standard or the extended option suit you better?"
- **Qualification:** "This may not suit everyone; let us check you meet the criteria."
- **Sense check:** "Does this make sense for you?" (humble and effective)

## 4. Takeaway selling

What is chased runs away; what is withdrawn is pursued. When a prospect wavers, reduce the pressure by making the offer conditional:

- **Disqualification criteria:** "This works only for firms that [condition]. Is that you?"
- **Real capacity limits:** "We take on [n] new clients each quarter." Only if true.
- **Walk-away:** "It sounds as if we are not the right fit. I would rather tell you now."
- **Confirmation after yes:** "Before we proceed, can you confirm you meet [criteria]?"

Rules:

- The more genuinely selective the offer, the more right-fit prospects want in.
- Every takeaway must be sincere; be prepared to walk away.
- The choice is between being busy with poor-fit clients or selective with good ones.

## 5. Proof

Order of typical strength (adjust by sector):

1. Named testimonials with outcomes and timeframes.
2. Visual before-and-after evidence.
3. Statistical proof from records.
4. Third-party validation: media, awards, endorsements.
5. Demonstration, live or recorded.
6. Guarantee (the seller carries the risk).
7. Client names and associations.

**How much proof:** slightly more than feels comfortable. Under-proof loses more sales than over-proof. Stop when proof becomes generic clutter rather than specific evidence.

## 6. Price conversations

- Never apologise for price.
- Never let the buyer compare you like-for-like with a cheaper rival; change the frame (different bundle, guarantee, timeline, outcome measure).
- Sell the fee as buying a larger return ("[fee] now to recover [verified gain] this year").
- Add value instead of cutting price: inclusions that cost little (templates, audits, extra sessions) or cost nothing (access, recognition, community).
- **Premiums at the close:** a valuable inclusion introduced at the decision point can close a difficult sale; it must be real and offered consistently, not as a pressure trick.
- Right answers to price resistance: re-sell value, reframe the comparison, add an inclusion, or walk away. Not a price cut.

## 7. Listening and discovery

- Aim to listen far more than you talk.
- Mirror: restate their point in their words.
- Pause after they finish; they will often say more.
- Take notes; it forces listening and shows respect.
- Ask "Tell me more about that."
- Ask deeper questions than expected: "What would have to be true for this to work?"
- Watch for signals in word choice and hesitation.

## 8. Preparation and presence

- **Negative preparation:** before every meeting, list what could go wrong (objections, technical faults, competitor counters, personality clashes) and prepare responses.
- **Expectation:** prepare well enough to expect the sale; anxiety leaks into tone and body language.
- **Remember why you are there:** the purpose of the meeting is a decision; rapport and education serve that purpose.
- **Personal packaging:** dress, grooming, email signature, profile photograph, office, and documents all communicate credibility. Match what the buyer expects of a trustworthy provider in the role.
- **Keep it simple:** no unexplained acronyms, no feature dumps, no process tour; tour the buyer's outcome.
- **Avoid contamination:** do not adopt cynical habits from under-performing colleagues.

## 9. Mystique

Perception shapes value. Build a distinctive identity for the method:

- Give the method a name and a defined number of steps.
- Tell the true story of how it was developed or learned.
- Include one memorable demonstration moment.
- Reveal the method openly; the skill in applying it remains the value.

## 10. Persistence and resilience

- A "no" often means not yet, not in this form, not the right person, or not enough information. Keep the relationship alive and revisit wait-list prospects on a set cadence (for example quarterly), with consent.
- Persistence repeats the same approach; resilience keeps the goal and changes the approach. Prefer resilience.
- Most sales are lost by stopping follow-up too early.

## 11. Channel proximity

For closing, richer channels usually outperform thinner ones: in person, then phone, then video, then email, then text. Move up the ladder as the deal nears a decision.

## 12. Sales sabotage diagnostics

Common management errors: volume quotas that ignore quality; forcing a yes or no on every call; rigid scripts that prevent adaptation; discounting to close.

Common seller errors: presenting before discovery; apologising for price; talking instead of listening; chasing wrong-fit prospects from need; trading margin for volume without the arithmetic; stopping follow-up too early.

## 13. Sales tools

- A website should work as a sales argument, not a brochure.
- Treat an email subject as the envelope and its first lines as the opening promise.
- Automate follow-up: cart recovery, sequences, webinar replays.
- Keep a demonstration ready on a laptop or tablet in the field.
- A CRM is mandatory for follow-up discipline.
- Group calls and webinars can present to many at once.
- Never use formats that imitate official, legal, or government notices; they mislead recipients and may be unlawful.

## Regional application notes (East Africa)

Treat as hypotheses:

- Positioning through writing, speaking at business associations, broadcast appearances, and press commentary suits relationship-based markets.
- A clear selection stance can reposition a provider where buyers expect to negotiate down.
- The morning-after routine can reduce refund and reversal requests on mobile-money purchases.
- Lead-generation advertising that leads to a landing page, an opted-in WhatsApp conversation, and a short sequence is worth testing against direct-sale advertising.
- Named signature methods often resonate strongly; test named versus generic service descriptions.
