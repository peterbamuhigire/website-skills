# Pre-Meeting Research and Review Meeting

Parent skill: [premium-sales-conversation](../SKILL.md). Read when a first call
has gone well and you are preparing the diagnostic review meeting, when
several people on the buyer's side will judge the work differently, or when
you need a run-sheet for a screen-share findings meeting.

`qualification-and-discovery-questions.md` holds the question bank for the
first call. This file covers what happens between that call and the decision:
the reframe that earns a second meeting, the research pack, the review-meeting
run-sheet, and a build for multi-stakeholder buyers.

## 1. End the first call with a reframe, not a pitch

Keep the first call short. After a few questions about the business, move from
salesperson to diagnostician:

"Rather than pitch you now, let me study your website, search listings and
enquiry path properly. When we speak again on [day], I'll show you where you're
strong, where you're losing [enquiries or bookings], and what we'd do about it.
Does that work?"

Book the review meeting before hanging up. Invite the other decision-makers.

## 2. Research pack (per prospect)

Prepare before the review meeting. Keep evidence (screenshots, dates, tool
names) so every finding can be shown.

**A. Website basics**

- Is the phone number or WhatsApp link visible at the top on mobile?
- Is there one clear call to action per page?
- Are photos of real staff and premises, or stock?
- Is there a page per service (and per location, where the business serves
  several)?
- Does the enquiry form work? Submit a test and note whether a reply arrives.
- Mobile speed and usability on a low-end device profile (see the deploy
  skills' performance gate for method).

**B. Search demand**

- Seed terms from the prospect's services, in the words customers use.
- Search volume for each, combined with the towns or districts served; re-check
  volumes after combining. Note the tool and date; volumes are estimates.

**C. Current visibility**

- Where the prospect appears for the list above, and who appears instead.

**D. Local presence**

- Business-profile completeness, name–address–phone consistency across
  directories, review count, rating and recency of replies.

**E. One-page findings sheet**

- Three things done well.
- Three biggest gaps.
- The cost of each gap in the prospect's terms, as a range with its basis.

Never claim a ranking, volume or benchmark you cannot show with its source and
date.

## 3. Review-meeting run-sheet (60 minutes, screen-share)

| # | Step | Content | Minutes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Rapport | Something specific and genuine about their business | 3 |
| 2 | Background and goals | How they win customers today, rough size, current revenue range, and the **twelve-month question**: "If we met in twelve months and everything had gone to plan, what would have changed?" | 10 |
| 3 | Findings: strengths first | Praise real strengths before any gap. Never rubbish the current site or its builder | 5 |
| 4 | Findings: gaps | Show the gaps, the search demand, and where they do not appear: "People search for this every day and find [competitor], not you" | 12 |
| 5 | Solution narrative | Exactly what you would do and the causal chain to enquiries, revenue and margin | 10 |
| 6 | Options and close | Two or three options, one recommended; the choice close (see `price-presentation-and-choice-close.md`) | 10 |
| 7 | Pre-close and next step | 1–10 readiness question; if undecided, decide-by date and booked walk-through | 10 |

**Bridge from findings to solution:**

"You're here: [current measure]. You want to be here: [goal]. The gap comes
from [cause 1, 2, 3]. Here's how we close it: [mechanism], so that [leading
indicator] rises, which brings [outcome]."

## 4. Rehearse the hardest questions

Before an important meeting, write down every question the prospect could ask,
in a sceptical voice, and draft the answers. Twenty questions is a good target.
If the prospect is far more senior or wealthy than you, remember that they buy
expertise, not status: compare yourself within your field, not theirs. The
rehearsed list doubles as FAQ content for the proposal and the website.

## 5. Multi-stakeholder buyers: a tailored rational build

When several people decide (owner, finance, operations, IT, board), each
weighs different things. Build the case in blocks:

1. **Gather your evidence**: delivery data, comparable results, timelines.
2. **Map stakeholder priorities** and write one message per person:

| Stakeholder | Main concern | Message | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Managing director | Growth, reputation | | |
| Finance | Predictable cost, payback | | |
| Operations or front office | Minimal disruption, fewer manual tasks | | |
| IT or security | Access, data, hosting | | |

3. **Prepare visuals**: one-page summary, page plan or sitemap, cost and
   payback view, timeline, risk table.
4. **Offer scenarios**: for example full build, phased build, or phase one
   only, each priced.
5. **Anticipate questions** in a list grouped by stakeholder.
6. **Present in modular blocks** (summary, approach, cost, delivery, risk) so
   each person can go to their block.
7. **Add two or three relevant client stories** with permission.
8. **Show measures**: current, target, how they will be measured, and what
   is not promised.
9. **Tie explicitly to the client's published goals** (strategic plan,
   annual report, board priorities).

After the decision, send a short record to each stakeholder in their terms
and agree the reporting rhythm.

## 6. Handoff

If the buyer runs a formal procurement or asks for a tender response, route to
the proposal engine (proposal-skills) with the research pack, the findings
sheet and the stakeholder map.
