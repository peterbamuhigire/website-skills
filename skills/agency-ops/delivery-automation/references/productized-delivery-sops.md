# Productized Delivery and SOPs

**Purpose**: Turn website delivery into a Teachable, Valuable, Repeatable product
documented well enough that any trained operator can run it — the prerequisite for
automation and for margin.

**Read this when**: defining the productized offering or writing delivery SOPs.
Assumes you have read `delivery-automation/SKILL.md`.

---

## Productize before you automate

John Warrillow (*Built to Sell*, 2011): a service becomes a scalable, sellable
asset when it is **Teachable, Valuable, Repeatable** — you identify the one thing
you do best and turn it into a standardised offering with fixed scope and price.
Michael Gerber (*The E-Myth*): work *on* the business by building systems, not
just *in* it doing the work.

A productized service = a defined, repeatable offering with **fixed scope, fixed
price, and a predictable delivery process** — not open-ended time. For a web
studio: a clearly bounded site product for the niche, with named deliverables and
no surprises. This is what makes the same work get faster each time (templates,
refined process, no relearning) — the mechanism behind specialist agencies'
higher reported margins.

This pairs with niche positioning (`../../agency-positioning/references/niche-selection-rubric.md`):
one niche × one repeatable product is the combination that compounds.

## The SOP / playbook categories

Document delivery as four repeatable systems:

1. **Client onboarding** — intake-form triggers, the first-48-hours protocol,
   asset and access collection, kickoff and the referral-expectation line
   (`../referral-program/references/ask-scripts-and-timing.md`).
2. **Request / build handling** — task assignment, the build sequence, who does
   what, and review-before-submission.
3. **Communication standards** — cadence, channels, status updates, escalation.
4. **Quality control** — pre-submission checklists and the canonical CI gates as
   approval gates (`../../launch-ops/deploy/SKILL.md`).

"Quality scales through SOPs, standards, and peer review — not heroics."

## Make SOPs usable

- Write each SOP as a checklist a trained operator can follow without you.
- Record short videos for the steps that are easier shown than written.
- Keep them in one internal wiki — establish the wiki **before** you scale headcount.
- New operators get productive by following playbooks, watching the videos, and
  completing checklists — not by shadowing.

## Connect to certification and governance

The engine already has an operator path: `certification/syllabus.md` and the
exam define the baseline an operator must meet; the maintenance calendar
(`../../orchestration/website-builder/references/maintenance-calendar.md`) sets the
cadence. Delivery SOPs are the project-level layer beneath that governance.

## The point: margin at held quality

Productization + SOPs + reusable assets lower the hours per project. The win is
measured in delivery hours saved while every quality gate still passes — never in
cutting the gate to go faster.
