# The Ask: Scripts and Timing

**Purpose**: When to ask for a referral, and the exact words — at kickoff, after a
win, and by email. Adapt to the client and niche; keep the structure.

**Read this when**: setting up the referral ask or training someone on it.
Assumes you have read `referral-program/SKILL.md`.

---

## Reframe the ask: you're helping them help someone

John Jantsch (*The Referral Engine*, 2010): asking for a referral is "offering to
help them get more of what they want" — connecting their contacts to a result
they value, not begging a favour. Hold that framing and the ask stops feeling
transactional.

## The intention–action gap is about asking and ease

Industry-reported: ~83% of satisfied customers say they'd refer, but only ~29%
actually do. The gap is almost entirely (1) never being asked and (2) friction.
Both are yours to fix.

## Timing: set it early, ask at the peak

### At kickoff — set the expectation
Plant the referral at the start so the later ask is expected:

> "One thing I do with every client: once we've launched and you're happy with
> the results, I'll ask you to introduce me to one or two other [niche] owners
> who'd benefit. No pressure — I just want to earn that introduction."

### At a win — make the ask
Ask at peak happiness — right after a measurable result or a strong satisfaction
signal, not on a fixed calendar date:

- After a launch that's converting, a milestone, or a clear thank-you.
- **NPS-triggered:** target Promoters (9–10 scorers); make the ask within ~24–48
  hours of the high score, while the goodwill is fresh.

Joey Coleman (*Never Lose a Customer Again*, 2018): the referral lives in the
final **Advocate** phase — earn it by getting the first 100 days right (20–70% of
new customers otherwise churn early). Don't ask before you've delivered the win.

## Ask scripts (face-to-face or call)

After gratitude:
> "That means a lot. Can I ask a small favour? Who else do you know — another
> [niche] owner — who's frustrated with their website the way you were? I'd love
> an introduction, and there's a [reward] in it for you when it works out."

After a delivered result:
> "Since the new site's bringing in [result], I'd like to do the same for someone
> you rate. Is there one business you'd be glad to introduce me to?"

Always: name the *type* of person who's a good fit, so they can picture someone
specific instead of scanning their whole contact list.

## Email / WhatsApp template

> Subject: A quick favour (and a thank-you in it for you)
>
> Hi [name],
>
> I'm really glad the new site is working for you — [specific result].
>
> I grow mostly by word of mouth from clients like you. If you know another
> [niche] owner who'd benefit, just reply with their name or forward the line
> below. When it turns into a project, [reward].
>
> *Forwardable: "I worked with [studio] on my website — [result]. Worth a
> conversation if yours needs work: [link/contact]."*
>
> Thank you — [you]

Give them the forwardable sentence so referring costs them one tap.

## Make it effortless

- Provide a referral link or a one-field form, not "email me their details".
- Pre-write the introduction the referrer can copy.
- Confirm receipt and keep the referrer updated on what happened — closing the
  loop earns the next referral.
