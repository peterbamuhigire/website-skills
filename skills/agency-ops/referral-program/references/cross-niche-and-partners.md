# Cross-Niche and Partner Referrals

**Purpose**: Handle the referral that falls outside your niche — reward the
client anyway, route the lead well, and build a reciprocal partner network — while
keeping the niche positioning intact.

**Read this when**: a client refers a business outside your vertical, or you're
setting up partner relationships. Assumes you have read `referral-program/SKILL.md`.

---

## The tension: niche focus vs not wasting goodwill

A single-vertical studio ("web design for [niche]") will receive referrals to
businesses *outside* the niche — a happy salon client knows a restaurateur. You
do not want to break positioning by taking the work, but you must never waste the
goodwill or leave the referrer unrewarded. Resolve it with routing + reward.

## The rule: reward the referrer regardless of fit

Reward the client for *any* referral that closes, inside the niche or not. The
behaviour you're reinforcing is "introduce people to me" — punishing an
out-of-niche introduction by withholding the reward teaches them to stop.

Tell referral-sourced leads, plainly, that you also serve other niches when they
come by recommendation — a warm referral can justify stepping outside the strict
niche occasionally, the "Vertical Core + Horizontal Edge" pattern from
`../../agency-positioning/references/niche-selection-rubric.md`.

## Routing options for out-of-niche referrals

1. **Take it on the Horizontal Edge** — if the referral is warm and the work is
   well within your capability, take it without rebuilding the brand around it.
2. **Route to a partner** — send it to a trusted complementary or
   different-niche studio, ideally for a reciprocal arrangement (they send you
   their in-niche leads). Cross-routing referrals between sibling brands is a
   proven model at scale (e.g. multi-brand home-service networks).
3. **Route to a sibling line** — if you operate more than one niche brand, route
   the lead to the right one and still reward the introducer.

In all three, the referrer is rewarded and the lead is served — nothing is dropped.

## Build a reciprocal partner network

Start from people who already send you business informally, then formalise:

- Identify complementary, non-competing businesses your clients also use
  (photographers, accountants, signage, POS vendors, other-niche web studios).
- Agree two-way referral terms and how each side rewards the other.
- Keep a short, named partner list rather than a sprawling affiliate scheme.

## Client referral vs partner program — keep them distinct

- **Client referral (this skill's core):** lightweight, informal-friendly but
  tracked; clients introduce people they know; reward per closed deal.
- **Partner program:** a formal, contracted relationship with another business —
  more engagement, shared materials, sometimes product training, often a
  dedicated contact. Heavier; use only when partner volume justifies it.

Don't bureaucratise client referrals into a partner program prematurely; don't run
high-volume partner relationships on a casual handshake.
