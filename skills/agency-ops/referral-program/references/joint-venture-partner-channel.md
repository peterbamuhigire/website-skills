# Joint-Venture Partner Channel

Parent skill: [referral-program](../SKILL.md). Read when a person or firm that
already sells to your niche could introduce the agency to its customers, when
setting terms for a recurring revenue-share with such a partner, or when
deciding whether a relationship is a client referral or a partner channel.

`cross-niche-and-partners.md` handles out-of-niche client referrals and small
reciprocal networks, and distinguishes client referrals from formal partner
programmes. This file specifies one kind of formal partner: the joint venture
(JV) with someone who already holds the niche's trust.

## 1. Why a JV partner is a distinct channel

A client referral is one happy client introducing one contact. A JV partner
already speaks to many of your ideal buyers: a practice-management trainer for
clinics, a school-management software vendor, an accountant serving hotels, a
sector association's events organiser. One endorsement to their audience can
open more doors than months of outreach, because the trust is borrowed.

Keep the two channels separate in terms, records and reporting.

| | Client referral | JV partner |
|---|---|---|
| Who | A delivered, satisfied client | A business already selling to the niche |
| Volume | Occasional, one-to-one | Potentially many, one-to-many |
| Reward | Per closed referral (see `reward-structures.md`) | Negotiated; may be a one-off fee or a recurring share |
| Agreement | Programme rules | Signed agreement |

## 2. Find and screen partners

List everyone who sells to your niche but does not compete with you. Screen
each on:

1. **Audience fit.** Do their customers match your best clients by size and
   need?
2. **Trust.** Are they respected in the niche? Ask your own clients.
3. **Values and quality.** Would you be comfortable with their name next to
   yours if something went wrong?
4. **Channel.** Do they have a lawful, consented way to reach their audience
   (their own newsletter, events, training sessions, client meetings)?
5. **Reciprocity.** What can you give them: introductions, content for their
   audience, a better experience for their shared clients?

## 3. Start with a proof-first trial

Before any long-term terms:

1. Deliver something useful to the partner's audience at your cost: a talk at
   their event, a checklist co-branded with them, or a clinic for their
   clients.
2. Agree how interested people reach you (a dedicated booking link or form) so
   attribution is clear from day one.
3. Review after one or two cycles: bookings, wins, client feedback, and how the
   partner felt about the experience.

**Lists stay with the partner.** The partner promotes you to their own
consented audience. You do not receive, copy or buy their contact list. People
who respond give their details to you directly, with your privacy notice.
Uganda requires consent to collect personal data and honouring written
direct-marketing objections within 14 days (register PL-01); Kenya requires
consent and a free, simple opt-out for direct marketing, and businesses mainly
in direct marketing must register with the ODPC regardless of size (register
PL-02).

## 4. Agreement checklist

Get commercial terms approved by leadership and the finance owner before
offering them. Where the agreement is a legal contract, have it reviewed.

- [ ] Parties, niche and territory covered.
- [ ] What the partner will do (channels, frequency) and what the agency will
      do in return.
- [ ] Attribution rule: which link, form or code counts; how long a referral
      stays attributed; how disputes are settled.
- [ ] Reward: one-off fee per closed client, or a share of recurring fees for a
      defined period; paid only on cleared client payments.
- [ ] Disclosure: how the partner tells its audience about the commercial
      arrangement, and how the agency tells referred clients.
- [ ] Sector rules: regulated professions may bar or limit referral fees;
      check before agreeing (see `tracking-and-ethics.md`).
- [ ] Data protection: who controls which data; no list transfer.
- [ ] Brand use and approval of co-branded material.
- [ ] Quality exit: either side can end the arrangement if clients are poorly
      served.
- [ ] Review date.

**Revenue share and the client book.** A share paid to a partner is different
from pay-for-performance terms with a client, which `agency-positioning`
does not permit. Keep partner shares out of client contracts and within the
approved margin. Nelson describes paying JV partners about 10% of recurring
revenue per referred client; that is **one practitioner's US arrangement**,
not a market rate. Size your own terms against the cost-to-fulfil sheet in
`../../agency-client-retention/references/retainer-design-and-cost-to-fulfil.md`
and route tax treatment of partner payments to the finance engine
(chwezi-accounting-doctrine).

## 5. Run the channel

- Keep a named partner list, short and actively managed.
- Report per partner: introductions, bookings, wins, revenue, share paid.
- Give back each quarter: introductions, useful content, recognition.
- Treat partner-referred clients with extra care; a poor experience harms two
  relationships.

## 6. Worked example (illustrative)

A Kampala agency serving private clinics identifies a trainer who runs
practice-management courses for clinic owners. Trial: the agency teaches a
free 45-minute module, "Patient enquiries from your website", at two of the
trainer's courses, with a booking link unique to the trainer. Eleven owners
book reviews; three become clients over four months. The parties then sign an
agreement: a fee per closed client, disclosed to course participants, with a
quarterly review and a quality exit clause.
