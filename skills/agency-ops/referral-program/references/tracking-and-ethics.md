# Tracking, Attribution, and Ethics

**Purpose**: Make every referral a tracked, attributable, ethically clean
transaction — closing the gap where most referral programs quietly die.

**Read this when**: setting up referral capture, payout rules, or checking
compliance. Assumes you have read `referral-program/SKILL.md`.

---

## The #1 failure mode: informal intros with no record

The most common reason referral programs fail is operating through informal
introductions — email forwards, WhatsApp messages, verbal "I sent someone your
way" — with no formal submission record and no automated attribution. When
payout time comes, no one can reconstruct who referred whom, rewards are missed
or disputed, and clients stop bothering.

Fix it by capturing the referral at submission, not reconstructing it at payment.

## Formal submission + attribution at the point of referral

- Provide a **referral link or a one-field form** that timestamps the submission
  and records the source automatically.
- For verbal/in-person referrals, the operator logs the referral the same day
  against the referring client's record.
- Track per referral: source (who referred), referred business, date, status
  (submitted → qualified → closed → paid), and reward owed.
- A lightweight CRM or a disciplined spreadsheet is enough to start; referral
  software (e.g. Referral Factory, GrowSurf, Viral Loops) adds links and
  dashboards once volume justifies it.

## Payout rules (write them down, make them known)

- **Pay only on a qualified, closed sale** — not on an introduction or an
  unqualified lead. This protects against low-quality referrals.
- State the reward, the trigger, and the timing in plain terms the client sees
  before they refer. No surprises, no arbitrary calls.
- Close the loop: tell the referrer what happened to their referral and pay
  promptly when it closes. Prompt, visible payment is the strongest driver of the
  next referral.

## Measure the program

Report these into `monthly-report`:

- Number of referrals, by source.
- Referral → qualified → closed conversion.
- Value of referred clients vs other channels (expect higher — referred clients
  are measurably more valuable and lower-churn).
- Participation: share of eligible clients who referred at least once.

## Ethics and disclosure

Referral fees are legitimate, but some contexts restrict or require disclosure of
them:

- **Regulated client sectors** (legal, financial, medical and similar) may bar or
  limit referral fees, or require they be disclosed to the end client. Check
  before offering a fee that touches such a sector — see also
  `../policy-pages/SKILL.md` and the sector skills.
- **Disclose** referral relationships where a reasonable client would want to
  know one exists. Public perception of undisclosed referral fees skews negative.
- **Jurisdiction:** confirm local rules on referral commissions and tax treatment
  of rewards.

When in doubt, disclose the arrangement and document consent. A clean, transparent
program is more durable than a quietly lucrative one.
