# Reward Structures

**Purpose**: Decide what to give referrers (and referred clients), how much, and
in what form, sized against the value of a won client.

**Read this when**: designing or revising the referral reward. Assumes you have
read `referral-program/SKILL.md`.

---

## Start from the value of a referred client

Referred clients are not ordinary leads. Peer-reviewed research (Schmitt, Skiera
& Van den Bulte, *Journal of Marketing*, 2011; ~10,000 customers tracked ~33
months) found referred customers are **~16% more valuable** and **~18% less
likely to churn** than comparable non-referred customers. Size the reward as a
fraction of that extra lifetime value, not as a token. A reward that feels mean
relative to the work referred suppresses the behaviour you want.

## Default: double-sided

Reward **both** the referrer and the new client. Roughly 78% of referral programs
are now double-sided (industry-reported). In B2B this removes the awkwardness of
"profiting off my contact": the referrer feels they did their friend a favour
(the new client gets a perk), not that they sold them.

- **Referrer reward:** cash, account credit, or a service add-on.
- **New-client reward:** an onboarding discount, a free add-on, or priority
  scheduling — something that makes accepting the introduction feel like a win.

## Offer a small menu, not one option

Different referrers are motivated differently, and some professional contexts
make a personal cash gift awkward or non-compliant. Offer a short menu:

- **Cash / bank or mobile-money transfer** — simplest, universally understood.
- **Account / service credit** — keeps value inside the relationship; good for
  clients on retainers.
- **Charity donation** — fits professional networks and clients for whom a
  personal cash reward is restricted or off-brand.
- **Recognition / non-monetary** — a public thank-you or a client-spotlight, on
  top of (not instead of) a tangible reward for a closed deal.

There is no rigorous head-to-head study showing one form beats the others, so let
the client choose.

## Tiering

Flat rewards are simplest to start. Introduce **tiers** to turn one-time referrers
into repeat ones once the program has traction — e.g. a larger reward, or a step
up to a recognised "partner" status, after the third closed referral. Do not
over-engineer tiers before the program has volume.

## Agency referral-fee norms (reference points, not rules)

Industry-reported structures for web/design agencies:

- A flat fee per referral that becomes a website sale (commonly cited around
  **$500**) plus a percentage (≈10%) on other design services.
- A percentage of the initial project (commonly **10%**, sometimes capped).
- General service-referral fee range cited at **5–25%** of project value.
- Non-cash alternative: a discount on the client's next project, or a free
  maintenance period, for a successful referral.

Calibrate these to your market floor (`../../agency-positioning/references/pricing-rules.md`
and the Uganda calibration). The exact number matters less than: it is generous
relative to the work, it is paid only on a closed deal, and it is known in advance.

## Payout gating

Pay rewards only **after the referral is qualified and the sale closes** — not on
an introduction or an unqualified lead. State this in the program terms so it
never feels arbitrary. See `tracking-and-ethics.md`.

## Sources

See `sources.md`. The 16%/18% figures are from Schmitt/Skiera/Van den Bulte
(peer-reviewed, primary). The 78%-double-sided figure and the agency fee norms
are industry-reported (vendor blogs) — frame accordingly.
