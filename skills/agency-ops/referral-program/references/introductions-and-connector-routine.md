# Introductions and Connector Routine

Parent skill: [referral-program](../SKILL.md). Read when the agency's pipeline
depends too much on cold contact, when building a weekly habit of making and
earning introductions, when approaching someone who knows you socially but not
professionally, or when planning a curated gathering.

`ask-scripts-and-timing.md` covers asking clients for referrals after a win.
This file covers the wider habit: becoming the person who connects others, so
that introductions flow back without a formal ask.

## 1. Principle

What others say about you persuades far more than what you say about yourself.
A warm introduction from someone the buyer respects removes most of the
resistance a cold approach meets. In Uganda and Kenya, business already runs on
introductions: alumni networks, professional bodies, churches and mosques,
Rotary and Lions clubs, chamas and SACCOs, community associations and WhatsApp
groups.

## 2. Weekly and quarterly routine

| Cadence | Action |
|---|---|
| Weekly | Make **one unprompted, useful introduction** between two people in your network who should know each other |
| Weekly | Send **one useful item** (an article, an event, a contact, a relevant opportunity) to a client or contact, with no request attached |
| Weekly | Log the **stories** people tell you (their projects, concerns, milestones) in the CRM, not only their titles |
| Monthly | Call two or three important contacts with an opportunity for them, not a request for you |
| Quarterly | Host one curated gathering (see §4) |

**Connector message template:**

"[Name], meet [Name]. [One line on why each should know the other.] I'll let
you take it from here."

Ask both people privately before connecting them if either might not welcome
it. Never share someone's contact details without their permission.

**Opportunity call:** "I came across [opportunity] and thought of you straight
away. No agenda; I thought it might be useful."

## 3. Asking for help

A sincere request for guidance disarms busy people and gatekeepers:

- "I wonder if you could help me with something. I'm trying to find the right
  person to speak to about [topic]. Would that be you, or someone else?"
- "You know this sector better than anyone I've met. Who else should I be
  learning from?"

Use it only when you genuinely need guidance. It is not a disguised pitch.

## 4. The curated gathering

A small, by-invitation breakfast or roundtable, for example 20–30 decision
makers from one sector plus a few experts.

1. Choose a theme useful to the guests, not a showcase of the agency.
2. Assign seating so guests meet people they do not already know.
3. The host briefly introduces each guest (name, organisation, one interesting
   fact, agreed in advance).
4. No pitch. Offer one short, useful talk or panel at most.
5. Follow up within 48 hours with a thank-you and any introductions promised.

Check the guest list and hospitality against conflict-of-interest rules when
public officials or procurement staff may attend; keep gatherings open and
modest.

## 5. The warm-market permission script

For people who know you socially (from a club, a church, an alumni group) but
not professionally:

1. Acknowledge the relationship: "We've known each other for years through
   [group]."
2. Say what you do: "I help [type of organisation] [achieve outcome]."
3. Explain your restraint: "I've never raised it because I didn't want you to
   think I joined [group] to find clients."
4. Leave the decision with them: "Would you have any objection to us meeting
   once on a professional basis, so I can show you what we've done for
   [similar organisation]?"

Accept a "no" warmly and never raise it again unprompted.

## 6. Three networks to maintain

- **Inside the agency and its suppliers**: delivery, finance, hosting and
  specialist partners.
- **Inside the industry**: other agencies (including competitors), trainers,
  associations, journalists who cover the niche.
- **The wider community**: alumni, faith and civic groups, clubs.

Review each quarter: who in each network have you helped recently?

## 7. Measures

- Introductions made per month.
- Introductions received per month, and meetings they produced.
- Win rate and sales-cycle length for introduced prospects compared with other
  sources.

Record introduced prospects in the referral register with their source, as
`tracking-and-ethics.md` requires; rewards apply only where a programme or
partner agreement says so.
