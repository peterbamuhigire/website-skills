# The 5-Minute Audit Checklist

**Purpose**: A fast, honest, phone-runnable check of a prospect's current website
that turns a cold visit into a conversation about *their* business. The audit is
the door-opener, not the deliverable.

**Read this when**: preparing to walk a territory, or building the leave-behind.
Assumes you have read `local-in-person-acquisition/SKILL.md`.

---

## Why an audit opens doors

Walking in with a specific, checkable observation ("your site takes nine seconds
to load on my phone and your booking button is below the fold") outperforms any
generic pitch. It proves you looked, it is about them, and it creates the gap the
engagement fills. Practitioners report reply rates roughly double when a short
free audit is attached to outreach (industry-reported — see `sources.md`).

## The seven checks (run in order, ~5 minutes)

Run these on your phone, on the prospect's real site, ideally before you walk in:

1. **Mobile experience** — open it on a phone. Does it reflow, or do you pinch and
   scroll sideways? Most local customers arrive on mobile; a broken mobile site is
   the single most persuasive finding.
2. **Speed** — count the seconds to usable, or use Google PageSpeed Insights.
   Anything over ~4 seconds on mobile is a finding.
3. **SSL / padlock** — is the address `https://` with a padlock? "Not secure" in
   the browser bar is a trust killer you can show them instantly.
4. **Stale footer year** — a "© 2019" (or earlier) footer signals an abandoned
   site to both customers and Google. Easy to point at, hard to argue with.
5. **Call-to-action above the fold** — can a customer book / call / find the
   address without scrolling? For most niches the primary action is "book" or
   "call now".
6. **Clickable phone number on mobile** — tap the number. Does it dial? A
   non-tappable number on a mobile site loses calls every day.
7. **Google Business Profile link** — is the site connected to their Google
   listing (map, hours, reviews)? Missing local presence is a recurring,
   high-value gap.

If the business has **no website at all**, the audit is the listing instead:
check the Google Business Profile, the Instagram/Facebook page, and how a customer
would actually book today — then show them the gap.

## Turning findings into the conversation

- Name **two** findings, not seven. Two is concrete and credible; seven is a
  lecture and overwhelms.
- State each finding as a **business cost**, not a technical fault: "customers
  can't tap to call you" beats "the tel: link is missing".
- End on the gap, not the fix: "here's what it's costing you — want to see how it
  could work?" The fix is the paid engagement.

## How this feeds the paid work

- The free 5-minute audit is the door-opener. The **paid, deep audit** is a
  product — see `../../agency-positioning/references/premium-audit-offers.md` and
  the `seo-audit` skill for the full version (Core Web Vitals, full SEO, content,
  conversion).
- Never give away the deep audit for free; give away the observation that makes
  them want it.
