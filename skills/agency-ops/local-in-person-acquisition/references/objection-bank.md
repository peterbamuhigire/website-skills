# SMB Objection Bank

**Purpose**: Scripted, non-arguing responses to the objections a local SMB owner
raises at the doorstep or counter. Adapt the wording to the niche; keep the
structure.

**Read this when**: preparing for or debriefing in-person visits. Assumes you
have read `local-in-person-acquisition/SKILL.md`.

---

## The method: Acknowledge → Explore → Respond (never argue)

Adapt the LAER pattern (Listen, Acknowledge, Explore, Respond). The owner is not
wrong; they are protecting their time and money. Acknowledge the concern, ask one
question to understand it, then respond to the real issue — not the surface line.
Winning the argument loses the client.

Give them permission to say no early; it lowers the pressure and surfaces the real
objection faster.

## The five SMB classics

### 1. "My business doesn't need a website / I get customers by word of mouth"
- **Acknowledge:** "Word of mouth clearly works for you — that's a real strength."
- **Explore:** "When someone hears about you, what's the first thing they do?"
- **Respond:** They search. Today the website (or Google listing) is what they
  find — or don't. A credible site turns word-of-mouth referrals into bookings
  instead of losing them to the competitor who shows up first.

### 2. "It's too expensive"
- **Acknowledge:** "Fair — it should be an investment, not a random cost."
- **Explore:** "What would one extra [booking / client] a week be worth to you?"
- **Respond:** Reframe against the return, not the sticker. Itemise what's
  included so it reads as value, not a number. Hold the market floor — do not
  discount at the doorstep (see `../../agency-positioning/references/pricing-rules.md`).

### 3. "I don't have time for this"
- **Acknowledge:** "That's exactly why most owners put it off — totally get it."
- **Explore:** "If I handled it end to end, would time still be the blocker?"
- **Respond:** The offer is done-for-you: you handle everything, they approve.
  Make the next step tiny — a 15-minute look at a mockup, not a project kickoff.

### 4. "I can build one myself / I'll use Wix"
- **Acknowledge:** "You absolutely could — the tools are there."
- **Explore:** "What's stopped you so far?" (Usually: time. If they cite time,
  ask what will free up later that hasn't yet.)
- **Respond:** DIY sites stall half-built and rank poorly because the owner's time
  goes to running the business. A niche-built site is finished, found, and
  converting — and they keep their evenings.

### 5. "I have a Facebook / Instagram page"
- **Acknowledge:** "Social is doing real work for you — keep it."
- **Explore:** "How do customers book or find your hours from there right now?"
- **Respond:** Social is rented reach and a poor booking surface; a site is the
  asset you own that gives customers info and a booking without a login, and that
  Google can rank. Best result: the site and the social feed working together.

## "No budget right now"

Do not discount. Plant for later:

> "I respect that the budget isn't there this quarter. Let me leave you the two
> findings and a rough number, so when you're ready you're not starting from
> scratch — and if anything changes, my line's open."

Then log a return date in the CRM (see `cadence-and-crm.md`). "No budget now" is a
timing objection, not a no.

## When it really is a no

If the owner clearly opts out, thank them, leave the one-pager, and note it. A
graceful exit protects your reputation in a niche where owners talk to each other.
