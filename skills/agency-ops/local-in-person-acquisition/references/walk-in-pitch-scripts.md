# Walk-In Pitch Scripts

**Purpose**: Give the operator the exact words for the first 30 seconds of an
in-person cold visit, how to reach the decision-maker, what to show, and what to
leave behind. Adapt the bracketed parts to the niche and business; do not read
them robotically.

**Read this when**: preparing to walk into businesses cold, or training someone
to do it. Assumes you have read `local-in-person-acquisition/SKILL.md`.

---

## 1. The principle: open with them, not with you

The fastest way to be dismissed is to lead with "I build websites." Open with an
observation about *their* business or a free offer. Earn the next sentence.

In-person is the highest-response channel that exists for selling to small local
businesses: HubSpot ranks meeting in person as the #1 most effective sales
channel, and RAIN Group found 82% of buyers accept meetings with sellers who
reach out proactively. The visit itself is the advantage — use it.

## 2. The 10-second opener (permission first)

Earn 60 seconds before you say anything else:

> "Hi, I'm [name] — I work with [niche, e.g. salons] here in [area]. I had one
> quick thing about [business name]'s website I wanted to show you — do you have
> 60 seconds, or is there a better time today?"

If they're busy: get a name and a time. "No problem — when's the quietest part
of your day? I'll come back." Then actually come back.

## 3. The 30-second pitch (once you have the floor)

> "I'm not sure if you're the right person for this, so tell me if I should
> speak to someone else. I run a small studio that builds websites just for
> [niche] here in [city]. I had a look at [business name]'s site this morning and
> spotted a couple of things costing you [bookings / customers] — for example
> [one concrete audit finding]. I've got a quick idea for how it could look. Can
> I show you for two minutes?"

Why it works: names a niche (credibility), admits you might have the wrong person
(disarms), leads with a concrete finding (value before ask), and asks for a tiny
commitment (two minutes, not a sale).

## 4. The free-offer opener (for the audit-first approach)

> "We're doing free 5-minute website check-ups for [niche] on [street/area] this
> week. No catch — I'll show you where your site is losing customers on a phone.
> Want me to run yours now?"

Lead with the gift. The audit (see `5-minute-audit-checklist.md`) is the reason
for the conversation; the engagement is the follow-up.

## 5. Reaching the decision-maker

- Ask directly: "Who handles the website decisions for [business name]?"
- If it's not the person in front of you, get the name and the best time they're
  on site. Do not hand your pitch to a gatekeeper to relay.
- Resist "just email us." Email from a stranger is the channel owners ignore —
  that is the entire reason you came in person. Respond:
  > "I could, but you get a hundred of those. I'm here now and it'll take two
  > minutes — is the owner around, or when are they usually in?"
- Qualify lightly with BANT (Budget, Authority, Need, Timeline) before investing
  in a full demo — see `cadence-and-crm.md`.

## 6. Show, don't tell

Carry on your phone or tablet:

- **One before/after** from the same niche (most persuasive single asset).
- **A 60-second live critique** of their current site on the phone, so they feel
  the mobile experience their customers feel.
- **A rough mockup** built for a business like theirs — a personalised mockup is
  the strongest foot-in-the-door. If you can pre-build one for the prospect
  before the visit, do.

Never show a slide deck at a counter. Show the work.

## 7. The one-page leave-behind

The owner's attention peaks right after you leave. Leave one page, not a
brochure. It must:

- Be skimmable in 15 seconds: bold headings, white space, no walls of text.
- Recap the two findings you named and what they cost the business.
- Show one before/after thumbnail.
- State exactly one next action: a 15-minute slot, a phone number, a QR code to
  book. One action, not five.
- Carry your name, the niche line, and a real local phone number.

## 8. Walk-in etiquette (do not get yourself banned from the street)

- Never pitch during a visible rush. Read the room first.
- For a counter business, buy something or wait for a lull; chat as a person
  before you sell.
- If they say not now, leave gracefully and note when to return. Burned goodwill
  travels fast in a niche where owners know each other.
- Be the person they're glad came in — that reputation is the channel's compounding asset.
