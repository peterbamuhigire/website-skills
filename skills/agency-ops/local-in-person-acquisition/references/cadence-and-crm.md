# Cadence, Follow-Up, and CRM

**Purpose**: Define the daily prospecting rhythm, the multi-touch follow-up
sequence, doorstep qualification, what to log, and the weekly pipeline metrics
that tell you whether the channel is working.

**Read this when**: setting up the in-person acquisition operation or reviewing
its numbers. Assumes you have read `local-in-person-acquisition/SKILL.md`.

---

## Daily cadence

- **Target 25–50 in-person contacts per active prospecting day**, clustered by
  area so the route is walkable.
- **Count conversations, not doors.** A contact only counts when you speak to a
  person about their business. Reaching 25 people beats knocking 60 closed doors.
- Block prospecting into the part of the day when owners are on site and not in a
  rush — usually mid-morning or mid-afternoon, not the lunch peak.

## The follow-up sequence (Day 1 / 3 / 7 / 14)

Most deals need five or more touches; most people quit after one. Run a fixed
two-week sequence after a first in-person contact, then space further touches:

1. **Day 1 — same-day recap.** A short message (WhatsApp / SMS / email per their
   preference) thanking them, restating the two audit findings, and confirming
   the one next action.
2. **Day 3 — new observation.** One additional value point — a second finding, a
   competitor comparison, or the rough mockup. Not "just checking in".
3. **Day 7 — value-add.** Send something useful with no ask: the mockup link, a
   one-page checklist, or a short audit video.
4. **Day 14 — graceful breakup.** "I'll stop here so I'm not a pest — if a new
   site moves up your list this quarter, my line's open." Breakups often reopen
   the conversation.

Log a return date for anyone who said "not now" — timing objections are revisited,
not closed.

## Doorstep qualification (BANT)

Qualify lightly before investing in a full demo or proposal:

- **Budget** — can they invest at the market floor? (Don't quote; gauge.)
- **Authority** — are you talking to the person who decides? If not, get the name and time.
- **Need** — did the audit surface a real, felt problem?
- **Timeline** — is there a reason to act this quarter, or is this someday?

A prospect missing Authority or Timeline goes into the nurture sequence, not the
proposal stage.

## What to log (every interaction, same day)

For each contact, record:

- Business name, person spoken to, role, contact detail.
- Date, outcome, and the audit findings named.
- BANT read and qualification status.
- The single next action and its due date.

Log it the same day or it is lost. A simple field-sales CRM (e.g. SalesRabbit,
SPOTIO) handles map-based territories, status filters, and follow-up reminders;
a disciplined spreadsheet works to start. The discipline matters more than the tool.

## Weekly pipeline KPIs (read weekly, not monthly)

The in-person cycle is short enough that monthly review wastes a month. Read these
every week and correct the weakest link:

| Metric | What it tells you | Healthy direction |
|---|---|---|
| Contacts/day | Activity volume | At or above target (25–50) |
| Contact (conversation) rate | Are you reaching people, not closed doors? | Rising; aim well above half of visits |
| Conversation → meeting rate | Is the pitch/audit landing? | Rising |
| Meeting → close rate | Is qualification + proposal working? | Rising |
| Touches per deal | Are you persisting to five-plus? | At least 5 before recording a no |

If contacts are high but conversations are low, the territory or timing is wrong.
If conversations are high but meetings are low, the opener/audit is weak. If
meetings are high but closes are low, qualification or pricing is the issue.
