# Hybrid Agency Operating Model

The internal operating model for a premium hybrid website agency: strategy, design, development, content, SEO and analytics under one roof, selling builds plus monthly retainers. The principles are tool-independent; choose current tools yourself.

Pairs with `retainer-package-catalog.md` (what we sell), `expansion-offers-matrix.md` (account growth), `subscription-economy-model.md` (recurring-revenue finance) and `okrs-and-client-reporting.md` (measurement).

---

## 1. Firm type and what "hybrid" means

Classify the agency against the alternatives so positioning stays honest.

| Type | Traits | Implication |
|------|--------|-------------|
| Integrated hybrid (our type) | Tech-fluent, versatile generalists, value-based pricing | Target position |
| Legacy traditionalist | Strong brand, top-heavy staffing, weak digital integration | Competitor to out-execute |
| Software-led service arm | Vendor with a services team | Competitor with structural advantage; partner or differentiate on independence |
| Specialist | One discipline only | Project work; partner or referral source |
| Connector | Network of subcontractors | Flexible but inconsistent quality |
| Freelancer | One person | Capacity-limited |

Three structural habits define a hybrid:

1. **Immersion.** Leadership tracks technology change and adopts tools early.
2. **Integration.** Services are woven through every engagement by generalists, not run as divisions.
3. **Partnership.** Build core competences in-house; partner for the rest using compatible workflow tools.

Three forces justify the model: speed of technical change, buyers choosing when and where to engage (inbound beats interruption), and buyers demanding measurable outcomes rather than activity.

## 2. Productised services

Billable hours reward slowness and hide value. Productise wherever scope can be defined.

**Service-product template (fill one per service):**

- Description: one benefit-led paragraph.
- What makes a good fit: criteria so clients self-qualify.
- What is included: concrete deliverable specification (for example, a 700-word page written to a persona, with priority keywords).
- Turnaround: working days.
- Set price: value-based, not hourly.
- Add-on fees: stated up front (extra design, extra planning).

**Rule:** if you can define the scope, you can fix the price. The agency's burden is to build the systems and hire the talent to deliver profitably at that price. This repository is the documented playbook that makes it possible; without it each engagement reinvents itself and margin falls.

**What stays variable:** open-ended work where the deliverable is judgement, such as pre-sale strategic discovery, before a tier is chosen. Price it as a discrete diagnostic.

## 3. Pricing model and margin metrics

### Seven variables to set before pricing any engagement

1. Estimated hours at peak efficiency (clients do not pay for your inefficiency).
2. Hourly revenue target (HRT): revenue goal divided by realistic client-service hours per year. Illustration: a five-person agency targeting UGX 1,800,000,000 with 5,500 client hours needs about UGX 327,000 per hour.
3. Pass-through costs (freelancers, licences, stock).
4. Perceived value: what the market will pay.
5. Builder or driver: foundation work versus work tied to measurable results. Drivers justify a premium.
6. Loss-leader status: acceptable only with a 12-month contract attached.
7. Service level: junior, mid or senior talent required.

### Revenue-efficiency rate

RER = ((price - costs) / hours) / HRT. A value of 100% means each hour earns the target.

Worked illustrations (invented): a UGX 40,000,000 site with UGX 8,000,000 costs and 100 hours at HRT UGX 300,000 gives ((32,000,000 / 100) / 300,000) = 107%. A UGX 12,000,000 plan taking 90 hours with UGX 2,000,000 costs gives 37%, acceptable only if it leads into a 12-month retainer.

### Recurring-revenue and cost guardrails (starting hypotheses; calibrate on your data)

- Aim for most revenue on 12-month or longer contracts.
- Cap any single client at about 20% of revenue.
- Keep base salaries near a third of revenue and total labour near half; aim for a net margin in the low-to-mid double digits.
- Project-only revenue suits soloists; agency growth needs a retainer book.

### Time tracking

Track time even under value-based pricing: it monitors efficiency, informs performance review, produces activity reports and improves future prices. Log lead-handling time too, to measure business-development efficiency.

## 4. Capacity and hiring

**Client-hour targets per month (starting hypotheses):**

| Role | Client hours |
|------|--------------|
| Junior (0-3 years) | 120-140 |
| Mid (3-7 years) | 100-120 |
| Senior (7+ years) | 80-100 |
| Founder | about 50 |

Seniors carry fewer client hours because they own pricing, positioning, hiring, business development and quality.

- Soft capacity: 100% of those targets. Absolute capacity: soft plus 20%.
- Hire when forecasts exceed absolute capacity for three or more months. Never hire on a one-month spike.
- Hire ahead of need when: a growth initiative launches; a partnership will bring demand; the pipeline covers three months at absolute capacity; you are replacing an underperformer; a core account is understaffed; a high-potential candidate appears.

**Retention.** Recruit people who see the agency as a destination. Drivers in order: intrinsic motivation (autonomy, mastery, purpose), a written career path, clear purpose, daily culture (protected focus blocks, shared rituals), then pay (necessary, not primary). Check current sector turnover figures locally before setting targets.

## 5. Recruiting and training

**Trait rubric.** Score candidates 1 to 5 on: analytical, balanced, confident without arrogance, creative, detail-oriented, focused, self-motivated, good listener, positive, relationship-building, comfortable with risk, digitally fluent, strategic, team-minded, technically fluent, and a strong writer. Add a yes/no field for overall fit; if unsure, the answer is no. Writing is among the most valuable skills for an agency professional.

**Recruiting steps**

1. Define brand and culture first.
2. Publish the career path.
3. Use the agency's authority content as a talent magnet.
4. Capture inbound candidates with a careers page and qualifying questionnaire.
5. Hold informational interviews even when not hiring.
6. Be selective: declining is cheaper than dismissing.
7. Review public professional signals.
8. Network offline (meetups in Kampala or Nairobi).
9. Grade every candidate on the same rubric.

**Training.** No course produces a hybrid marketer; build them with curated webinars, a reading list, a shared internal channel, written playbooks (this repository), role-play, conferences, certifications (analytics and ad-platform fundamentals plus one sector certificate), writing exercises and group book study.

**Career path (default three titles):** Associate Consultant, Consultant, Senior Consultant, with optional manager overlays. Prefer "consultant" to "account executive" to signal strategic work.

## 6. Internal operating rhythm

| When | What | Length |
|------|------|--------|
| Daily huddle | Trends, account issues, each person's top three priorities | 15 min |
| Weekly opening meeting | Good news, numbers, pipeline, projects, client loyalty, priorities | 30-45 min |
| Weekly group check-ins | 3-5 people per group; each reviews their clients | 15-30 min |
| Monthly | Forecasts, scorecards, P&L, retainer churn review | 90 min |
| Quarterly | Campaign and budget reviews; objective setting | 2 hours |

**Founder's management dashboard** (spreadsheet or workspace): monthly client-hour forecast by client and person; account status (project or retainer); income-potential rank 1 to 5; soft and absolute capacity; RER per account; forecast versus actual; sortable by priority.

**KPIs to read:** revenue per employee (set a target from your own cost base); RER per account; share of revenue on 12-month contracts; largest client share; capacity utilisation; hours logged versus forecast; pipeline volume, quality, conversion and cycle time. Add the subscription metrics from `subscription-economy-model.md`.

**Cash discipline.** Revenue is not cash. An agency that looks profitable on invoices can draw down reserves when two clients pay late and one unplanned bill lands. Watch both sides of cash daily. The Care and Maintenance retainer book stabilises cash: collected in advance, high margin.

## 7. Sales and lead management

**Qualification (four tests).** A prospect must have budget, decision authority, real desire for you specifically, and demonstrated engagement with your content. Missing any one makes it a suspect, not a lead. See `agency-positioning/references/qualification-guide.md` and `lead-qualification-scorecard.md`.

**Discovery questionnaire (15-20 minutes)** covering: existing foundation (brand, site, infrastructure); platform reach and influence; expectations and goals; potential for sustained impact; candidate strategies.

**Six-part proposal**

1. Snapshot: one to two pages, plain narrative summarising where the win is.
2. Discovery: keyword, competitor, social and content-gap findings.
3. Objectives: measurable and benchmarked.
4. Activity plan: services, pricing, timeline, from a standard package.
5. Appendix: team, experience, testimonials.
6. Terms: plain-language contract covering pricing, payment, reporting, expenses and add-ons.

**Buying stages:** awareness, search, evaluation, decision, post-purchase. Match content to stage.

**Reference policy.** Offer client references only when you are sure you want the client and they confirm references are the final step before signing. Otherwise point to case studies, reviews and methodology content. This protects existing clients from pointless calls.

**Reality check on selling.** Do not assume account staff can sell. Many delivery professionals lack a consultative process and qualification skill. Until the agency passes about 12 people, the founder and senior strategist are the sales team; train others with a documented process.

## 8. Client relationship management

**Onboarding (30 days):** discovery in week 1; benchmarks in week 2; 12-month approach in week 3; first-quarter activities approved in week 4.

**Monthly scorecard, three parts:** (1) snapshot dashboard with top metrics, comparisons and RAG flags; (2) analysis covering site health, traffic, keywords, links, lead sources and quality, content, social, conversions; (3) updated plan for next month with reallocated time and budget. See `monthly-report-template.md`.

**Account team dashboard.** One visible board listing owners for every standard activity, updated weekly, to end "who was meant to do that?" confusion.

**Client advocate role.** Assign a senior person to review each account from the client's side: join calls, review analytics, challenge the account team's strategy, question invoices. In an agency under about 12 people the founder does this part-time; above that, hire full-time. It is how quality scales without founder bandwidth.

**Account prioritisation.** Rank clients on: efficiency (a large invoice that consumes disproportionate hours is unprofitable); financial stability (15 or more days overdue is a warning; be willing to walk away); realistic expectations; engaged decision-maker; shared values; commitment length and budget history; growth potential; profile and credibility halo; niche experience; willingness to innovate (case-study material).

**Ideal-client profile (adapt).** Example: budget from UGX 45,000,000 for the build plus UGX 5,000,000 or more monthly; 12-month contract; integrated services; sectors such as financial services, healthcare, professional services or B2B technology; buyer is founder, partner or marketing director; low risk, moderate-to-high profit. Aim for most of the portfolio (test 60-75%) to match it.

**Relationship diversification.** Hold active relationships with at least two people at each retainer client (day-to-day contact plus decision-maker) so the account survives when the champion leaves.

## 9. Service mix and partners

Full hybrid mix: brand, web development, SEO, paid media, local search, email and nurture, social, copywriting, video, PR, design, mobile, content, analytics.

Decide for each: in-house, partnered or declined. Our default: in-house for brand and website strategy, design, development, SEO foundation, content engine, conversion architecture, multilingual rollout and technical support; partner for paid media, video, PR and mobile apps; decline black-hat SEO, pay-for-performance deals and generic social media management.

**Partner criteria:** complementary services; transparent pricing compatible with yours; compatible process tools; performance record; financial stability.

## 10. Scaling past founder bandwidth

- **2x-3x rule.** For each infrastructure decision (office, systems, tooling), ask whether it will work at two to three times current size.
- **Horizons.** Plan the next 1-3 years firmly, 3-5 years as contingencies, beyond that as vision only.
- **Five lessons:** expect constant change; build on trusted providers and pilot start-ups before committing; know your limit (people capacity is the real ceiling); keep advisers for finance, technology, HR, legal and accounting; arrange funding before you need it.
- **Documented playbook.** Pricing formula, grading rubric, meeting cadence, proposal template, persona templates, onboarding flow: write every system down. New hires onboard against the repository, not tribal knowledge.

## 11. Revenue streams

1. Services (retainers, the core).
2. Reseller or partner commissions.
3. Affiliate programmes.
4. Educator or publisher income (speaking, courses, webinars, reports).
5. Software (higher margin, but a services firm chasing product envy usually damages its core).

Sequence: years 1-3 services only; year 3 onward add education and publishing (paid roundtable, annual benchmark report); year 5 onward consider reselling preferred tools; avoid building software.

---

## 12. Twelve pitfalls

1. Product envy: a side product drains the core.
2. Selling digital work before you can deliver it.
3. Hiring on spikes (cash crunch) or too late (overwork, quality collapse).
4. Black-hat shortcuts: short-term gains, long-term penalties.
5. Cheap strategic plans as loss leaders; clients take them in-house.
6. Single-contact relationships.
7. One client above about 20% of revenue.
8. Top-heavy staffing, which makes value pricing impossible.
9. Dependence on formal tender processes that undervalue expertise.
10. Growth without a profitable model.
11. Always-on culture: interruptions destroy focus. Protect fixed unplugged blocks of at least two hours.
12. Leaders delegating the decisions that only they can take.

**Dated (replace with current evidence):** named tools from that era, ranking-factor lists, the social network roster, spam-penalty case studies, wire releases as a main strategy. Modern SEO work centres on experience-and-trust signals, page-experience metrics, semantic search and AI-answer visibility; verify current guidance.
