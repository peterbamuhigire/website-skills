# New-Client Assimilation and Retention Rhythm

Parent skill: [agency-client-retention](../SKILL.md). Read when a contract has
just been signed, when planning the first 120 days of a retainer, when
redesigning the monthly review call, or when a client with good results is
drifting away.

This file covers the stretch from signature to the end of the first four months
and the monthly call that follows. It complements `post-launch-review-cadence.md`
(the launch-day, 30-day, 90-day, quarterly and annual reviews) and the monthly
call agenda in `legacy-guidance.md`, which it extends with a homework loop and
an attempt log.

## 1. Why clients leave

Most clients do not leave because results were poor. They leave because they
**feel ignored**. Nelson calls this perceived indifference. Clients look for
costs to cut every month; a supplier they rarely hear from is the easiest one.
Good results alone do not secure renewal. Visible attention does.

## 2. Assimilation: a distinct stage after the sale

Treat the weeks after signature as their own stage, owned by the same people
who will run the account, so there is no seam between sales and delivery.

**Assimilation call (within 5 working days of signature):**

1. Thank the client and check that onboarding has started without friction.
2. Reinforce the decision: restate the problem they hired you to solve and the
   first milestone.
3. Profile them: preferred channels and times, who else must receive updates,
   who approves content, who pays invoices.
4. Assign a grade and a contact plan (see `client-grading-and-contact-plan.md`).
5. Name the account lead who will run the relationship from here.
6. Note any early signs of a client you should not keep: chronic late payment
   in onboarding, abusive treatment of staff, demands far outside scope.
   Escalate these now, while exit is cheap.

**Design aid.** List how your team welcomes a new colleague (introductions,
a guide, a buddy, a first-week check-in) and translate each item into a client
contact.

**Proof method (optional, for larger books).** Where you onboard many similar
clients, compare a group that receives the full assimilation routine with one
that receives the minimum, and track renewal and expansion over twelve months.

## 3. The kickoff kit

| Instrument | Contents | Owner | When |
|---|---|---|---|
| Client set-up sheet | Access and logins (stored in the password manager, never in email), brand assets, services and priority keywords, contacts, approval owner | Account coordinator | Before the launch call |
| Launch call (30–90 min) | Goals, what the client does better than anyone in their town or sector, who they most want more of, other marketing already running, service priorities, preferred contacts | Account lead **with the copywriter on the call** | Week 1 |
| Welcome sequence | Weekly messages for 60–90 days from **different senders**: founder ("why we took you on"), account lead (90-day roadmap video), delivery lead (how we work), support (how to raise an issue) | Account lead schedules; each sender writes their own | Weeks 1–12 |
| Physical welcome | Printed welcome binder (programme overview, timeline, who is who, how to reach us), a locally made gift, a **handwritten** note from the person who sold the work | Founder or sales lead | Within the first 2 weeks |

**Why the writer joins the launch call.** The copywriter hears the owner's own
words for their business, customers and difference. That language is hard to
recover from notes, and it is what makes the site sound like the client.

**Local adaptation.** A welcome kit can be a printed binder plus coffee or tea
from a named local cooperative, or a notebook in local fabric, delivered by
courier. Write the note by hand; a greeting in the client's language, where
known, is a courtesy. Budget the kit in the per-client cost-to-fulfil sheet
(`retainer-design-and-cost-to-fulfil.md`).

**Ongoing surprise.** Put something physical and useful in the client's hands
at least once a quarter for AA and A grades, less often for lower grades,
within the contact budget.

## 4. The 90–120-day danger window

The first months ride on onboarding energy. Around day 90–120 the client starts
to ask what they are getting for the money. Change gear before that point:

- Show the first leading indicators the client can see (enquiries, bookings,
  calls), not only technical progress. This is why every retainer needs a
  fast-signal component (see `retainer-design-and-cost-to-fulfil.md`).
- Move the conversation from "what we built" to "what changed for you and what
  happens next".
- Book the 90-day review before day 60 and make sure the decision-maker, not a
  delegate, attends.

## 5. Monthly review call with a homework loop

| Step | Content | Time (45-min call) |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Rapport | "How's business? How are you tracking against your goals?" Listen for news that affects the plan | 5 |
| 2. Follow up last month's homework | Did the client do what they agreed? If not, what got in the way? | 5 |
| 3. Work done | Actions and reasons, not hours | 5 |
| 4. Results | Enquiries or bookings tracked, traffic trend over 3/6/9 months, search visibility; each number with a "so what" | 10 |
| 5. Next 30/60/90 days | The plan and why | 10 |
| 6. Seed the vision | Seasonal pushes, new services, events, products to feature next quarter | 5 |
| 7. Client homework | One or two specific tasks (request reviews from recent customers, send team photos, confirm the next offer, approve two pages) with dates | 3 |
| 8. Confirm next call | Date and time in both calendars before hanging up | 2 |

Send a written summary the same day (email with a short WhatsApp note if that
is the client's channel). Track **homework completion rate** per client: a
falling rate is an early churn signal.

**Seasonal prompts for East African clients:** school terms and admissions
windows, Christmas and Easter, Eid, graduation season, harvest cycles, the
tourism high seasons, and budget-reading week.

## 6. Log every contact attempt

If a client stops taking calls, keep trying and keep a record. After three or
four attempts, send a short, courteous email that lists the dates you tried to
reach them and offers two times. The record protects the relationship (the
client can see you pursued them) and gives the account health register real
evidence rather than an impression.

## 7. Measure retention

**Client Retention Rate for a period:**

`CRR = ((E − N) ÷ S) × 100`

where S = clients at the start of the period, E = clients at the end, and
N = new clients won during the period.

Worked example (illustrative): 40 clients on 1 July; 3 new clients won in July;
41 clients on 31 July. CRR = ((41 − 3) ÷ 40) × 100 = 95% for the month.

Track logo retention and revenue retention separately; losing one AA client can
hurt more than losing three D clients. Nelson reports that his own agency kept
about 97% of clients a month and treats roughly 5–8% monthly attrition as
natural. These are **one practitioner's anecdotal figures from a US agency**,
not benchmarks. Set your own target with leadership and label it as agency
policy.

## 8. Checklist

- [ ] Assimilation call held within 5 working days; grade and contact plan set.
- [ ] Set-up sheet complete; credentials stored securely.
- [ ] Launch call held with the copywriter present; owner's phrases captured.
- [ ] Multi-sender welcome sequence scheduled for 60–90 days.
- [ ] Physical welcome delivered with a handwritten note.
- [ ] 90-day review booked before day 60 with the decision-maker.
- [ ] Monthly call runs the homework loop; completion rate tracked.
- [ ] Contact attempts logged; CRR computed monthly.
