# Retainer Design and Cost to Fulfil

Parent skill: [agency-client-retention](../SKILL.md). Read when designing a new
retainer or programme, when a client asks "where is my return?" in the first
months, when turning one-off website builds into recurring relationships, or
when checking whether a client's fee covers what it costs to serve them.

`retainer-package-catalog.md` holds the agency's packages. This file gives the
design rules behind them and the per-client cost sheet that tests them.

## 1. Rule: every retainer pairs a fast signal with a slow compounder

Search visibility, content authority and conversion learning compound over
months. A client paying monthly needs to see something move long before that.
Design each programme with two components:

| Component | Purpose | Examples for a website agency | First visible |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fast signal | Evidence in the first weeks that the programme is working | A focused landing page with tracked enquiries; a WhatsApp enquiry path from the new site; fixing a broken booking or form flow; a paid-search or paid-social campaign run by the client's media partner to a new page | Weeks 2–6 |
| Slow compounder | The durable asset | Search visibility, content library, proof system, conversion improvements | Months 3–12 |

Test each package: "What will the client see by the month-2 review?" If the
answer is only "progress on the build" or "rankings are moving", add a fast
signal or reset expectations in writing before signature. The fast signal is
what carries the client through the 90–120-day window described in
`new-client-assimilation-and-retention-rhythm.md`.

Paid media planning and ad operations belong to the social-media engine
(social-media-skills). Where the fast signal is a paid campaign, this skill
owns the landing page, tracking and reporting; the media plan is handed over
(see §5).

Never promise the fast signal's size. Promise that it will be live, tracked and
reported by a date.

## 2. Wrap the build as the set-up phase of a recurring programme

Project income arrives once and leaves. For a web-design-led agency a strict
"no projects" rule is wrong, because the site is the core deliverable. Instead:

1. **Sequence the build as phase one of a named programme** (for example,
   "Admissions Growth Programme: phase 1 build, phase 2 monthly growth").
2. **Price and scope both phases explicitly.** The build fee and the monthly
   fee appear as separate lines with separate exclusions. Do not hide
   recurring work inside the build fee; `agency-positioning` forbids it.
3. **Agree the start date of the monthly phase at signature**, normally at
   launch or at the 30-day review.
4. **Where the client declines the monthly phase**, give them the self-care
   handbook in `client-site-care-plan.md` and record in writing who owns
   post-launch duties.

Proposal wording for this structure is owned by the proposal engine
(proposal-skills); this file defines the delivery structure only.

## 3. Payment method is part of the model

Recurring revenue that depends on chasing payments is not recurring. At
signature, agree an automated or standing method the client can sustain:
a bank standing order, a direct debit, a card on file, or a mobile-money
merchant arrangement. Invoice on a fixed day with automatic reminders. Avoid
cash collection and "come and collect the cheque" arrangements. Check the
current terms, fees and settlement times of each method with the provider
before offering it; do not quote them from memory.

## 4. Per-client cost-to-fulfil sheet

Build one sheet per programme, then one per client where scope differs.
Three columns per line: the deliverable, its hard cost, and its basis (one-off
set-up, per month or per year).

| # | Line | Hard cost | Basis | Notes |
|---|---|---:|---|---|
| 1 | Design and graphics | | set-up | In-house hours at loaded rate, or supplier invoice |
| 2 | Copywriting and content | | set-up / month | |
| 3 | Development and QA | | set-up | |
| 4 | Hosting, domain, CDN, email sending | | month / year | |
| 5 | Care plan work (backups, checks, updates) | | month | See `client-site-care-plan.md` |
| 6 | Search and content work | | month | |
| 7 | Analytics, call or enquiry tracking tools | | month | Usage-based tools grow with volume |
| 8 | Review or reputation platform, if used | | month | |
| 9 | Reporting and review meetings | | month | Hours from the contact matrix in `client-grading-and-contact-plan.md` |
| 10 | Welcome kit and quarterly physical touches | | set-up / quarter | Including courier |
| 11 | Payment-collection fees | | month | Card, bank or mobile-money merchant charges |
| 12 | Account management and senior time | | month | |

**East African check lines (add where relevant; do not enter a rate until
checked):**

- Mobile-money merchant or payment-gateway fees: confirm the current tariff
  with the provider.
- Withholding tax deducted by clients on service fees: confirm the current
  position and rate with the finance engine before relying on net receipts.
- VAT treatment of the agency's own fees, and VAT charged on foreign digital
  services the agency buys (for example advertising or software): route to the
  finance engine. The currentness register records 18% VAT on non-resident
  digital services in Uganda, with a partial verification status (register
  PL-05); do not rely on it without a finance-engine check.
- USD-priced software subscriptions and the foreign-exchange risk they carry
  against a UGX or KES fee.
- Data bundles, connectivity and power backup where delivery depends on them.

**Totals:** set-up cost per client; monthly cost to serve per client; monthly
fee. The gap between the monthly fee and the monthly cost to serve is the
contribution before overheads.

**Decision gate:** if contribution falls below the margin floor set by
leadership, reprice, narrow scope, automate, or decline. Do not discover this
after six months of delivery.

## 5. Handoffs

| To | What is handed over | Artefact | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|
| Finance engine (chwezi-accounting-doctrine) | Margin and contribution analysis, tax treatment (withholding, VAT), foreign-exchange exposure, revenue recognition for set-up versus monthly fees | Completed cost-to-fulfil sheet with sources for every cost line | Agency finance lead |
| Proposal engine (proposal-skills) | Commercial wording, price presentation and terms for the two-phase programme | Scope and exclusion list per phase; cost floor | Commercial lead |
| Social-media engine (social-media-skills) | Paid media plan and ad operations where the fast signal is a campaign | Landing page URL, tracking plan, reporting schedule | Media partner or social team |

## 6. Worked example (illustrative, UGX)

A school website rebuild takes eight weeks. Search visibility is expected to
build over six to nine months. The programme adds, from week 2 of the monthly
phase, a Term 1 admissions landing page with a WhatsApp enquiry path and form
tracking; the school's media partner drives traffic to it. At the month-2
review the school sees tracked enquiries from the new page, which carries the
relationship through the danger window while the slower search work matures.

Cost sheet (figures illustrative only): monthly cost to serve UGX 2.6M
(content UGX 0.9M, care and hosting UGX 0.4M, tracking and reporting tools
UGX 0.2M, review meetings and account time UGX 0.9M, collection fees and
sundries UGX 0.2M) against a monthly fee of UGX 4.0M. Contribution UGX 1.4M
before overheads, to be tested by the finance lead against the margin floor.
