# Account Cube Expansion Sequence

Parent skill: [agency-client-retention](../SKILL.md). Read when planning where
an existing client's next piece of work should come from, when preparing the
expansion part of a 90-day, quarterly or annual review, or when a client buys a
small share of what they spend on their web and digital presence.

`expansion-offers-matrix.md` lists *what* the agency can sell to an existing
client. This file sets the *order* in which to look for it, the test that
separates a real need from a wish, and the internal-referral asks that open
each new layer.

## 1. The four moves, in order

Grow inside the account one layer at a time. Do not jump to a later move while
an earlier one is open and unexamined.

| Move | Where the growth comes from | Website-agency example |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Service depth | More service lines to the same person | The marketing manager who bought a site adds a care plan, then a CRO sprint |
| 2. Buyer group | Colleagues who own the same kind of problem | The admissions team's site success leads to the alumni office's giving pages |
| 3. Function at the same site | Other departments at the same organisation | HR asks for a careers micro-site; finance asks for an investor page |
| 4. Other locations | The same need at other branches, subsidiaries or countries | The Kampala subsidiary's result is repeated for the Nairobi office |

Keep a **penetration grid** per key client:

| Layer | Named person | Need identified | Status | Next action and date |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Service depth | | | bought / not bought / blocked | |
| Buyer group | | | | |
| Function | | | | |
| Other location | | | | |

"Blocked" means a known reason (a sister agency holds it, budget sits elsewhere,
a policy forbids it). Record the reason; do not keep pitching into it.

## 2. The need-versus-wish test

Every proposed addition must name a business problem the client already owns.
A wish ("it would be nice to have a blog") is not a need. Pass the addition
only if all four hold:

1. **Problem:** Can you state the problem in the client's own words, with the
   person who owns it?
2. **Evidence:** Does the client's data or your account record show the
   problem (a plateaued metric, a failed campaign, a manual workaround)?
3. **Cost of inaction:** Can you state what the problem costs them per month
   or per season, even as a range?
4. **Fit:** Is the addition one of your productised offers, deliverable at
   margin without harming the existing retainer?

If any answer is "no", log it as a watch item, not an offer. This matches the
refusal rules in `expansion-offers-matrix.md` and the evidence rule in
`post-launch-retention-playbook.md`.

## 3. Share-of-wallet review (quarterly, key accounts)

Agencies often undersell: they propose the smallest safe scope for fear of
seeming pushy, and competitors hold the rest.

1. Estimate the client's total spend in your category: site, hosting and care,
   search, landing pages, analytics, content, related freelancers or in-house
   staff time.
2. Compute your share: your annual revenue from the client ÷ that total.
3. List the unmet needs that pass the §2 test.
4. Prepare a fuller recommendation with options, one recommended.
5. Ask for it directly at the next review.

Treat the estimate as a working hypothesis. Mark any figure the client has not
confirmed as an estimate.

## 4. Internal referral asks

Each layer opens with an introduction from someone who has already seen the
result. Ask after a verified win, never before.

- **Service depth:** "The enquiry form is now doing its job. The next
  bottleneck is [named metric]. Would it help if I showed you what fixing that
  involves?"
- **Buyer group:** "Who else here looks after [the same kind of problem] and
  might want to see how we got [result] for your team?"
- **Function:** "You mentioned HR is struggling to attract [role type]. Would
  you be comfortable introducing me to whoever owns that, so I can listen
  first?"
- **Other location:** "If the Nairobi office faces the same [problem], would
  you share this result with their lead and let them decide whether to talk?"

Ask the existing champion to make the introduction; do not approach the new
person cold using details learned inside the account. Confirm that the champion
is comfortable before you use their name.

## 5. Sequencing rules

- **Repair before expansion.** If trust, delivery or margin is weak on the
  current retainer, fix that first (see the decision rules in `../SKILL.md`).
- **One new layer at a time.** Stacking expansions exhausts the client's
  capacity to take part. Respect the two-expansions-per-year cap in
  `expansion-offers-matrix.md`.
- **Launch new offers to existing clients first.** A new service is easier to
  prove with clients who already trust you than with strangers; start the
  pilot here and turn the result into public proof with consent.
- **Frame expansion as solving a named problem.** Never call it an "upsell" in
  front of the client.

## 6. Worked example (illustrative)

A private school group in Kampala bought a Growth Website System and a care
plan.

1. **Service depth:** the 90-day review shows admissions enquiries rising but
   half arrive after the boarding deadline. The need passes the test (owner:
   admissions head; evidence: enquiry dates; cost: unfilled boarding places).
   Offer: a Term 1 admissions landing-page sprint.
2. **Buyer group:** the admissions head introduces the head of the primary
   section, who has the same enquiry problem.
3. **Function:** the bursar mentions fee-payment queries swamp the front
   office. Log as a watch item until the bursar confirms the cost.
4. **Other location:** after the case study is approved, the group's second
   campus lead is introduced by the director.
