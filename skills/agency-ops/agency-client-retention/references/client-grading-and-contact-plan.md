# Client Grading and Contact Plan

Parent skill: [agency-client-retention](../SKILL.md). Read when deciding how much
senior time, how many meetings and which reports each retainer client can
afford, when a client's service cost is outgrowing its fee, or when designing
client surveys that predict renewal.

This file complements `post-launch-review-cadence.md` (which sets *when*
reviews happen) and the Client Health Scorecard in `legacy-guidance.md`
(which rates *how* a client feels). It adds the missing economic step: the
contact each client receives must be affordable from the revenue that client is
expected to bring.

## 1. Write the definition of an active client first

Churn hides inside total counts. Before grading, write a testable definition:

- "An active retainer client has paid at least [amount] in the last [12]
  months and has held at least one review in the last [3] months."
- Classify the whole book as **active**, **lapsed** (no billing or review in
  the window), **at risk** (see §6 triggers) or **former**.

Record people, not only organisations. For each client, keep separate records
for the day-to-day marketing contact, the managing director or owner, the
finance approver and any operational user of the site. Each has their own
contact history, needs and preferred channel.

## 2. Grade clients by expected value

1. Rank clients by **expected** annual revenue (or expected lifetime
   contribution), not by last year's invoices.
2. Set aside two groups before grading: flagship strategic accounts (they get
   a bespoke plan; see `key-client-relationship-health.md`) and very small,
   infrequent buyers (they get a reactive, low-cost stream).
3. Split the rest into five grades by count:

| Grade | Share of graded clients (heuristic) | Default treatment |
|---|---|---|
| AA | top ~5% | Named senior lead, in-person or video reviews, sponsor contact |
| A | next ~15% | Monthly live review, quarterly strategy session |
| B | next ~25% | Monthly live review, twice-yearly strategy session |
| C | next ~25% | Monthly written report plus short call, annual review |
| D | last ~30% | Written report and digital updates; reviews on request |

The five-way split is a practitioner heuristic from Hunter and Tietyen, not a
statistical rule. Adjust the shares to your book, but keep five steps: they give
enough resolution to balance contact against value.

4. **Promote look-alikes.** A C client whose profile resembles your A clients
   (growing sector, expanding team, strong decision-maker access) gets a
   time-boxed uplift in contact to test whether revenue follows.

## 3. Build the contact matrix

For each grade, list every contact type, its frequency per year and its unit
cost. Unit cost is senior and staff time at loaded internal rates plus any
travel or courier cost.

| Contact type | Unit cost (fill in) | AA | A | B | C | D |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| In-person or video strategy review | | | | | | |
| Monthly live review call | | | | | | |
| Written monthly report | | | | | | |
| Insight note or trend brief | | | | | | |
| WhatsApp or email value update | | | | | | |
| Physical touch (welcome kit, quarterly surprise) | | | | | | |

**Affordability check (per grade):**

`Annual contact cost per client ÷ expected annual revenue per client = contact-to-revenue ratio`

Set a target ratio per grade with the finance owner. Where the ratio is over
target, move content down the cost ladder (visit → call → written or digital)
until it fits. Do not cut the content the client values; change the channel
that carries it.

**Rules:**

- Start from what the client says they value (§4), then reconcile with cost.
  Customer preference usually exceeds budget; close the gap with lower-cost
  value contacts, not more staff.
- Lower grades get fewer but denser contacts. If C receives a third of A's
  updates, each C update carries about three pieces of useful content.
- Judge the whole contact system by retention, expansion and the ratio above,
  never by open or reply rates of individual messages.
- **Capacity check:** total the senior review hours the matrix demands and
  compare them with the hours your account leads actually have (see capacity
  math in `hybrid-agency-operating-model.md`). A gap of two to three times is
  common; fix it in the matrix, not by overpromising.

**Cost-to-serve trigger.** When a client's contact and delivery cost breaks the
target ratio for three consecutive months, choose one: reprice, repackage to a
productised lower-touch tier, narrow scope, or exit. Record the decision in the
retention decision log. Margin analysis itself belongs to the finance engine
(chwezi-accounting-doctrine); see `retainer-design-and-cost-to-fulfil.md`.

## 4. Ask clients what contact they value

Run a short **client council** once a year (for example 6–8 retainer clients,
90 minutes, lunch provided) or collect the same answers a few questions at a
time during routine calls:

1. Which of our updates do you actually use? Rank the top five.
2. How often do you want each one?
3. In which format: WhatsApp summary, emailed PDF, live dashboard, voice note,
   in-person review?

Record the answers as a grid: content type × frequency × channel, coded
Preferred / Acceptable / Support-only. The grid becomes the input to §3.

## 5. Behavioural surveys that predict renewal

Liking surveys do not predict churn; many clients who leave describe themselves
as satisfied. Measure intent and behaviour instead.

**Six design rules:**

1. **Survey in real time**, tied to a recent event: within 7–10 days of a
   launch, a monthly review or a resolved issue.
2. **Do not survey current clients anonymously.** Say who is asking. It lets you
   follow up and it signals that you care. (Anonymity may still be right for
   internal staff pulses, and for peer appraisal in some cultures; that is a
   different instrument.)
3. **Ask the behavioural pair:** "Based on the last quarter, how likely are you
   to renew with us?" and "Would you be comfortable recommending us to a
   colleague in [sector]?"
4. **Ask about problems directly**: "Did anything nearly go wrong or frustrate
   you this month? Did you tell us?" Cross-check against the issue log.
5. **Close the loop.** Tell the client what changed because of their answer,
   by name and date.
6. **Validate against behaviour.** Compare stated intent with what the client
   did six months later (renewed, expanded, referred, reduced scope).

**Segment each client on the answers plus behaviour:**

| Segment | Stated view | Behaviour | Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| Secure | Will renew; would recommend | Share of their spend with us rising | Ask for referral or case study |
| Complacent | Probably renew; probably recommend | Flat | Seed the next 90 days; show new value |
| At risk | Unsure | Flat at best | Senior call within one week; repair plan |
| Defecting | Unlikely | Falling scope, slow replies | Founder-level conversation; decide repair or clean exit |

Weight answers by grade when summarising: one AA client's "unsure" matters more
than several D clients' "yes".

**Data protection.** Survey responses are personal data. Tell the client what
you collect and why, keep it within your client-management purpose, and honour
any objection. In Uganda, register with the PDPO and obtain consent to collect
(register PL-01); in Kenya, confirm registration status with the ODPC
(register PL-02).

## 6. Early-warning triggers

Add these rule-based alerts to the health register. Each fires a named owner
and a dated action.

- No content or copy approval from the client for 14 days.
- A missed monthly review, or a delegate sent twice in a row.
- The client's own advertising or marketing spend drops.
- A change of marketing lead, managing director or finance approver.
- An invoice overdue beyond the agreed cure period.
- Replies on WhatsApp or email noticeably slower for a month.
- Fewer brief requests or content inputs than the plan assumes.

## 7. Output checklist

- [ ] Active-client definition written and applied.
- [ ] Every client graded AA–D with the expected-value basis recorded.
- [ ] Contact matrix costed; ratio per grade within target or flagged.
- [ ] Capacity check done against real account-lead hours.
- [ ] Client-defined contact grid on file (council or call-by-call).
- [ ] Behavioural survey scheduled after the next trigger event.
- [ ] Early-warning triggers wired into the account health register.
