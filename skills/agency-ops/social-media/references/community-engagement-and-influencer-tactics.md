# Community, Engagement and Influencer Tactics

Parent skill: [social-media](../SKILL.md). Read when planning community building, influencer or creator partnerships, review handling, contests, feedback incentives, added-value offers, or the listening sources that feed daily trend monitoring.

Channel roles, content buckets, repurposing, and response-time rules live in `legacy-guidance.md` and `platform-playbook.md`. This file adds the engagement and partnership tactics those files do not detail.

---

## 1. Community before broadcast

- Behave as a helpful member of the community, not only as a supplier. The relationship matters more than any single product post.
- Create fewer posts that resonate more; quality of response beats volume of output.
- Build a space where experienced customers help prospective ones (a group, forum, or member channel). Appoint a moderator with subject knowledge and publish short community rules: purpose, what is welcome, what is removed, and how disputes are handled.
- Seed conversation: post open questions, introduce members to each other, and highlight useful member contributions.
- Enforce rules firmly but courteously; explain removals privately where possible.
- Track community health by member-to-member replies, not only by brand-to-member replies.

## 2. Influencer and creator partnerships

### Plan
1. Write the objective (awareness, content, sales, event attendance) and tie it to the overall marketing plan.
2. Define the audience the partner must reach and the character attributes the partner must share.
3. Prefer creators with smaller, niche, highly engaged audiences where relevance matters more than reach; test before committing to large-audience partners.
4. Judge authenticity first: does the creator already use or credibly relate to the product or category?
5. Check the creator's audience quality (comment substance, audience location and fit) with the platform's data or a verified tool before paying.
6. Agree deliverables, approvals, usage rights, disclosure wording, and payment in writing.
7. Require clear disclosure of every paid or gifted relationship; check current local advertising rules and platform branded-content policies before launch.
8. Measure with tracked links or codes per creator; report cost per outcome, not only reach.

### Ambassadors and advocacy
- Identify existing customers, staff, and partners who already speak well of the brand.
- Give them early access, shareable assets, and recognition rather than scripted posts.
- Keep a simple ambassador register: name, channel, permission status, last contribution.

## 3. Reviews and feedback

### Review monitoring
- List every review surface relevant to the business (search maps listing, sector directories, travel or marketplace sites, social pages).
- Check them on a fixed schedule and log each review with date, rating, and theme.
- Respond to every review, positive and negative. Prospects read the response as evidence of how the business treats people.
- For negative reviews: thank, acknowledge the specific issue, avoid argument, move resolution to a private channel, and follow up publicly once resolved. Escalation tiers are in `legacy-guidance.md`.
- Feed recurring themes into service fixes and website FAQ content.

### Asking for feedback
- Give customers clear reasons and easy routes to leave feedback.
- Incentives (discounts, entry into a draw) may be restricted: many review platforms and consumer-protection rules prohibit rewarding reviews or rewarding only positive ones. Check the current policy of each platform and the local law before offering any incentive, and never condition a reward on sentiment.
- Test different request timings and wording rather than escalating rewards.

## 4. Contests, promotions and direct engagement

- **Photo or video contests:** ask customers to share the product or service in use; state entry rules, judging criteria, eligibility, prize, closing date, and data use. Check local promotion or lottery rules and platform promotion policies before launch.
- **Seasonal promotions:** plan against the client's calendar of public holidays, sector events, and cultural dates at least six weeks ahead.
- **Choice posts:** show two options and ask which the audience prefers; use the answers as light product or content research.
- **Added-value offers:** free webinars, how-to guidance, short consultations, return-customer offers, or referral rewards (see `../../referral-program/SKILL.md`). Each offer must have a tracked destination.
- **Humanise:** use promotions to show the people behind the brand, within the character sheet.

## 5. Listening and trend sources

Set these up on day one and review them in the daily routine.

| Source type | Use | Set-up note |
|---|---|---|
| Keyword alerts | Brand name, founder names, product names, competitor names, sector terms | Include common misspellings |
| Platform trending panels | Emerging topics in the client's country and sector | Check daily; trends can fade within hours |
| Meme and culture trackers | Understand a format before using it | Confirm meaning and origin before joining in |
| Awareness-day and holiday calendars | Plan dated content in advance | Verify each date for the client's country |
| Topic-audience search tools | Find accounts that discuss a subject | Use for list building and partner research |
| Competitor accounts | Content types, volume, partnerships, promotions | Monitor a fixed set of five to ten rivals |

Before joining a news topic (newsjacking), check: is it relevant to the brand's purpose, is the tone appropriate to the event, and does it add value rather than exploit a sensitive story? When in doubt, stay silent. Idea sources for newsjacking are in `../../../content-copy/blog-idea-generator/references/ideation-frameworks.md`.

## 6. Content support tactics

- Share each new blog post as a social update to prompt return visits; mention other organisations genuinely where relevant so they have reason to reshare.
- Add a discreet brand mark to original infographics and visuals so shared copies stay attributable.
- Use audio where the audience listens: short audio clips, recorded customer testimonials (with consent), and audio versions of articles.
- Test paid social beyond the largest network when the audience is present elsewhere; start with small, tracked budgets.
- Keep learning from the platforms' own current guidance; platform features and algorithms change often, so date-stamp any platform claim used in client work.
