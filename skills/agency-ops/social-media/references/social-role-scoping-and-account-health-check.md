# Social Role Scoping and Account Health Check

Parent skill: [social-media](../SKILL.md). Read when scoping a social-media engagement, deciding which kind of social specialist a client needs, hiring or briefing a social-media manager, or running a free or paid health check on an existing social presence.

Social-media management is several jobs at once. Scope the engagement by naming which duties and which specialist roles the client actually needs, then audit the current presence against the common failure patterns before quoting.

---

## 1. Duty map (four layers)

Use this map to agree scope in writing. Tick each duty as `in scope`, `client-owned`, or `excluded`. Anything left blank becomes scope creep later.

### Strategy layer
- [ ] Own the channel strategy: which platforms, which objective per platform.
- [ ] Define customer personas and the goal each persona serves.
- [ ] Build the operating budget (people, tools, paid amplification).
- [ ] Plan campaigns from objective to day-to-day execution.
- [ ] Recruit or brief the people who produce and approve content.
- [ ] Integrate social into how the organisation works (approvals, staff participation, escalation).
- [ ] Recruit staff as voluntary amplifiers to supplement organic reach, with a written staff social policy.
- [ ] Align with the other marketing functions so each activity reinforces the others.

### Implementation layer
- [ ] Set KPIs and measure leads, reach, sales, and engagement against them.
- [ ] Run campaigns and daily activity without micro-managing contributors.
- [ ] Publish content, respond to comments, and share material from partners, fans, and customers (with permission).
- [ ] Find blogs, forums, and creators to collaborate with (advocacy).
- [ ] Build and maintain a network of brand ambassadors.
- [ ] Take part in community groups where the business's problems are discussed.
- [ ] Manage influencer outreach and partnerships.
- [ ] Answer questions and join relevant debates.

### Content layer
- [ ] Seed content into third-party communities, not only owned profiles.
- [ ] Write editorial content to an agreed weekly output target (set the target from capacity and budget, not from a generic quota).
- [ ] Produce short-form and long-form video where the channel plan requires it.
- [ ] Comment on and contribute to relevant blogs and publications.
- [ ] Maintain the editorial calendar and posting schedule.

### Monitoring layer
- [ ] Record baseline metrics before the engagement starts.
- [ ] Test tactics and analyse campaign results.
- [ ] Run periodic effectiveness reviews.
- [ ] Track trends and competitor activity.
- [ ] Monitor brand mentions and the relationship between social activity and search or website traffic.

## 2. Eight specialist roles

A single manager rarely covers all eight well. Identify the two or three roles the client most needs, then staff or price accordingly.

| Role | What it delivers | Core skills to check | Choose it when |
|---|---|---|---|
| Copywriter | Platform-specific posts from one-line updates to long captions, in one consistent brand voice | Brand voice, adaptability, concision, placement judgement | The client has material but no one who can write it for each channel |
| Multimedia producer | Photos, graphics, short video, stories optimised for mobile | Design software, basic photography and video, visual judgement | The channel plan is visual and the client has no in-house production |
| Metric analyst | Macro results (reach, leads) and micro tests (post variants, frequency) | Data analysis, experiment design, presentation of findings | The client spends money on social and cannot say what it returns |
| News curator | Timely, tactful commentary on news, trends, and tags; acts as an extension of PR | Monitoring discipline, fast judgement, restraint | The brand's authority depends on being current in its field |
| Customer service representative | Brand spokesperson in public and private messages across purchase stages and moods | Calm under pressure, problem-solving, product knowledge | Customers already use social channels to ask questions or complain |
| Community manager | A community where members talk to each other, not only to the brand; seed questions, moderation, rules | Connecting people, facilitation, firm but gentle moderation | The business benefits from peer support, groups, or member networks |
| Funnel manager | Content matched to top, middle, and bottom of the funnel; handover to sales | Funnel understanding, basic sales skill, liaison with sales and service | Social must produce qualified leads or sales, not only attention |
| Campaign coordinator | Triage of internal requests for social sharing; coordination with other agencies at launches | Organisation, negotiation, data-based prioritisation | Many departments or partners compete for the same channels |

### Scoping procedure
1. Ask the client what social media must achieve: trending content, awareness, website traffic, lead generation, or exposure to a defined audience. Record one primary objective.
2. Map the objective to roles: awareness to copywriter and producer; leads to funnel manager and analyst; retention to service and community roles.
3. Mark the roles the client already covers internally.
4. Price only the uncovered roles and state the others as client-owned.

## 3. Hiring and briefing rubric

Score candidates or freelancers 1–3 on each line. Treat any 1 on the first three as a disqualifier for a client-facing role.

**Traits**
- Genuine interest in the medium; active personally on more than one platform.
- Communication: listens well, collaborates across departments, keeps continuity when handing over.
- Flexibility: can respond out of hours when the service level requires it, thinks on their feet, admits and corrects mistakes.

**Skills (in priority order)**
1. Communication: writes for any platform, length, or audience; communicates with internal stakeholders and customers.
2. Writing: concise copy that provokes a response; adapts voice per platform.
3. Creativity: produces differentiated content and proposes calculated creative risks within brand character.
4. Organisation: runs from a calendar; protects focus time in fixed blocks; batches similar tasks.
5. Marketing knowledge: understands email, events, lead generation, PR, and where social fits in the mix.
6. Customer care: anticipates needs and uses conversation history to personalise replies.
7. Relationship building: forms one-to-one digital relationships; understands the audience from observation and from remembered conversations.
8. Agility: pivots on new trends, acts decisively in a crisis, revises long-term plans when evidence demands.
9. Data analysis: reads quantitative and qualitative data and turns it into an action plan stakeholders understand.

## 4. Daily list-building by intent

When the plan includes growing a targeted following, reach a small number of relevant new accounts each working day and file them in private lists by intent: potential partners or creators, campaign participants, prospects, and press. Review list activity weekly instead of scrolling the main feed. Do not use automated follow/unfollow tactics; check the platform's current terms before using any growth tool.

## 5. Account health check (audit procedure)

Use as a free door-opener or as a paid diagnostic. Deliver a written report, not a verbal opinion.

### Procedure
1. Collect access or public URLs for every account the business has opened, including dormant ones.
2. Record baseline figures for the last 90 days per account: posting frequency, replies to comments and messages, link clicks, leads or enquiries traced to social, and share or save activity. Mark anything unavailable as `not assessed`.
3. Score each check below as Pass, Partial, or Fail.
4. Convert the result into a simple percentage (Pass = 2, Partial = 1, Fail = 0, divided by the maximum) so the client can see progress at the next review. State that the score is your own rubric, not an industry standard.
5. List the three highest-impact fixes and the effort each needs.
6. Close with a recommended scope from the duty map and roles in sections 1–2.

### Checks (common failure patterns)
| # | Check | Fail signal |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Every open account is active or formally closed | Dormant profiles that make the business look unresponsive |
| 2 | Posts serve what the customer values, not only what the business wants to announce | Feed is mostly promotions and sales messages |
| 3 | Two-way engagement happens | Unanswered comments, questions, or messages |
| 4 | Posts carry relevant visuals | Text-only posts on visual channels |
| 5 | Success is judged by response quality, not follower count | Reports cite follower totals only |
| 6 | The business concentrates on the channels it can sustain | Thin activity spread across many platforms |
| 7 | A written strategy states objectives and how success is measured | No document; activity is improvised |
| 8 | Measurement includes leads, link clicks, and impressions, not vanity figures alone | No tracked links or lead attribution |
| 9 | Content is adapted to each platform's format and register | Identical copy pasted to every channel |
| 10 | Someone monitors trends and conversations during working hours | Brand reacts days late or not at all |

### Report skeleton
- Current state (one paragraph, evidence-based).
- Score table with the ten checks.
- Top three fixes, owner, and effort.
- Recommended scope and next step (proposal or strategy workshop).

Before quoting any industry figure in the report (engagement benchmarks, response expectations, platform reach), check a current dated source and cite it, or omit it.
