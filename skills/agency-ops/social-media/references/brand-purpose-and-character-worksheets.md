# Brand Purpose and Character Worksheets

Parent skill: [social-media](../SKILL.md). Read when onboarding a social-media client or founder, writing the purpose statement and character rules that govern every post, segmenting audiences by channel, or deciding how to react to analytics without drifting from brand values.

These worksheets complete the three-element model (purpose, character, content) described in `legacy-guidance.md`. Run them in the order below at onboarding and revisit them once a year.

---

## 1. Purpose worksheet

Run this as a facilitated session with the founder or leadership. Record answers verbatim first, then edit.

### Step 1 — Past successes
List three to five successes, professional or personal. Small wins count (first client, first account launched, first repeat customer). Purpose: surface evidence of what the business is genuinely good at.

### Step 2 — Contributions
List work that has helped more people than the business itself: mentoring, community projects, advocacy, process improvements shared with others. Purpose: reveal the kind of impact and working model that fits.

### Step 3 — Goals on three horizons
| Horizon | Guidance | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| One year | Attainable; within reach with current resources | |
| Five years | Needs a visible action plan and significant effort | |
| Ten years | May be broad; should feel slightly daunting and exciting | |

### Step 4 — Purpose statement
Complete the slot template:

> We are [name], which is [what we do, specifically how we differ], and what we want to do for people is [impact].

Tests before accepting it:
- Could a referrer repeat it after one hearing?
- Does it control the narrative about what the business does, rather than just announce its name?
- Would it help a stranger decide whether to refer someone?
- Is it specific enough to reject content that does not fit?

For a client engagement, add a social-specific line: "On social media we exist to [objective] for [audience]."

## 2. Character attributes

1. Offer a list of candidate attributes (for example: creativity, integrity, community, freedom, meaningful work, warmth, directness, expertise).
2. Ask the client to choose eight to ten and rank them.
3. For the top three, write one "sounds like" and one "never sounds like" example each.
4. Check the online persona against the in-person experience: a follower who later meets the team should not notice a jarring difference.
5. Use the ranking to resolve tone disputes; a higher-ranked attribute wins.

---

## 3. Ten rules of social character

Give these to everyone who posts or replies on the account.

1. **Be kind.** Approach disagreement diplomatically; goodwill compounds over years.
2. **Show, do not boast.** Let the work demonstrate competence; think about the reader on the other end before posting.
3. **Write as you speak.** Plain, natural phrasing; add cues that replace the missing tone of voice.
4. **Be genuine.** Audiences detect pretence; do not adopt a persona that contradicts the character sheet.
5. **Listen.** Read and respond to what the community posts, not only to replies on your own posts.
6. **Contribute.** Share resources and ideas that help others; generosity drives referrals.
7. **Do not overshare.** Choose what to share with judgement; respect the audience's time and keep some reserve.
8. **Support others.** Celebrate other people's successes publicly and specifically.
9. **Stay grounded.** Do not believe your own hype; protect time to create rather than only publish.
10. **Hold to your values.** Do not post controversial material for attention if it contradicts the ranked attributes.

## 4. Content intersection check

Before approving a content pillar, place it on the three circles: profession (capability and results), personality (interests and values), and passions (what matters beyond work). Prefer ideas in the overlap of all three. Phrase posts so they invite a reply: end with an open question or an honest prompt for the reader's view.

## 5. Audience segment per channel

The same brand meets different audiences on different channels. Fill one row per active channel during onboarding and brief content to that row, not to "our audience" in general.

| Channel role (see 4-ring model) | Typical segment | What they want | Content emphasis |
|---|---|---|---|
| Home base (website, blog) | Loyal followers and serious prospects | Depth, long-form, the full story | All content buckets |
| Gallery or amplifier | Visually driven followers | How the brand shows up in real life | Process, finished work, inspiration |
| Voice or soapbox | Smaller, engaged, opinion-driven readers | Commentary, news, humour, culture | Process, inspiration, community |
| Discovery channel | Broad, less loyal, often new to the brand | Tips, shareable solutions, ideas to save | Finished work, inspiration, practical tips |

Check the actual segment in each platform's own audience data before finalising; the table is a starting hypothesis.

## 6. Onboarding sequence

1. Write the purpose statement and the social-specific line (section 1).
2. Rank the character attributes and issue the ten rules (sections 2–3).
3. Weight the five content buckets for this client (see `legacy-guidance.md`).
4. Select two to four channels using the 4-ring model.
5. Give each channel one role; no two channels may share the same role entirely.
6. Set a posting cadence each channel can sustain with the agreed capacity; check current platform guidance before quoting frequencies.
7. Record the audience segment per channel (section 5).
8. Set KPIs beyond vanity figures: leads, link clicks, impressions, and engagement rate per channel.

## 7. Analytics-led iteration rules

Use data to make better decisions that stay aligned with the purpose and character, not to chase follower growth for its own sake.

- **After a post performs far above normal:** record why it may have worked, but do not force exact repeats; test one variable at a time.
- **After a post loses followers:** ask whether it breached the character sheet. If it did, adjust. If it was deliberate and on-values, accept the loss.
- **Frequency:** compare engagement in periods of higher and lower posting volume before changing cadence; change only one factor per test period.
- **Format:** compare formats (for example illustrated versus plain-text posts) over equal periods and equal topics before concluding.
- **Personalisation:** when data shows a segment responds to a theme, adapt copy and offers for that segment on its channel.
- **Cadence:** observe continuously, review formally each month, and rerun the worksheets each year.

---

## 8. Personal brand as business development

Use with founders, consultants, and freelancers whose reputation drives sales.

- Treat public profiles as the working portfolio: clients and employers check them before any CV or proposal.
- Make referral easy: a clear purpose statement lets collaborators describe and recommend the business in one sentence.
- Stay visible: regular, useful posting keeps the person top of mind when a need arises.
- Document work as it happens (process posts, short case notes); this keeps the portfolio current and prompts inbound enquiries.
- Remember that people recommend those whose reputation reflects well on them; protect that by staying on-character.
- Avoid describing too many services at once; an unclear offer costs referrals.
