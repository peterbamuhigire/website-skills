# Points of Difference and Pitch Lines

Parent skill: [agency-positioning](../SKILL.md). Read when writing or testing
the agency's differentiators, a "category of one" line, elevator pitches for
events and introductions, the founder's short back story, or when reviewing
sales and About-page language for filler.

`positioning-language.md` rewrites service language into outcome language and
holds the competitive-differentiation script. This file supplies the tests and
slot-templates that come before it: separating real differences from table
stakes, and producing spoken lines people can repeat.

## 1. Sort claims into parity and difference

Most of what any agency offers, its competitors also offer. Those are
**points of parity (POPs)**: necessary, but they do not win the choice.
**Points of difference (PODs)** are what a buyer cannot get elsewhere.

**Procedure:**

1. List every claim on the home page, About page, proposal cover and
   credentials deck.
2. Mark each one POP or POD. Test: could two named local competitors say the
   same sentence truthfully? If yes, it is a POP.
3. For each remaining POD, attach evidence (a case, a method document, a
   published benchmark, a named team capability). A POD without evidence is a
   hope; drop it or earn it.
4. Keep POPs, but say them briefly and pair them with a POD.

Common POPs to stop leading with: "great service", "experienced team",
"years in business", "creative", "passionate", "affordable", "on time".

**Uncopyable elements** usually sit in: the founder's story and values, a named
method, proprietary data or benchmarks, a specific team mix, and relationships
in the niche. Look there for PODs.

**POD line template:**

"Yes, we [parity item], like any serious agency. The difference is that we
also [point of difference], which means you [client benefit]."

## 2. The category-of-one line

Define a category small enough that the agency is first, only or best in it.

**Template:** "We are the only [category] in [market] that [distinctive
mechanism] for [niche]."

Rules:

- The mechanism must be true, checkable and still true next year.
- "Only" and "first" claims need evidence on file. If you cannot prove "only",
  use "the [category] that [mechanism]" without the superlative.
- Test it with the positioning-statement tests in `niche-selection-rubric.md`
  (a stranger understands it in 12 seconds and can repeat it).

**Passability test.** After hearing it once, can someone else repeat the idea
accurately in one sentence? If not, simplify.

## 3. Elevator pitches in three lengths

Prepare several versions per audience and length. Each includes, in order:
who you help, the outcome, a short proof story, and what you are working on
now.

| Length | Use | Template |
|---|---|---|
| 10 seconds | Introductions, name tags, WhatsApp bios | "I help [specific audience] [achieve outcome]." |
| 30 seconds | Events, first calls | "I help [audience] [achieve outcome] without [common pain]. Last quarter, for example, [short proof story]. Right now I'm working on [current project], which is why I'm here." |
| 2 minutes | Panels, podcast intros, first meetings | 30-second version + how the method works in three steps + one client story with a verified number + an invitation |

Write audience variants (for example: school bursars, clinic owners, a
development-partner programme officer). Rehearse them aloud until they sound
like speech, not copy.

## 4. The founder's back story (60–90 words)

"I started in [origin]. I kept seeing [problem] — [one concrete scene]. After
[struggle or turning point], I built [method or solution]. Today I [what you
do] so that [who] can [outcome]. That's why I care about [cause]."

Keep it true and checkable. For longer founder stories, use
`founder-story-templates.md` and the epiphany-bridge structure in
`expert-authority-framework.md`.

## 5. Cut filler words

In pitches, calls and recorded video, remove "nothing words" that weaken
authority: *basically, literally, obviously, I mean, probably, kind of, sort
of, I think, just, you know, like*. Keep "I think" only where genuine
uncertainty must be signalled.

**Lock in value** instead of dismissing real effort. When a client thanks you
for something that took work, do not say "no problem" or "it was nothing".
Say: "Thank you. I was glad to make it happen for you."

## 6. Before-and-after rewrites (illustrative)

| Before | After |
|---|---|
| "We're basically a creative agency that does, like, websites and digital stuff." | "We build enquiry-ready websites for Kampala private schools. Last year three of our schools filled Term 1 boarding places by December." |
| "Experienced team, quality service, client focus." | "Every build is led by a strategist and a developer together from the first workshop, so the page plan and the performance budget are decided on the same day." |
| "We are an award-winning, passionate team." | "Our method is documented in a 12-page playbook we share with every client at kickoff." |
| "No problem, it was nothing." | "Thank you. It took some juggling, and I was glad to do it." |

## 7. Checklist

- [ ] Every claim marked POP or POD; each POD has evidence on file.
- [ ] One category-of-one line that passes the 12-second and passability tests.
- [ ] Elevator pitches at 10 s, 30 s and 2 min, with audience variants.
- [ ] Back story written, true and under 90 words.
- [ ] Filler words removed from the sales script and video scripts.
