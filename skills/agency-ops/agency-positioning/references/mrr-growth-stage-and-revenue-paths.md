# MRR Growth Stage and Revenue Paths

Parent skill: [agency-positioning](../SKILL.md). Read when setting the agency's
twelve-month revenue target, choosing the monthly fee level for the core
programme, deciding how many clients and sales conversations the target needs,
or diagnosing why the founder is stuck at a stage.

This is a planning structure. The agency's own business plan belongs to the
business-plan engine (business-plan-skills) and its financial model to the
finance engine (chwezi-accounting-doctrine); see the handoff in §6.

## 1. Name the growth stage

Stages are defined by the problem the owner faces, not by fixed amounts. Set
the thresholds from your own cost base (salaries, rent, tools, owner draw).

| Stage | What is true | Typical constraint |
|---|---|---|
| Start-up | No stable client base; owner does everything | Finding the first repeatable clients |
| Struggle | A handful of clients; still cannot afford a hire | Owner time; cash flow |
| Scale | Enough recurring revenue to reinvest in process and people | Owner must leave delivery |
| Success | A team runs delivery and accounts | Management depth; systems |
| Significance | Annual revenue at the owner's long-term goal | Keeping quality and culture |

**Illustrative UGX thresholds** (monthly recurring revenue; for structure only,
to be recalibrated against local salary and rent data): Start-up under
UGX 10M; Struggle UGX 10–20M; Scale UGX 20–60M; Success UGX 60M and above;
Significance at the owner's annual goal. Do not import USD thresholds from
foreign sources.

Tie hiring triggers to the stage thresholds you set, not to feelings of
overload. See `../../agency-client-retention/references/hybrid-agency-operating-model.md`
for capacity-based hiring rules.

## 2. Compute the gap

1. Record current monthly recurring revenue (MRR).
2. Record the target MRR in twelve months.
3. Gap = target − current.
4. Name the stage now, in twelve months and in three years.

## 3. Build the revenue-paths table

Selling a larger programme takes about as much sales effort as selling a small
one, and small-fee clients are often the most demanding. Lay out several fee
levels side by side and choose the one your lead-generation method can
actually feed.

**Illustrative example:** annual target UGX 1.2B, which is UGX 100M MRR.

| Monthly fee | Clients needed | Implication |
|---:|---:|---|
| UGX 10M | 10 | Few buyers can pay; long sales cycle; concentration risk |
| UGX 5M | 20 | Workable with a strong niche and proof |
| UGX 2.5M | 40 | Workable with a steady lead engine and a team |
| UGX 1M | 100 | High sales volume and service load; hard for a small team |
| UGX 0.5M | 200 | Usually unworkable for a premium agency |

For each row, add three more columns: gross wins needed per month after
churn, sales conversations needed at your close rate, and bookings needed at
your show rate (§4). Pick the fee level where those numbers are achievable.

**Floor principle.** If the agency cannot win clients at its floor fee, it is
probably not solving a problem large enough, lacks proof, or lacks confidence
in its price. Test that belief against firms already paying for marketing
before lowering the floor. Use the floors in `pricing-rules.md` and
`uganda-market-pricing-calibration.md`.

## 4. Back-solve the pipeline

`net new clients needed ÷ 12 = net wins per month`

`gross wins per month = net wins + expected churned clients per month`

`sales conversations per month = gross wins ÷ close rate`

`bookings per month = sales conversations ÷ show rate`

`contacts, registrants or enquiries per month = bookings ÷ booking rate`

Use your own measured rates. Where you have none, run the first quarter to
measure them and mark the plan `provisional`.

**Worked example (illustrative, UGX).** Current MRR UGX 18M (12 clients at
UGX 1.5M). Target UGX 60M in twelve months. Gap UGX 42M. New clients join a
UGX 3M programme, so 14 net new clients are needed. Allowing for churn on a
growing base, plan about 18 gross wins, or 1.5 a month. At an assumed 25%
close rate on diagnostic meetings, that is 6 meetings a month; at an assumed
70% show rate, about 9 bookings a month. The question then becomes which lead
engine (association talks, webinars, partner introductions) can produce 9
bookings a month, not "post more".

## 5. Review cadence

Set monthly, quarterly and annual goals. At each, stop and review: celebrate
what worked, or diagnose the cause (lead volume, conversion, churn or fee) and
change one thing.
