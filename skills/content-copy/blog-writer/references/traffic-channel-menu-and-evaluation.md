# Traffic Channel Menu and Evaluation

Parent skill: [blog-writer](../SKILL.md). Read when planning how published content will reach readers beyond search and the email list, choosing which off-site channels to test, joining question-and-answer communities, or judging whether a channel and a piece of content are earning their cost.

Core distribution (email, LinkedIn, niche communities, X, Quora answers, Facebook, slide platforms, YouTube, email signature, guest posts, syndication) is in [content-distribution.md](content-distribution.md). This file adds the job content must do, the evaluation rules, the participation rules for Q&A communities, and the wider menu of traffic tactics.

## 1. The job content must do

Content marketing attracts, engages, and acquires customers by creating or curating material the audience values.

| Stage | Reader becomes | Content job | Check |
|---|---|---|---|
| Attract | Audience | Be found and be worth opening | Does the piece answer something the reader searches or asks? |
| Engage | Prospect | Build familiarity and trust over repeated visits | Is there a reason to return or subscribe? |
| Acquire | Customer | Offer a clear next step when the reader is ready | Does the piece end with a relevant call to action? |

Two tests for every piece:

1. **Value test.** The piece must be educational (answers a question, solves a problem) or entertaining (the reader enjoys it for its own sake), and preferably both. Advertising that offers neither fails.
2. **Capture test.** The piece must move the reader somewhere: subscription, related content, consultation, or purchase. Content published only to fill a calendar fails.

## 2. Evaluate the channel before scaling it

Traffic is the top of the funnel, not the goal. Before adding a channel or increasing spend on one:

1. **Destination ready.** Confirm the receiving page converts: clear offer, sound copy, usable design, and a product people want. Fix the destination before buying or chasing more visitors.
2. **Product fit first.** Look for signs that the offer already satisfies buyers: customers refer others unprompted, usage spreads without paid push, and customers return without reminders. Without these, more traffic mostly measures the leak.
3. **Unit economics per channel.** For each channel, estimate customer lifetime value and the cost to acquire a customer through that channel (money plus staff time). A channel is worth scaling only when acquisition cost sits comfortably below lifetime value. Use the definitions in `commerce/ecommerce-analytics/references/kpi-framework.md` for CAC and CLV.
4. **Test several, keep few.** Run small, time-boxed tests on two or three channels, measure qualified outcomes, and keep the ones that pay. Record each test's hypothesis, cost, and result.

## 3. Signals that a piece of content is working

Judge quality by what readers do with it, not by visits alone:

- saves and bookmarks;
- shares, especially by people who do not already follow the business;
- newsletter sign-ups from the piece;
- links from other writers and publications;
- substantive comments and replies;
- return visits from the same readers.

Report these alongside traffic and conversions in the monthly review.

## 4. Question-and-answer community participation

Q&A sites and forums reach people at the moment they ask. Participation earns attention only when it serves the asker first.

Procedure:

1. **Scout.** Read the community's rules and etiquette before posting. Note which questions have many followers, votes, or views; these reach a larger audience.
2. **Select questions.** Answer questions the target audience asks, and prefer those whose best answer delivers the same value the offer delivers ("best resources to learn [area]", "best tools for [task]", "how can I [outcome]").
3. **Answer fully in place.** The answer must stand on its own without a click. Link to the business's page only where it adds depth the answer cannot hold.
4. **Disclose.** State any affiliation with a product or business you mention, as the platform's policy requires.
5. **Set the profile.** Where the platform shows a short bio beside each answer, tailor it to the topic and link it to the site.
6. **Measure and persist.** Use the platform's own statistics to see which answers draw views and visits. Results build as answers and followers accumulate.
7. **Do not republish whole posts.** Posting a full blog article on a third-party blog or answer feature creates duplicate copies. Write a summary answer and link to the original, or use a platform that supports a canonical reference to the original (see [content-distribution.md](content-distribution.md) "High-DA Platform Syndication").
8. **Platform promotion credits or boosts.** Some platforms let members spend earned credits or pay to promote answers. Check the current mechanics before planning around them.

## 5. Off-site traffic tactic menu

Pick tactics that match where the audience already spends time. Test before committing.

| Group | Tactic | Fits when | Guardrail |
|---|---|---|---|
| Speaking and teaching | Speak at conferences, events, and local meet-ups | The audience gathers in person or at sector events | Teach; do not pitch from the stage. Point to one resource on the site. |
| | Host webinars or online workshops | The topic needs demonstration or Q&A | Deliver the promised content before any offer. |
| | Teach a class on a learning platform | The business has a teachable method | Check platform terms on linking and promotion. |
| | Organise a small conference or roundtable | The business already has an audience and partners | Start small; a cancelled event harms trust. |
| Social engagement | Reply helpfully to prospective customers' public posts | The audience asks for help publicly | Help first; no unsolicited pitches. |
| | Use relevant hashtags and link the site in the profile bio | The platform uses hashtags for discovery | One or two relevant tags; see `agency-ops/social-media/references/platform-playbook.md`. |
| | Mention people featured in or relevant to a post | Someone is quoted or credited | Mention only people genuinely involved. |
| | Follow prospective customers | The platform notifies on follows | Follow selectively; mass follow-and-unfollow breaches most platform rules. |
| | Promote posts or the profile with paid placement | Organic reach is proven but small | Set a test budget and a qualified-outcome metric. |
| Local groups | Start a group on a meet-up platform with the site linked in the description | A local community of practice is missing | Run it for members' benefit; the link is secondary. |
| | Sponsor existing groups whose members match the audience | A group already gathers the audience | Agree visible, honest sponsorship credit. |
| | Meet people in person and tell them about the site | Always | Offer a specific resource, not a general plug. |
| Partnerships and affiliates | Partner with organisations that already serve the audience; offer value in exchange for access | A non-competing business shares the audience | Put terms in writing; see `agency-ops/referral-program/references/cross-niche-and-partners.md`. |
| | Recruit affiliates who earn commission on sales | The offer has margin and clear tracking | Require affiliates to disclose; see `agency-ops/referral-program/references/tracking-and-ethics.md`. |
| Sharing mechanics (product-led) | Reward sharing with extra features, content, or discounts | The product is digital or repeat-use | Rewards must not buy reviews or fake endorsements. |
| | Let users show achievements (badges, milestones) | Users reach meaningful progress | Sharing stays opt-in. |
| | Enable user-generated content users want to show friends | Users create something of value | Moderate; obtain rights to reuse. |
| | Make the product better when friends also use it | Collaboration is natural to the product | Never upload contacts without explicit consent. |
| Networking and referrals | Go where the audience goes; ask for warm introductions | Relationships drive buying | Ask specifically, and thank every introducer. |
| | Ask contacts to forward the site to people who would benefit | A resource is genuinely useful to a named group | One clear ask; no chain messages. |
| | Attend events where customers gather; exchange contact details | Sector events exist | Follow up only with consent to contact. |
| Paid | Pay-per-click on search, social, and newsletter sponsorships | Intent is clear and unit economics are known | Cap spend per test; see section 2. |
| | Native content-discovery advertising | Content converts cold readers | Label as sponsored; judge on qualified outcomes. |
| Press and PR | Pitch coverage to large outlets and niche publications | There is a genuine story or achievement | Pitch one specific angle; most outlets list a news-submission route. |
| | Public-relations stunt | A memorable, lawful, brand-fit idea exists | Avoid deception, danger, or disrespect to communities. |
| | Distribute a press release through a wire service | There is real news | A release alone rarely earns coverage; pair with direct pitches. |
| | Write guest articles for publications customers read | The publication accepts contributed pieces | See [content-distribution.md](content-distribution.md) "Guest Posting Strategy". |
| | Answer journalists' source requests (source-request services) | The business has quotable expertise | Answer only within real expertise; meet deadlines. |
| Offline and community | Place flyers, handouts, or cards where the audience goes | A physical location concentrates the audience | Get permission from the venue. |
| | Post in relevant discussion forums with contextual links | The forum permits links | Contribute first; follow forum rules. |
| | Comment on blogs the audience reads | The comment adds something | Comment for the discussion; a link only where the rules allow and it helps. |
| | List a deal on a group-discount site | Margin can absorb the discount and the buyers fit | Model the cost per retained customer before listing. |

## 6. Ethical limits that override any tactic

- Never ask friends, colleagues, or staff to up-vote content on voting communities; coordinated voting breaches their rules and can get the domain banned. Share with people who would genuinely value the post and let votes follow.
- Disclose every paid, sponsored, affiliate, or gifted relationship.
- Do not buy reviews, followers, links, or engagement.
- Respect each platform's promotion and linking rules; they change, so check them before each campaign.

## 7. Build the audience before launch

When a product or service is not yet launched:

1. Start publishing on the topic months before launch.
2. Offer every visitor the option to receive new posts and updates by email.
3. Grow the list steadily; on launch day, the business has an audience that already knows it.
