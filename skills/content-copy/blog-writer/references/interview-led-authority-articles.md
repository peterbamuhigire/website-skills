# Interview-Led Authority Articles

Parent skill: [blog-writer](../SKILL.md). Read when an article will be built from a recorded conversation with the client's own expert (a founder, partner, clinician, engineer) or with one of the client's customers, so that the expert gives an hour and the agency does the rest. Complements [ideation and research](ideation-and-research.md) and the idea-level "interview material" section in `blog-idea-generator` (`references/topic-prompts-segments-events-and-owned-material.md`); this file covers production from consent to publication.

Why this format: busy experts rarely write, but they explain well when asked good questions. A planned interview captures their judgement in their own words, which is the hardest thing for an agency writer to supply and the thing that makes an article credible.

## 1. When to use and when not to

Use when:

- the article's value depends on first-hand expertise or a client's real experience;
- the expert has more knowledge than writing time;
- the topic comes from the buyer-question register and needs an authoritative answer.

Do not use when:

- the expert cannot commit to a fact-check and approval step before publication;
- the subject is regulated advice (medical, legal, financial, investment) and no qualified reviewer is available;
- the interviewee is a customer who has not given written consent to be named, quoted or recorded.

## 2. Workflow

### Step 1. Select the question

Take the topic from the buyer-question register (see [they-ask-you-answer](../../../ux-conversion/they-ask-you-answer/SKILL.md)). Write the article's one-sentence point of view as a hypothesis for the expert to confirm, correct or reject.

### Step 2. Agree consent and scope in writing

Before recording, send a short consent note and keep the signed or replied copy. It states:

- what is recorded (audio, video, screen) and where the file is stored;
- how the material will be used (article, quotations, short clips) and for how long;
- that the interviewee sees the draft and approves every quotation and figure before publication;
- that they may withdraw a statement before publication;
- who else will see the recording (transcription service, editor), and any tool that processes it.

Data protection: a recording of an identifiable person is personal data. In Uganda, collecting personal data requires prior consent, and data collectors must be registered with the Personal Data Protection Office (register PL-01). In Kenya, the Data Protection Act 2019 and its regulations apply (register PL-02). Check that any transcription or AI tool used on the recording is covered by the client's data-processing terms before uploading. For customer interviews, never record health, financial or other sensitive details unless the article needs them and the consent covers them explicitly.

### Step 3. Write the pre-interview brief

Send the expert a one-page brief two days before:

- the reader, the question and the working point of view;
- five to seven questions (see section 3);
- the request to answer each question in a self-contained way, restating the question at the start of the answer, so that each answer stands alone as a quotation or clip;
- a request for one real example per answer, and a note of any figure they will need to check afterwards;
- the time needed: usually 30 to 45 minutes of recording.

Decide the outputs before recording (article, pull quotes, any clips), so questions and answer lengths suit them.

### Step 4. Record

- Quiet room; test recording before starting; the expert's microphone close to the mouth. Poor audio makes a competent expert sound less competent.
- Let the expert talk; ask follow-up questions that request a number, an example, or "what would you tell a client who disagrees?".
- Note timestamps of strong answers as you go.

### Step 5. Transcribe and mark

Produce a transcript (tool plus human correction). Mark every passage that answers a question substantively, every claim of fact, and every figure.

### Step 6. Draft the article

A transcript is raw material, not an article.

1. Build the structure from the reader's question, not from the order of the conversation.
2. Keep the expert's own phrasing for key judgements; paraphrase the rest in the brand's voice.
3. Use direct quotation only for sentences the expert actually said, lightly cleaned of filler. Never merge separate remarks into one quotation.
4. Add what speech skipped: definitions (use the [plain-language explainer recipe](plain-language-explainer-recipe.md)), links, sources and a clear next step.
5. Attribute: name, role, organisation and why they are qualified to answer.

### Step 7. Fact-check

Create a claim register for the draft: each factual claim, its source (the expert, a document, an external source), and its status.

- Figures the expert quoted from memory are checked against their records or a named source.
- External facts (laws, prices, statistics, platform rules) are checked against a primary source and dated. Unverified facts are removed or rewritten as a check the reader should make.
- Regulated statements are reviewed by a qualified person.

### Step 8. Approval

Send the full draft, with quotations highlighted, to the interviewee. Record their approval with the date. For customer interviews, also confirm approval from the client organisation if its name appears.

### Step 9. Publish and hand off

Publish only with the authority set out in the parent skill. Record: interview date, approval date, sources, and a review date for time-sensitive facts. Hand clip and social distribution to the social media engine (see section 6).

### Step 10. Mine the next topics

Questions the expert raised but did not answer, and questions readers ask after publication, go back into the buyer-question register.

## 3. Question bank (original prompts)

Each question asks for one idea, one example and one takeaway.

- "What is the most common mistake [reader type] makes with [topic], and what should they do instead?"
- "If a [client type] in [place] had only [budget or time], where should they start?"
- "Walk me through how you would fix [specific problem] in the first 30 days."
- "What is the one number every [role] should watch for [topic], and why?"
- "What do most people believe about [topic] that is wrong?"
- "Tell me about a client who [situation]. What changed, and what did they do themselves?" (anonymise unless consent covers naming)
- "Who should not do this, or not yet?"

## 4. Answer starters to coach the expert

Share these so each answer can stand alone:

- "The biggest mistake [reader type] makes with [topic] is ..."
- "If you are a [role] in [place] with [constraint], start with ..."
- "Most people think [belief]. In practice ..."
- "The number to watch is [metric], because ..."

## 5. Expert time budget

| Activity | Typical expert time |
|---|---|
| Approve topic and working point of view | 10 minutes |
| Read brief | 10 minutes |
| Record | 30 to 45 minutes |
| Fact-check questions from the writer | 15 minutes |
| Approve draft and quotations | 15 to 20 minutes |

Report expert minutes per published article to the client; it is the measure that keeps the format sustainable.

## 7. Measurement

Do not judge authority articles by last-click conversions alone. Track, alongside page metrics:

- enquiries or discovery calls in which the prospect mentions the article (captured in the CRM "how did you hear" field or call notes);
- invitations, media requests or partner approaches that cite it;
- the cumulative number of published interview-led pieces per topic cluster.

Never promise a lead or ranking result from the format.

## 8. Worked example (illustrative)

A Kampala law firm partner has no time to write. The agency takes the buyer question "What changes for employers under [named new regulation]?" from the firm's enquiry log, sends a consent note and brief, and records a 35-minute conversation. The writer drafts a 1,200-word article structured around the reader's three decisions, quotes the partner on the two judgement calls, and moves statutory detail to a linked summary. A second partner checks the legal statements against the gazetted text and dates the article. The interviewing partner approves six quotations. Total partner time: about 75 minutes.

## 9. Acceptance checklist

- [ ] Written consent covers recording, uses, tools, storage and approval rights.
- [ ] The article answers a question from the buyer-question register.
- [ ] Every quotation was actually said and approved in writing.
- [ ] The claim register shows every factual claim checked, dated or removed.
- [ ] Regulated statements were reviewed by a qualified person.
- [ ] The article adds definitions, sources and a next step that the conversation lacked.
- [ ] Consent record, approval date and review date are stored with the draft.
