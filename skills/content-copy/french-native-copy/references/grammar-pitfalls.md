# French Grammar Pitfalls That Expose Translation

**Purpose**: Catalogue the specific grammar points that machine translation and non-native writers get wrong in French, with the correct form, so copy is grammatically native.

**Read this when**: You are writing or proofreading French copy and need to verify articles, agreement, negation, pronouns, relative pronouns, or verb constructions.

## 1. Partitive articles — the clearest tell

French marks an unspecified quantity of something with the partitive: `du` (masc.), `de la` (fem.), `de l'` (before a vowel/h), `des` (plural). English has no equivalent word, so translation drops or mistranslates it.

- `Nous proposons du conseil et de la formation.` (We offer consulting and training.) — not `le conseil et la formation` in a generic offer sense.
- `Cela demande du temps et de la rigueur.` (That takes time and rigour.)
- `Avoir de la patience, du courage, de la chance.` Abstract qualities take the partitive.

Two collapses that translation misses:

- **After negation, the partitive becomes `de`**: `Pas de frais cachés.` (No hidden fees.) — not `pas des frais`. `Il n'y a pas de risque.`
- **After a quantity word, it becomes `de`**: `beaucoup de clients`, `un peu de patience`, `un kilo de farine`, `plus de choix`, `assez de temps`. Never `beaucoup des clients` (unless "many of the [specific] clients", a different meaning).

## 2. Gender and adjective agreement

Every adjective agrees in gender and number with its noun. Translation routinely leaves the masculine default.

- `une solution complète`, `des services personnalisés`, `une équipe expérimentée`, `des résultats concrets`.
- Position: most descriptive adjectives follow the noun (`un produit fiable`), a short set precedes it (`un grand projet`, `une nouvelle offre`, `un bon rapport qualité-prix`, `une belle expérience`).
- Useful gender signals: `-tion`, `-sion`, `-té`, `-ée`, `-ance`, `-ence` are feminine (`la solution`, `la qualité`, `la performance`); `-ment`, `-age`, `-isme` are masculine (`le paiement`, `l'accompagnement`, `le professionnalisme`). Before a vowel sound, feminine nouns take `mon/ton/son` instead of `ma/ta/sa`: `mon entreprise`, `mon équipe`, `son université`, `ton adresse` — all feminine, all correct. Using `ma équipe` is the error.

## 3. Negation scope and placement

The two-part `ne … pas` brackets the conjugated verb.

- `Nous ne facturons pas de frais de dossier.`
- Variants: `ne … plus` (no longer), `ne … jamais` (never), `ne … rien` (nothing), `ne … personne` (nobody), `ne … que` (only — restrictive, not negative: `Nous ne travaillons qu'avec des matériaux certifiés`).
- `ne … ni … ni …` for "neither/nor": `Ni frais cachés ni engagement.`
- In speech the `ne` drops (`On facture pas`), but written professional copy keeps `ne`. Dropping it in copy reads as transcribed slang.

## 4. Object pronoun placement

Pronouns sit **before** the conjugated verb, not after as in English.

- `Nous vous accompagnons.` (We support you.) — not `Nous accompagnons vous`.
- `Je le recommande.` (I recommend it.)
- Negative: `Nous ne les facturons pas.`
- Affirmative imperative is the exception — pronoun attaches after with a hyphen: `Contactez-nous`, `Faites-le`, `Inscrivez-vous`. Negative imperative reverts to before: `Ne vous inquiétez pas.`
- `y` (replaces `à` + thing/place) and `en` (replaces `de` + thing, or a partitive): `Nous y répondons sous 24 h`, `Profitez-en`.

## 5. Relative pronouns: qui / que / dont / où

- `qui` = subject of its clause: `une équipe qui vous écoute`.
- `que` (`qu'` before a vowel) = direct object: `le service que vous attendez`, `l'offre qu'elle préfère`.
- `dont` = replaces `de` + noun (whose / of which / about which): `un projet dont vous serez fier`, `les outils dont vous avez besoin` (because `avoir besoin de`).
- `où` = place or time: `la région où nous intervenons`, `le jour où tout change`.

Choosing `qui` vs `que` is a subject/object test, not a person/thing test — translation engines confuse the two.

## 6. Verb + preposition collocations

The preposition a verb takes rarely matches English. Getting it wrong is unmistakable to natives.

- `attendre` takes no preposition: `attendre le résultat` (wait **for** the result).
- `chercher` takes none: `chercher une solution` (look **for**).
- `demander` takes none for the thing: `demander un devis` (ask **for** a quote).
- `penser à` (think about/of), `penser de` (have an opinion of), `s'occuper de` (handle), `bénéficier de` (benefit from), `répondre à` (answer), `s'adapter à` (adapt to), `dépendre de` (depend on), `faire confiance à` (trust).
- `téléphoner à quelqu'un`, `aider quelqu'un` (no `à`).

## 7. High-frequency verbs and their idioms

- `avoir` for states English builds with "be": `avoir besoin de`, `avoir envie de`, `avoir raison`, `avoir lieu` (to take place). Age: `J'ai trente ans`, never `Je suis…`.
- `faire` for activities and weather: `faire appel à` (call on/engage), `faire le point` (take stock), `faire confiance`, `il fait beau`.
- `aller` for near future and wellbeing: `Nous allons vous recontacter`, `Ça va`.
- `venir de` + infinitive = "to have just": `Vous venez de vous inscrire`.
- `il faut` (it is necessary) + infinitive or `que` + subjunctive: `Il faut réserver à l'avance`, `Il faut que vous confirmiez`.
- `connaître` (be acquainted with people/places/brands) vs `savoir` (know facts / how to): `Vous connaissez notre marque`; `Vous savez ce que vous voulez`.

## 8. Subjunctive triggers (keep copy correct, not stilted)

Use the subjunctive after `il faut que`, `bien que`, `pour que`, `avant que`, `afin que`, and verbs of wish/doubt (`vouloir que`, `souhaiter que`): `Pour que votre projet aboutisse`, `Bien que ce soit simple`. Do not over-use it; most web copy stays in the indicative.

## 9. C'est vs Il est

- `C'est` + noun/pronoun/adjective standing alone: `C'est simple`, `C'est notre métier`, `C'est gratuit`.
- `Il est` + adjective + `de` + infinitive, or for time/profession without article: `Il est important de réserver`, `Il est conseiller` (no article before an unmodified profession: `Elle est architecte`).
