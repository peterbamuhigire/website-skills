# Service, Hospitality, and Sector Lexicon

Parent skill: [swahili-native-copy](../SKILL.md). Read when writing Kiswahili copy for booking, shopping, directions, signage, hospitality, food and beverage, or agriculture, or when you need the market-calibration and trust notes that sit beside the register rules.

Use this file with [register-and-greetings.md](register-and-greetings.md), [idiom-and-cultural.md](idiom-and-cultural.md), and [numbers-time-dates.md](numbers-time-dates.md). Default to `Kiswahili sanifu`. Tags: (K) more common in Kenya, (T) more common in Tanzania.

## 1. Booking and appointments

| Need | Kiswahili |
|---|---|
| appointment | `miadi` (`Nataka kuweka miadi` — I would like to make an appointment) |
| meeting | `mkutano` |
| reception | `mapokezi` |
| book a table or room | `Naweza kuwekesha meza / chumba?` |
| any rooms available? | `Je, mnavyo vyumba?` |
| breakfast included? | `Hiyo ni pamoja na kifungua kinywa?` |
| check-out time | `Saa ngapi inabidi kutoka chumbani?` |
| you are welcome (reply to thanks) | `Unakaribishwa` |

## 2. Shopping and service counter

| Need | Kiswahili |
|---|---|
| Can I help you? | `Nikusaidie?` |
| I am just looking | `Ninaangalia tu` |
| I am looking for… | `Ninatafuta…` |
| How much is this? | `Hii ni bei gani?` |
| Can I pay by card? | `Naweza kulipa kwa kadi?` |
| I will take it | `Nitaichukua` |
| That is all, thank you | `Ni hiyo tu, asante` |
| It depends | `Inategemea` |

## 3. Directions

`iko wapi?` (where is it?) · `moja kwa moja` (straight ahead) · `kushoto` / `kulia` (left / right) · `karibu (na)` / `mbali` (near / far) · `katikati ya mji` (city centre).

## 4. Signs and interface labels

| English | Kiswahili |
|---|---|
| Open | `WAZI` |
| Closed | `IMEFUNGWA` |
| Sale | `SELI` (loanword sign; for discount wording use `kipunguzo cha bei`) |
| Cash desk | `DESKI LA MALIPO` |
| Download | `Pakua` (verb `kupakua`) |
| Wi-Fi | `wai fai` (casual spelling; `Wi-Fi` is acceptable in interface text) |

Large numbers for prices: `laki` = 100,000 (common in speech, especially Tanzania: `laki mbili` = 200,000). In web copy, show digits and the currency code; see numbers-time-dates.md.

## 5. Opening-hours copy rule

For "9 a.m. to 5 p.m." write `Saa tatu asubuhi hadi saa kumi na moja jioni`, not `saa tisa hadi saa tano`. Subtract six hours and always add the day-part word (`asubuhi`, `mchana`, `alasiri` for afternoon, `jioni`, `usiku`). Where the audience is mixed, show the international time in brackets.

## 6. Hospitality and food lexicon

### Hospitality core

- `Karibu` / `Karibuni` — welcome (plural for respect and groups).
- `sinia` — the shared serving tray; signals communal warmth.
- The "always something ready to share" ethos suits hospitality, food, tourism, and agriculture copy. Use it as a value, not as a cliché.

### Signature dishes

`ugali` (maize staple) · `pilau` (spiced rice; `pilau la sikukuu` — the festive pilau present at every celebration) · `kachumbari` (tomato, onion, and chilli relish) · `chapati` · `nyama choma` (grilled meat; conviviality) · `maandazi` (fried dough) · `vitumbua` (rice pancakes) · `sambusa` / `samosa` · `chai ya viungo` (spiced tea) · `futari` and `uji` (Ramadan evening meal and porridge).

### Ingredient glossary

| Group | Terms |
|---|---|
| Spices | `iliki` cardamom · `karafuu` cloves · `mdalasini` cinnamon · `tangawizi` ginger · `pilipili` chilli · `tui la nazi` coconut milk · `ukwaju` tamarind |
| Staples | `mchele` uncooked rice · `wali` cooked rice · `muhogo` cassava · `ndizi` banana or plantain · `maharage` beans · `mtama` millet or sorghum |
| Fruit | `embe` mango · `nanasi` pineapple · `parachichi` avocado · `nazi` coconut · `papai` papaya |
| Meat and seafood | `nyama ya ng'ombe` beef · `nyama ya mbuzi` goat · `kuku` chicken · `samaki` fish · `pweza` octopus · `kamba` prawns · `dagaa` small dried fish · `sato` tilapia |
| Vegetables | `mchicha` amaranth greens · `kisamvu` cassava leaves · `mboga` vegetables · `nyanya` tomato · `vitunguu` onions |

### Cooking patterns for menus

The pattern agrees with the noun class of the dish:

- `… wa / za kukaanga` — fried (`samaki wa kukaanga`);
- `… za kuchoma` — grilled or roasted (adjust `wa / ya / za` to the dish's noun class);
- `… wa kuchemsha` — boiled;
- `… wa nazi` — in coconut sauce (`samaki wa nazi`);
- `Mchuzi wa …` — stew or curry of …;
- `Supu ya …` / `Mchemsho wa …` — soup or broth of ….

Check each dish name with a native speaker from the target market before print or signage; names vary by region.

## 7. Warmth and flow phrases for story copy

`Ninashukuru sana kwa…` (I am very grateful for…) · `Karibu nyumbani` (welcome home) · `haijalishi jinsi…` (no matter how…) · `kwa upole` / `kwa nguvu` (gently / forcefully). Keep full concord and proper tense markers; these phrases only read as fluent inside correct grammar.

## 8. Market calibration notes

- **Tanzania:** Kiswahili is the language of official life. Write rich, eloquent Kiswahili and keep English to a minimum; English-heavy copy can feel distant.
- **Kenya:** for upmarket and corporate audiences, English often carries more prestige. Polished bilingual or English-led copy with Kiswahili warmth is normal. Very deep, formal Kiswahili can read as classroom language for these audiences.
- **Trust rule:** the language culture values mutual understanding over pedantry. Copy that connects beats copy that is merely correct, but for premium and formal copy, correct concord is itself a signal of respect.
- **Varieties to avoid for standard audiences:** Congo or Katanga varieties (sometimes called Kingwana) and work-camp pidgins. Write `Kiswahili sanifu`.
- **Accents and dialects:** never mock a regional or vernacular accent in copy or scripts; present mutual understanding as the value.
