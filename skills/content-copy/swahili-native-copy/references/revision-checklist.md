# Swahili Copy Revision Checklist

**Purpose**: A final pass that catches the errors that survive drafting. Run it on every piece of Kiswahili copy before sign-off, whether authored fresh or adapted from English.

**Read this when**: The Swahili draft exists and you need to verify it is native-quality before it ships, or you are reviewing someone else's Swahili copy.

## How to use

Work top to bottom. Any failure is a blocking edit. Record the market (Tanzania/Kenya/regional) and voice decision at the top so the next writer inherits it.

## 1. Concord audit (the most important pass — see noun-classes-and-concord.md)

For every noun, identify its class, then verify each dependent word carries that class's marker:

- Adjectives agree and follow the noun: `nyumba nzuri`, `vitabu vizuri`, `mradi mkubwa`. Invariable Arabic adjectives (`bora`, `safi`, `rahisi`, `ghali`, `maalum`) correctly take no prefix.
- Possessives agree with the thing owned and follow the noun: `duka letu`, `bidhaa zetu`, `kitabu changu`. Family terms take N-class possessives: `dada yangu`, not `dada wangu`.
- Demonstratives agree: `huduma hii`, `vitu hivi`, `mradi huu`.
- Agreeing numbers (1–5, 8) agree; others are invariable: `watu watano`, `watu sita`.
- Verb subject (and object) markers agree with the subject's class: `Bidhaa zetu zinapatikana`, `Mradi huu utakamilika`. Non-human subjects never take `wa-`.
- `-a` of association uses the right connector: `huduma ya kampuni`, `duka la nguo`, `viti vya wateja`.

## 2. Verb and mood (see verb-system-and-politeness.md)

- Tense markers correct (`-na- / -li- / -me- / -ta-`); perfect `-me-` for results, past `-li-` for events.
- Negatives correctly formed: `hatutoi` (we don't provide), `hatutachelewa` (we won't be late), `hawajalipa` (not yet paid) — negative prefixes `si-/hu-/ha-/hatu-/ham-/hawa-`.
- CTA mood fits the tone: subjunctive `-e` with `tafadhali` for soft asks (`Tafadhali ujiunge nasi`), imperative for direct ones (`Anza sasa`), negative subjunctive for "don't" (`Usikose ofa hii`).
- `wasiliana` takes `na`: `Wasiliana nasi`, never `Wasiliana sisi`.

## 3. Register and greetings (see register-and-greetings.md)

- Copy is `Kiswahili sanifu`; no `Sheng` or heavy dialect unless the brief demands it.
- Greetings fit the register and audience; `Karibu`/`Karibuni` used correctly for sg/pl.
- Reader addressed consistently (singular-personal or plural-respectful); honorifics appropriate; brand speaks as `tu-`/`sisi`.

## 4. Idiom and flow (see idiom-and-cultural.md)

- Native collocations (`kutoa huduma`, `kukidhi mahitaji`, `huduma bora`) rather than literal pairings.
- Trust language present where the page needs it (`bila gharama za siri`, `unaweza kutuamini`).
- At most one `methali`, used where it genuinely fits.
- Warmth before the sell; community/relationship framing where relevant.

## 5. Loanwords and anglicisms (see loanwords-and-anglicisms.md)

- Modern terms in their Swahili forms: `barua pepe`, `tovuti`, `mtandaoni`, `simu ya mkononi`, `jisajili`, `oda`, `punguzo`. No bare `email`, `online`, `website`, `checkout`.
- No calques from the table (`Karibu kwa tovuti`, `Wasiliana sisi`, `Bonyeza hapa` as link text, `Jifunze zaidi` where `Pata maelezo zaidi` is better).
- Non-human nouns not given human (`wa-`) agreement.

## 6. Orthography (see loanwords-and-anglicisms.md)

- `ng'` keeps its apostrophe where the sound requires it.
- Digraphs (`ny`, `ch`, `dh`, `gh`, `th`, `sh`) spelt standard, not anglicised.
- Double vowels written where the word has them; phonetic standard spelling throughout.

## 7. Numbers, time, dates, currency (see numbers-time-dates.md)

- Currency formatted for the market (`TSh`/`KSh`, comma thousands, full-stop decimals).
- The Swahili clock converted correctly (Western hour − 6); times shown unambiguously, ideally with international time in brackets. No silent six-hour errors.
- Days, months, and `tarehe`-format dates correct; relative-time words native.

## 8. Back-translation test

Translate the finished Swahili back into English in your head. The result must reproduce the intended meaning with no distortion, no missing fact, and no register change. If it drifts from the brief, the Swahili is wrong even if it reads smoothly.

## 9. Read-aloud test

Read the Swahili aloud. A native ear catches broken concord, a wrong preposition, or a calque immediately. Rewrite from meaning, not by patching words.

## 10. SEO and slug check (with i18n and seo)

- Target phrase is what an East African actually searches, researched in Kiswahili — not a transposed English phrase.
- Slug localized per `i18n` rules; `hreflang` and canonical resolve to the matching Kiswahili page.
- Metadata title and description are native Kiswahili within length limits.

## Acceptance criteria

The copy ships only when sections 1–10 pass. A native Kiswahili reader from the target market should read the page and find nothing that betrays it was produced for a multilingual build rather than written in Kiswahili from the start.
