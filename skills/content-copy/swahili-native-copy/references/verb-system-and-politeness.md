# Swahili Verb System and Politeness

**Purpose**: Build Swahili verbs correctly and choose the mood that carries the right tone — soft requests, direct calls to action, and clean negatives — for commercial copy.

**Read this when**: You are writing CTAs, instructions, offers, or any sentence with a verb, and need correct tense, negation, imperative, or subjunctive.

## Verb shape

A Swahili verb glues its parts into one word:

`subject prefix + tense marker + (object infix) + root + final vowel`

- `Tu-na-ku-saidi-a` = `tunakusaidia` (we are helping you): `tu-` (we) + `-na-` (present) + `-ku-` (you, object) + `saidi` (help) + `-a`.
- `A-ta-fik-a` = `atafika` (he/she will arrive).

### Subject prefixes (persons)

`ni-` (I), `u-` (you sg), `a-` (he/she), `tu-` (we), `m-` (you pl / polite you), `wa-` (they). For non-human subjects, use the noun-class subject concord from `references/noun-classes-and-concord.md`.

### Tense markers

- `-na-` present/progressive: `tunatoa huduma bora` (we provide excellent service).
- `-li-` past: `tulianzisha kampuni` (we founded the company).
- `-me-` perfect (done, result stands): `tumekamilisha mradi` (we have completed the project).
- `-ta-` future: `tutawasiliana nawe` (we will contact you).
- `-hu-` habitual (no subject prefix): `hufunguliwa saa mbili` (it is habitually opened at 8 a.m.).
- `-ki-` conditional/"if/when": `ukinunua leo` (if you buy today).

## Negation

Negation changes the subject prefix and often the tense and final vowel — it is not a separate word.

- Negative subject prefixes: `si-` (I), `hu-` (you sg), `ha-` (he/she), `hatu-` (we), `ham-` (you pl), `hawa-` (they).
- Present negative drops `-na-` and ends in `-i`: `hatutoi` (we do not provide), `sielewi` (I do not understand).
- Past negative uses `-ku-`: `hatukupokea` (we did not receive).
- Future negative keeps `-ta-`: `hatutachelewa` (we will not be late).
- Perfect negative uses `-ja-`: `hawajalipa` (they have not yet paid).

Commercial example: `Hakuna gharama za ziada.` (There are no extra costs.) — `hakuna` = "there is/are no".

## Imperative — direct commands

Single addressee: use the verb root + `-a` (Bantu verbs). Plural/polite: add `-eni` (with vowel harmony `-eni`/`-ini`).

- `Nunua sasa` (buy now) → `Nunueni sasa`.
- `Tembelea duka letu` (visit our shop) → `Tembeleeni duka letu`.
- `Wasiliana nasi` (contact us) → `Wasilianeni nasi`.
- `Karibu` / `Karibuni` (welcome — interjection, used as a warm imperative-style invite).
- Irregular: `njoo` / `njooni` (come), `nenda` / `nendeni` (go), `lete` / `leteni` (bring).

The bare imperative is direct. For consumer copy it can feel abrupt at scale; soften with `tafadhali` or switch to the subjunctive.

## Subjunctive — the polite request mood (-e ending)

Replace the final `-a` with `-e` and keep the subject prefix. This is the workhorse for courteous CTAs.

- `Tafadhali wasiliana nasi` (direct) vs `Tafadhali uwasiliane nasi` (subjunctive, softer "please do contact us").
- `Ujiunge nasi leo` (do join us today), `tushirikiane` (let us work together), `upate ofa hii` (so that you get this offer).
- "Let us / let's": first person plural subjunctive `tu- … -e`: `Tuanze` (let's begin), `Tuongee` (let's talk).
- Purpose "so that": subjunctive after the clause: `Jaza fomu ili tupate maelezo yako.` (Fill the form so we get your details.)

Negative subjunctive inserts `-si-`: `Usisahau` (don't forget, sg), `Msisahau` (pl), `Usikose ofa hii` (don't miss this offer).

## Politeness toolkit

- `tafadhali` (please) — front it: `Tafadhali jaza fomu hapa chini.`
- `naomba` (I request / may I) — humble and very common in service Swahili: `Naomba msaada`, `Naomba uthibitishe oda yako` (please confirm your order).
- `karibu` / `karibu sana` / `karibuni` (welcome / very welcome / welcome, plural) — the core hospitality word; opens pages and replies.
- `asante` / `asante sana` / `asanteni` (thank you / very much / plural) — use freely.
- `samahani` (excuse me / sorry) for interruptions and mild apology; `naomba radhi` / `tunaomba radhi` for a fuller apology in service recovery.
- `hodi` (may I come in?) → answered `karibu` — a culturally warm framing for "enter / get started".

## Useful verb-derived forms for copy

Swahili extends verb roots with suffixes that change meaning — use the right one:

- Applicative `-ia/-ea` (do for/to): `tunakuandalia` (we prepare for you), `tutakuletea` (we will bring to you).
- Causative `-isha/-esha` (cause to): `tunakuwezesha` (we enable you), `kurahisisha` (to make easy).
- Passive `-wa` (be done): `bidhaa zinapatikana` (products are available/obtainable), `huduma inatolewa` (service is provided).
- Reciprocal `-ana` (each other): `tushirikiane` (let's collaborate), `wasiliana` (be in contact).
- Stative `-ika/-eka` (be in a state / -able): `inapatikana` (is available), `inawezekana` (is possible).

These let you say things the native way: `Tunakurahisishia maisha.` (We make life easier for you.) carries applicative + causative naturally, where a calque would be clumsy.
