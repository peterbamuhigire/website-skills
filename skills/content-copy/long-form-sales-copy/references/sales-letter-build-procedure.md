# Sales Letter Build Procedure

Parent skill: [long-form-sales-copy](../SKILL.md). Read when writing, rewriting or auditing a long sales letter, sales page, sales email, VSL script or multi-message sales sequence, and you need the full research, draft, harden, launch discipline.

This is the engine's own working procedure, synthesised from the long-form references and general direct-response practice. It has six workstreams. Complete research before drafting; most weak letters fail in research, not in sentences.

| Workstream | Output |
|---|---|
| A. Research the reader | Reader brief, reading-moment note, priority comparison |
| B. Research the offer | Feature-benefit cards, hidden-benefit hypothesis, flaw list |
| C. Design delivery and first screen | Channel plan, opening promise, headline options |
| D. Build the argument | Complete draft with objections, price framing, action and P.S. |
| E. Harden | Release candidate |
| F. Launch and follow up | Live asset, sequence plan |

## A. Research the Reader

Answer in writing, from evidence (interviews, sales notes, reviews, support threads, community groups):

- **Mind share:** what occupies the reader before work and before sleep?
- **Pain and worry:** what annoys or frightens them about this problem?
- **Stated hope and private hope:** what do they say they want, and what do they want but rarely admit?
- **Change:** which trends are reshaping their work or household now?
- **Language:** what phrases do they use at work, at home and among friends? Record exact words.
- **Decision style:** analytical, status-sensitive, risk-averse, price-anchored, relationship-led?
- **Competing voices:** who else sells to them, and how?
- **Failed attempts:** who has tried to solve this before, and why did it fail?

**Reading-moment note.** Describe where and how the reader meets the message: device, place, time of day, interruptions. Write to that moment.

**Outsider research.** If the writer is not in the audience, read their trade media, watch their events, study their forums and reviews, and listen to unguarded talk.

**Priority comparison.** List sender priorities and reader priorities side by side; cut any section that serves only the sender. For institutional or donor asks, answer three points: the benefit that justifies the request, who else has committed, and where the money or time comes from.

## B. Research the Offer

1. Use, test or watch the product or service in operation.
2. Make one card per feature. On each, write the benefit, then the benefit of that benefit ("so that" chain), stopping when the reader's real motive appears.
3. Rank the cards by importance to the buyer, not to the maker.
4. Look for the **hidden benefit**: a non-obvious gain that matters deeply but is absent from specifications (time freedom, standing among peers, avoiding embarrassment, a better start for children, being taken seriously). It surfaces in informal conversation more than in formal feedback. Validate against customer evidence before headlining it.
5. Build the **flaw list**: every obvious weakness a sceptic will notice (price, age, limited fit, speed, appearance).

Principle: people buy what a thing does for them, not what it is.

### Damaging admission
Concede the most damaging flaw early and plainly, then show why it does not matter or why it explains the value. Slots:
- "We are not the cheapest option for [task]. Here is what the difference pays for."
- "This is not for everyone. It suits [buyer] who [condition]."
- "[Visible flaw]. Even so, [specific verified result]."
An unaddressed flaw becomes a silent doubt that stops the sale.

## C. Delivery and First Screen

### Choose the channel
Pick the route that reaches the decision-maker: a named email from a real person; hand-delivered mail for high-value B2B targets; a WhatsApp broadcast to an opted-in list; a landing page for paid traffic. Confirm consent, deliverability and list permission first.

### Win the first glance
The first surface decides whether the message is opened: envelope teaser, email subject and preheader, ad headline, link preview. Use curiosity or a specific promise, never a false pretext. Place a short framed promise block at the top (in email, the preheader does this job).

### Win the read
- The headline must stop the right reader: specifics, a question, a promise of gain or relief.
- Open with a story, a startling statement, a question or a striking fact. Do not open with "I am writing to ..." or company history.
- Use subheads, short paragraphs, white space and lists.
- **Scanner test:** a reader who sees only headline, subheads, emphasised lines, captions and the P.S. must grasp the offer and know how to act.

## D. Build the Argument

### Price
Apply the copy-level price tactics in [offer-propositions-and-price-framing.md](offer-propositions-and-price-framing.md): comparison shift, cost per day or use, honest value presentation, payment options, conditional incentives, return-on-fee framing, damaging admission about price.

### Choose devices deliberately, do not stack them all

| Device | Use |
|---|---|
| Attention, Interest, Desire, Conviction, Action | Add the conviction stage (proof, guarantee) for higher-stakes offers |
| Problem, agitation, solution | Name the pain, show its real consequence, offer relief; keep agitation factual |
| Before and after | Contrast the current state with the verified after-state |
| "So that" chains | Feature, so that benefit, so that deeper benefit |
| Testimonials | Specific, named, permissioned, with a measurable result and timeframe |
| Bridges | Short lines that pull the reader on ("Here is why that matters.") used sparingly |
| Specificity | An exact verified figure beats a rounded claim; never invent one |
| Reader-centred voice | Count "you" against "we" and "I"; the reader's words should dominate |

### Draft in three passes
1. **Fast draft:** fill every element of the skeleton (see Section G), even badly, without editing.
2. **Strategy pass:** is the right thing said, in the right order, to the right reader? Move, cut or add sections; recheck the priority comparison.
3. **Style pass:** voice, rhythm, sentence variety, read-aloud fluency.

### Objections
List every reason the reader will not act, one per card. Answer each where the doubt arises, using a story, a specific proof or a policy rather than a generic rebuttal.

### Prompting action
A letter that permits "I will think about it" loses most response. Use only triggers that are true and enforced:
- A dated deadline (never "limited time").
- A verified quantity or capacity limit.
- A bonus for responding by a stated date.
- A stated consequence of missing the date (price change, cohort closes).
- The strongest guarantee the business will honour.
- Several response routes (form, call, WhatsApp, email) so the reader picks the easiest.

### The P.S.
Readers often read the P.S. early. Give each one job: restate the offer in a sentence; remind of a response bonus; restate the deadline; add one strong testimonial; answer the biggest objection; or add a relaxed personal "by the way". Use several only if each has a distinct job. On web pages, test a boxed highlights panel with a CTA as the ending.

## E. Harden the Draft

Run in this order:
1. **Checklist:** the skeleton audit below, plus the parent skill's scanner, objection, proof and compliance reviews.
2. **Emphasis:** bold, underlines, callouts and captions only where they carry the scanner story; recheck after later edits.
3. **Conviction and clarity:** remove hedges without legal purpose and vague words; add commitment.
4. **Comparison:** set the draft beside proven examples for the same offer type and audience awareness; note what they do that yours does not.
5. **Pretest:** read aloud; ask an audience proxy to read it; test on a small segment where possible.
6. **Bring it to life:** add one real story or voice detail only this business could supply.
7. **Cut:** delete every sentence that does not move the reader forward.
8. **Mock-up:** view the real artefact on the real channel and device.
9. **Cool-off:** leave it at least a day, then review cold.
10. **Second opinions:** ask trusted readers what they would do next and what they doubted.
11. **Final review:** rerun the checklists and confirm every claim against the claim register.

## F. Launch and Follow Up

Confirm production quality on real devices and clients. One message rarely does the whole job; plan at least three.

| Message | Job |
|---|---|
| 1 | Full argument, offer and real deadline |
| 2 | "Did you see this?" Address the commonest objection; restate the offer |
| 3 | Final notice before a genuine deadline; strongest honest incentive; clear action |

Sequences work across post, email, WhatsApp, SMS and video. Respect consent, frequency limits and opt-outs. Treat any claimed uplift from sequencing as a hypothesis to measure.

### Channel translations

| Channel | Mapping |
|---|---|
| Email | Subject as teaser; preheader as promise block; first two sentences as hook |
| Long sales page | Same skeleton, paced for scrolling, CTAs at genuine decision points |
| VSL | Hook, story, problem, solution, offer, value presentation, real urgency, CTA, closing reminder |
| Webinar pitch | Extended version using the webinar structure in [funnel-scripts-and-sequences.md](funnel-scripts-and-sequences.md) |
| WhatsApp broadcast | Opening text as teaser; first message or voice note as promise; story message as hook; offer message; payment details and deadline as CTA; opt-in lists only |

## G. Skeleton Audit

Check any long-form asset against this order. Each missing element must be a deliberate choice.

```
HEADLINE                      stops the right reader
OPENING PROMISE / HOOK        promises something specific
STORY / PROBLEM / AGITATION   shows you understand the reader's situation
DAMAGING ADMISSION            pre-empts scepticism
PROMISE + BIG IDEA            what they get and why it works
PROOF                         testimonials, data, demonstrations (verified)
OFFER + VALUE PRESENTATION    inclusions, bonuses, terms (no invented values)
PRICE FRAMING                 comparison, cost per use, payment options
GUARANTEE                     risk reversal the business will honour
DEADLINE / LIMIT              only if real and enforced
CALL TO ACTION                every supported response route
SIGNATURE                     real person, real name
P.S. LINES                    restate, bonus, deadline, objection
```

## H. Regional Application Notes (East Africa)

Hypotheses to test, not facts to state:
- Long-form copy can suit considered purchases (land, school fees, insurance, equipment) when it answers real concerns.
- Hand-delivered, named mail to a specific B2B decision-maker may beat email for high-value offers.
- Relationship-based trust favours a short sequence over a single broadcast.
- Hidden benefits often sit in standing among peers, avoiding shame, a better start for children or being seen as serious.

## I. Guardrails

- No fabricated deadlines, quantities, testimonials, values or results.
- No agitation beyond the evidence or aimed at vulnerability.
- Regulated sectors (health, finance, legal, education) need compliance review before release.
- Remove any element the business cannot honour operationally.
