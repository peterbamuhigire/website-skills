# Offer Propositions And Price Framing

Parent skill: [long-form-sales-copy](../SKILL.md). Read when building the offer section of a sales page, letter, VSL, pricing page, or proposal, and when the copy must answer "why you, why this price, why now, why is it safe".

For business-level pricing decisions (setting the price, discount policy, staff enforcement), use `agency-ops/authority-offers/references/price-strategy-and-discount-policy.md`.

## 1. The five-proposition stack

A buyer's reaction to price depends on the case built around it. This stack is the engine's own synthesis: five questions the buyer silently asks. Build an answer to each, lead with the strongest for this market, and place the others behind it.

| # | Proposition | Question the copy must answer | Levers |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Distinct positioning | Why choose you over every other option, including doing nothing? | Positioning, category-of-one framing, benefit-forward claims |
| 2 | Value case | Why is this worth far more than the price? | Bundled value, return-on-fee proof, intangible gains |
| 3 | Offer strength | What would be hard to refuse? | Real incentives, response bonuses, genuine deadlines |
| 4 | Safety case | Why is it safe to try? | Guarantees, warranties, stronger-than-standard risk reversal, peer proof |
| 5 | Experience case | What will buying and using this feel like, before, during, and after? | Onboarding ritual, service moments, events, surprise-and-delight |

**Price-resilience test.** The copy should answer: "Why should I choose you regardless of price, stop worrying about price, and not shop around on price?" If it cannot, fix the propositions before defending the price.

### Worksheet

```
Positioning:    [buyer] choose us because [distinct mechanism or position], unlike [alternative].
Value:          For [price], the buyer gains [quantified or described outcome] over [period].
Offer:          [core deliverable] + [real inclusion] + [response incentive, if any] by [real date].
Safety:         If [defined failure], we will [remedy] within [time]. Proof: [peer evidence].
Experience:     Before: [ritual]. During: [service moment]. After: [follow-up or celebration].
Lead with:      [proposition] because [market reason].
```

## 2. Escape apples-to-apples comparison

Never let the page invite a like-for-like price comparison with a cheaper option.

- **Bundle:** combine elements so no competitor sells the same package.
- **Change one visible thing:** delivery method, packaging, guarantee, speed, access, format.
- **Name the method:** a proprietary process, named system, or signature programme cannot be compared line by line.
- **Compare to a different reference:** compare the fee with the cost of the problem, the cost of the alternative route, or the value of the result, not with a rival's fee.

## 3. Copy-level price tactics

| Tactic | How to write it | Guardrail |
|---|---|---|
| Comparison shift | Set the price against the cost of the problem or a costlier alternative route. | Reference prices must be real and current. |
| Cost per day or use | Divide the price by the period or uses; compare with a familiar small expense. | Use the true total cost including fees. |
| Honest value presentation | List inclusions by function and outcome; show total only from real list prices. | Never assign invented values to bonuses. |
| Payment options | Offer instalments where cash flow, not value, is the barrier; show the total. | State total cost, fees, and consequences of missed payments. |
| Conditional incentives | Reward early commitment, referrals, multi-unit or multi-period purchase. | The condition must be real and applied consistently. |
| Return-on-fee framing | Express the fee as buying a larger return: fee versus measured or client-stipulated gain. | Use documented results or buyer-stipulated value only. |
| Price admission | "Yes, this costs more than [alternative]. Here is what the difference pays for." | Follow with specific proof. |
| Premium option | Show a higher tier; its presence frames the main offer and serves buyers who want more. | The premium tier must be real and deliverable. |

## 4. Price as a quality signal

- A price that is too low for the category can trigger doubt about quality or legitimacy.
- Being deliberately more expensive, with reasons, can be a positioning choice. State the reasons.
- Test higher prices before assuming a lower price wins; also test lower prices before assuming the opposite. Let measured revenue per visitor decide.

## 5. Niche framing raises perceived value

Presenting the same offer specifically for a defined niche (industry, profession, subculture) raises its perceived relevance and the price buyers accept. Rule: a niche version is legitimate when it genuinely fits that audience (examples, language, context, use cases). Do not rename a generic product without adapting it.

## 6. Association

Price is judged by the company it keeps. Where true and permissioned, place alongside the offer:

- Recognisable client names or logos.
- Media appearances and awards.
- Institutional partners, venues, or affiliations.
- Endorsements from known figures.

Rule: every association must be verifiable and approved by the named party.

## 7. Shape expectations before the price appears

Buyers form a view of the acceptable price before they see it. Shape it upstream through the quality of the page, the channels and media where the offer appears, the proof and associations shown, the proposal or packaging, and the demeanour of staff and messages.

## 8. Competing with free

When the alternative is free (public service, free tool, do-it-yourself):

1. Make the hidden cost of free explicit: time lost, delays, red tape, loss of control, uncertain quality, privacy exposure.
2. Speak to buyers who value results and certainty; accept that price-first buyers will leave.
3. Give away useful decision-making information; charge for delivery, expertise, and results.
4. Never give away a premium offer for free. Add value with guarantees, inclusions, service, or access instead.

## 9. Preeminence signals in copy

Buyers resist price least when the provider is clearly the pre-eminent choice. Show three sides where evidence exists:

1. **Expertise and service:** advanced methods, trained team, proprietary process.
2. **Visibility:** publications, talks, media, events.
3. **Community contribution:** charitable, civic, or professional leadership.

## 10. Offer section checklist

- [ ] All five propositions drafted; the lead proposition chosen with a reason.
- [ ] The page offers no like-for-like comparison with a cheaper rival.
- [ ] Every price, instalment, fee, and total is accurate and visible before commitment.
- [ ] No invented component values, reference prices, or scarcity.
- [ ] Guarantee terms match the policy the business will honour.
- [ ] Associations and endorsements are verified and permissioned.
- [ ] Price-sensitive regulated offers (credit, health, education) have compliance review.
