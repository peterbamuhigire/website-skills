# Funnel Scripts And Sequences

Parent skill: [long-form-sales-copy](../SKILL.md). Read when planning a sales funnel, or writing a long-form VSL or sales-letter script, a webinar close, a post-purchase offer, a two-call phone close, or an opt-in email sequence.

These are structures, not scripts to paste. Fill every beat with verified facts about a real offer. Remove any beat that would need invented drama, invented values, or pressure the business cannot justify. For senior B2B buyers, use the register rules in `agency-ops/agency-positioning/references/expert-authority-framework.md`.

## 1. Plan the Funnel

### Four foundation questions
1. Who exactly is the ideal customer (sector, size, situation)?
2. Where do they already gather (lists, groups, forums, events, associations)?
3. What entry offer will attract them (guide, checklist, quiz, diagnostic, short book, consultation)?
4. Where is the relationship heading (the top rung of the offer ladder)?

### Work backwards from revenue
1. State the monthly revenue needed from this funnel.
2. Choose the model: recurring (price times members), back-end sales, or a recurring base topped up by back-end sales.
3. Compute the entry-offer sales needed, using the business's own measured take-up for the next step.
4. Compute the traffic needed, using the business's own measured conversion.
5. If the traffic is unrealistic for the budget or market, change the model or price, not the estimate.

Mark borrowed conversion rates as assumptions until measured.

### Offer ladder
Free entry offer, then low-price first purchase, mid-price offer, high-value offer, recurring offer. Each rung must serve the buyer better, not only raise revenue. The same knowledge can be packaged as self-serve, guided, done-with-you or done-for-you.

### Traffic ownership

| Type | Examples | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Paid | Search and social ads, sponsorships, affiliates | Rented |
| Earned | Search rankings, algorithmic reach, press | Borrowed |
| Owned | Email list, opted-in WhatsApp or SMS list, customers | Owned |

Every campaign should convert rented and borrowed attention into consented, owned contacts. Faster routes: partnerships with list owners, affiliate programmes, paid placements with established lists.

### Lead journey stages
Track each stage and measure it:
1. Temperature: hot (own list), warm (referred by a trusted partner), cold (strangers).
2. Bridge: the page, email, video or article that prepares the lead.
3. Subscriber: consented contact details gained.
4. Buyer: first purchase or firm commitment, however small.
5. Active buyer: buying at higher levels now.
6. Ascension: moving up the ladder over time.
7. Channel shift: moving to a richer channel (email, call, event, one-to-one).

Match the temperature: hot traffic takes direct offers and stronger claims; warm needs a bridge or endorsement first; cold needs an education step first. Test page headers per source, since what helps warm traffic can deter cold.

### Small-batch test
Before scaling, send a fixed small batch per source and read the conversion at each step. The weakest step limits the whole funnel; fix it before buying more traffic. Small batches are directional only; confirm on larger samples.

### Funnel types and parts
Pick one type rather than inventing: lead capture; free-plus-shipping book; live webinar; automated webinar; launch sequence (see `agency-ops/launch-campaigns`); physical product; pay-after-value; application funnel for high-value offers; membership or recurring.

Parts to assemble: bridge pages, opt-in pages, exit offers, post-purchase offers, upsell and downsell pages, order-form add-ons, thank-you pages, member areas, application pages, webinar registration and replay pages, sales letters, VSLs, surveys, email sequences, call scripts.

Study what already works in the market (public ad libraries, competitor funnels), then test one variable at a time against a control.

## 2. The Persona That Carries the Message

Define the person the audience will follow:
- Backstory: who they were before the change.
- Teaching stories: short, true parables.
- Admitted flaws: one or two real weaknesses, stated factually.
- Stances: what the persona publicly supports and refuses.

| Posture | Stance |
|---|---|
| Guide | "I have done this; here is the path." |
| Explorer | "Join me as I pursue this." |
| Reporter | "I asked the experts so you need not." |
| Reluctant teacher | "I did not plan to teach this, but it needs sharing." |

Story arcs: loss and recovery; us against a harmful practice; before and after; discovery; a lawful insider insight; a third-party success story. All must be true and permissioned.

## 3. Copy Mechanics for Every Script

- Match awareness: unaware and problem-aware readers need story and problem first; product-aware readers need offer, terms and guarantee.
- Name the silent question the reader is asking ("Why has this not worked for me?").
- One central promise, with the mechanism named.
- Short sentences, clear transitions, one idea per paragraph.
- Agreement checks ("Does that make sense?") after key points, used as comprehension checks; never stacked to manufacture consent.
- Desire comes from verified case studies, worked value examples and future pacing grounded in real outcomes.

## 4. Long-Form Story-Led Script (VSL, Long Page, Webinar Pitch)

Five movements. Include only the beats you can fill truthfully.

**A. Hook.** An opening line that stops the scroll; the desire question; why the goal has not yet been reached; the central promise; the presenter as someone who has stood where the reader stands.

**B. Story.** A true vivid moment; the obstacle met; naming the problem; the turning-point decision; what was tried first and what began to work; the hidden reason earlier attempts failed; relief from blame where genuinely deserved; the enemy as a practice, system or belief, never a person or group; growth after applying the insight; verified, permissioned results for others; unexpected extra benefits.

**C. Solution.** Introduce the offer; the effort behind it (if true); how it makes life easier and faster; "so you can" outcomes; proof; a plain statement of exactly what the buyer receives; inclusions and bonuses described by function, not invented value.

**D. Price and risk.** Compare with real reference prices only; test the emotional value ("If this achieved only [outcome], would it be worth it?"); state the real price and terms; state the guarantee the business honours; add a deadline or scarcity only if real and enforced.

**E. Close.** Future-pace the verified after-state; one clear call to action; say what happens after acting; state that it is for the right buyer only; restate offer and action.

## 5. Post-Purchase Offer

Shown straight after purchase, before the thank-you page. Sequence:
1. Confirm the decision and what they will receive.
2. Introduce a faster or larger path to the result and say why the first choice was sound.
3. State who the offer is for and who it is not for.
4. Describe the result, the speed gain and the mechanism; future-pace it.
5. Primary action and an equally visible decline link.
6. Guarantee, any real extra inclusion, and testimonials.
7. State exclusivity only if truly one-time.
8. Repeat the action.

Rule: the decline option must be obvious; never pre-tick, disguise or shame a refusal.

## 6. Webinar Structure and Closes

**Promise:** "How to [result] without [main obstacle]."

**Teaching body:** three points, each shifting one belief in turn:
1. The method works (belief in the vehicle).
2. You can do it (belief in yourself).
3. Your circumstances allow it (belief in your situation).

For each, share the insight and reframe the old belief with a true story, history or evidence.

**Bridge to the offer:** acknowledge the session cannot cover everything and ask whether attendees want help implementing.

**Close toolkit (choose relevant items, never all):**

| Close | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Recap | List everything included, then price and terms |
| Value check | "If this achieved only [outcome], would it be worth it?" |
| Money as exchange | Spending is trading money for a result |
| Time against money | Money can be earned again; time cannot |
| Knowing against doing | Support closes the implementation gap |
| Two paths | Carry on as now, or try with the guarantee |
| Sign-up walkthrough | Show exactly how to buy |
| Now and later | Contrast today with the reachable future |
| Hesitations | Name and answer the two or three commonest respectfully |
| Method credit | "The method did this, not special talent" |
| Final action | One clear step |

Exclude closes that shame, pressure or imply weakness in declining. For senior B2B audiences use one call to action and no stacked closes.

## 7. Two-Call Phone Close (Application Funnels)

**Call 1, qualifying:** introduce yourself; ask open questions about the situation; say you may be able to help; explain why the programme is selective; understand personal context; confirm affordability without pressure; clarify goals; secure four commitments: time, willingness to be taught, investment, decision authority.

**Call 2, enrolling:** reconfirm those four; explain what they receive; complete enrolment and send written terms.

Rule: if affordability or decision authority fails, stop, refer or decline.

## 8. Email Sequences After Opt-In

### Five-part welcome sequence

| Part | Job | Content |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Set the scene | Welcome, introduce the persona, set expectations, deliver the promised asset |
| 2 | Backstory | Tell the story to the obstacle; hint at the resolution |
| 3 | Insight | The turning point and how it connects to the offer |
| 4 | Hidden benefits | Unexpected gains customers report (verified) |
| 5 | Call to action | Clear offer; a real deadline only if one exists |

For premium B2B audiences, spread five emails over about ten days and drop cliffhangers.

### Ongoing story-led emails
Regular emails, each tied to an offer, in three styles: an incident with a lesson and a link; a realisation or teaching moment with a link; a direct how-to with a link. Match cadence to the audience: frequent can suit low-price consumer lists; weekly suits senior buyers.

## 9. Regional Application Notes (East Africa)

Hypotheses to test:
- The offer ladder with recurring revenue suits subscriptions, SACCOs, tuition, fitness, training and professional services.
- Opted-in WhatsApp lists often act as the primary owned audience.
- The welcome sequence can run as WhatsApp broadcasts or email onboarding.
- Paid traffic can be costly relative to order values in UGX or KES; run the small-batch test first.
