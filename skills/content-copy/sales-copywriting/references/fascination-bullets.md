# Fascination Bullets

Parent skill: [sales-copywriting](../SKILL.md). Read when writing benefit lists, lead-magnet promises, page teasers or feature lists.

Readers scan bullets even when they skip body copy. Each bullet has one job: create curiosity, state a benefit clearly, or prove expertise.

## 1. Choose the Bullet Mode

| Mode | What it does | Use on |
|---|---|---|
| Blind | Names the topic but withholds the answer, so the reader must act to learn it | Opt-in pages, sales pages |
| Naked | States the benefit or fact plainly | Service pages, feature lists, blog posts |

Rule: blend both. Naked bullets earn trust by giving real value; blind bullets create pull. Use blind bullets only when you can deliver the withheld answer in the product.

## 2. Mechanisms by What They Do

Each mechanism has a slot template and an original East African example.

### Create curiosity (blind)
- **Contradiction:** "[Common belief]? [Correction], and here is why." Example: "More stock on the shelf means more profit? Not in a Nakasero pharmacy with short-dated drugs."
- **Hidden reason:** "Why [surprising action or timing]." Example: "Why paying suppliers on Tuesday, not Friday, protects your cash."
- **Parenthetical hook:** "[Plain claim]. ([Teaser that sharpens the stakes].)" Example: "The one report to run every Monday. (Skip it and you will only notice the leak at month-end.)"
- **Question the reader cannot answer:** "Can you say right now [specific figure]? (Most owners cannot.)" Example: "Can you say, right now, how much float your agent should hold?"
- **Insider suspicion:** "The real reason [party] [behaviour]." Example: "The real reason wholesalers push large minimum orders."
- **Named method, unnamed detail:** "The [method] used by [type of business] and why it suits [small case]." Example: "The rotation habit busy Kisumu grocers use, and why a one-room kiosk can copy it." Use sparingly; overuse weakens it.

### Give value (naked)
- **Sample tip:** state a real, usable tip. Example: "Count the till at 3 pm, not at closing: differences are easier to trace while the shift is fresh." Good tips imply that the paid material is stronger.
- **Direct outcome:** "[Action] in [time], even when [difficult condition]." Example: "Serve a customer in under a minute on a market day."
- **Plain fact:** state the scenario when no mystery fits. Example: "If the network drops, sales keep recording and sync when you reconnect."
- **Topic label:** "What [reader should check or do]." Example: "What to check every morning before opening the shop."

### Trade a little for a lot
- **Small input, large return:** "Give [small effort] and get [large result]." Example: "Give 15 minutes to set prices once and stop re-pricing by hand each week."
- **Easy condition:** "If you can [everyday skill], you can [valuable task]." Example: "If you can send a mobile-money payment, you can raise an invoice."

### Use numbers and superlatives carefully
- **Counted list:** "[N] [things] that [outcome]." Example: "Six settings new users miss, each of which saves minutes daily." Use the true count of what is inside.
- **Single best:** "The one [thing] that [result]." Only when provable. Example: "The one report that shows which product actually earns you money."
- **Better-than:** "Better than [familiar option]: [improvement]." Example: "Better than an exercise book: stock levels that update as you sell."

### Warn and correct
- **Never-do:** "What never to [action]." Example: "What never to say to a customer who asks for a discount."
- **Challenge a habit:** "Do you [common mistake]? Here is why that costs you." Example: "Do you price by ingredient cost alone? You may be underpricing your busiest dishes."
- **Myth-busting:** "The truth about [misunderstood topic]." Example: "The truth about loyalty cards: some cost more than they return." Check that the claim is true before publishing.

### Teach
- **How-to:** name the specific result, not the vague topic. Weak: "How to manage stock better." Stronger: "How to keep shelves full of what sells and stop tying up cash in slow lines."
- **Themed group:** put bullets under one evocative heading ("Five quiet profit leaks in a restaurant kitchen").

## 3. Assembly Rules

1. Use at least three or four different mechanisms per page.
2. Group bullets in fives to sevens, with a line or subhead between groups.
3. Lead each group with its strongest bullet; readers notice the first and last.
4. Do not end a bullet with a full stop; end on an open thought or a strong word.
5. Keep bullets parallel in length where possible, and vary the opening word.
6. Never promise what the product or download does not contain. A blind bullet must be answered.
7. Do not invent statistics. State a figure only if you can source it.

## 4. Process

1. Draft five candidate bullets for each mechanism that fits the offer.
2. Score each on curiosity, clarity and truth; keep the strongest third.
3. Sort by section or reader concern.
4. Mix mechanisms within each group and order by strength.
5. Read aloud. If a bullet neither stirs curiosity nor states clear value, cut it.
6. Test variations on real traffic; do not assume a mechanism works in your market.
