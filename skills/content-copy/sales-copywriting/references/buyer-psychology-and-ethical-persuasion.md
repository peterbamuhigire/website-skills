# Buyer Psychology And Ethical Persuasion

Parent skill: [`sales-copywriting`](../SKILL.md).

This is an original synthesis of the supplied *The Bezos Letters*, *Yes!*, *Buyology*, and *The Adweek Copywriting Handbook*. Use these books for durable concepts only. Current platform behaviour, scientific claims, legal requirements, and performance benchmarks require current authoritative verification.

## The website belief path

Design each page around:

`recognise me -> understand the problem -> believe the mechanism -> trust the proof -> resolve risk -> choose the next step`

### Choice architecture

- Give the page one primary decision and one primary CTA.
- Present no more options than the reader can meaningfully compare; explain the criteria and trade-offs.
- Make price, scope, exclusions, commitment, cancellation, delivery timing, and what happens next visible.
- Use a recommended option only with a plain-language reason and a visible alternative.
- Match CTA friction to readiness: learn, diagnose, compare, book, or buy.

### Proof and social context

- Place proof beside the claim it supports.
- Prefer named, permissioned, specific proof over logos, vague praise, or anonymous claims.
- State the segment, period, baseline, and limitation of a result where material.
- Never imply “everyone is doing it”, scarcity, authority, or popularity without a traceable basis.
- Use customer language from interviews or approved records; do not invent a persona’s words.

### Memory, context, and sensory cues

Use a small, consistent set of brand cues—language, visual motif, interaction rhythm, onboarding ritual, or service handover—to help the reader recognise the offer. Test the observable effect on comprehension, recall, qualified enquiry, or completion. Remove any “neuromarketing” explanation that cannot be supported; a conversion change does not prove a brain mechanism.

### Copy momentum

- Make the first sentence earn the second.
- Move from reader situation to consequence, mechanism, proof, objection, and action.
- Use one curiosity bridge only when it adds information; do not manufacture suspense.
- Lead with emotion only when the page immediately justifies the feeling with facts, mechanism, and proof.
- Edit until the page sounds like this firm speaking to this buyer, not a generic marketing system.

## Ethical stop rules

Stop and qualify the page when it relies on hidden terms, fake urgency, fabricated testimonials, fear without an effective remedy, dark-pattern defaults, shame, unnecessary data collection, or unsupported psychological/scientific claims. Restore the last transparent baseline and record the defect.

## Conversion experiment record

For every variant, log:

`changed belief -> audience slice -> hypothesis -> primary measure -> guardrail -> duration/sample boundary -> result -> decision`

Primary measures may include qualified enquiry, completed checkout, or booked diagnostic. Guardrails include refund, complaint, cancellation, support burden, accessibility, and lead quality. Do not call a variant a winner from an unverified or confounded lift.

## QA checklist

- [ ] The page names its intended buyer and decision.
- [ ] The mechanism is clearer than the adjective.
- [ ] Proof is real, relevant, permissioned, and placed in context.
- [ ] The CTA promises a concrete next experience.
- [ ] Options, price, terms, and risk are transparent.
- [ ] The page works without colour, urgency, or social proof doing all the persuasion.
- [ ] Current claims and tests have evidence, dates, and review ownership.
