# Pricing-Page Choice Architecture and Honest Persuasion Cues

Parent skill: [sales-copywriting](../SKILL.md). Read when writing or reviewing
a pricing, packages, plans, investment or rate-card page; when deciding where
price appears on a site; when writing cancel, unsubscribe or switching copy;
or when adding contact, social-proof or loyalty cues.

Visitors decide mostly by cues, not by long deliberation. Whatever the page
mentions becomes a decision criterion. Good choice architecture makes the right
option easy to see and choose; it never tricks anyone. Every cue on this page
must be true.

## Order of work

1. **Usability first.** Speed, clarity, navigation and mobile layout are hygiene
   factors: persuasion cannot work on a page that is hard to use. Fix them
   before adding cues.
2. **Match expectation.** The headline must echo the ad, search or link that
   brought the visitor (see the channel-to-site handoff in `launch-campaigns`).
3. **Add one diagnostic cue per decision point**: a comparison, a
   recommendation, a rating or a guarantee.
4. **Offer a human** at high-anxiety points.
5. **Remove clutter** that reads as not credible.

## Seven rules for a plans or packages page

| # | Rule | Application |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Show 2–4 options per group | Three tiers for retainers; three packages per service line |
| 2 | Never show only one option | People judge by comparison; even bespoke work can show two or three scoped options |
| 3 | Group like with like | Website builds on one tab, care plans on another, one-off audits on a third |
| 4 | Align rows | Same features, same order, same words in every column |
| 5 | One true highlight | "Most chosen", "Best value" or "Recommended for [who]": one badge only, and only if it is literally true |
| 6 | Put the plan you most want to sell in the middle | People avoid extremes; on phones, where there is no middle, badge it and order it first or clearly |
| 7 | One strong visual cue | A rating, a guarantee or a recommendation; not all of them at once |

More moves:

- **Subordinate "Free".** A free column beside a much higher paid tier pulls
  attention and cannibalises the entry plan. Show a free option as a smaller
  link, or replace it with a trial or guarantee placed where the eye lands
  (below the price or above the button).
- **Decide the lead plan first**, then design around it: badge, middle
  position, the clearest description.
- **Order as anchor (a test, not a rule).** Listing the most expensive plan first
  sets a high reference and may raise revenue per visitor while lowering sign-up
  count; listing the cheapest first may maximise customer numbers. Run it as an
  experiment with revenue per visitor as the primary metric and enquiry count
  and lead quality as guardrails.
- **Name tiers by buyer or outcome** ("Solo practice / Clinic / Hospital group";
  "Launch / Grow / Lead"), not "Basic / Pro".
- **Put a contact route directly under the table**: "Not sure which fits? Call
  or WhatsApp [name] on [number]. We reply within [time] during [hours]."
- **Choice overload is context-dependent.** Research after the well-known jam
  study found the effect varies and is sometimes near zero; test option counts
  rather than asserting a rule.

## When to show price

| Offer position | Where price appears |
|---|---|
| Premium or high-value offer | Not in the home-page hero. Build value and proof first; give ranges or starting prices on an Investment or Pricing page that is always easy to find |
| Commodity, crowded market or offer campaign | Early: home page or landing page, so the question is answered at once |
| Pages where price is expected (pricing, product detail, checkout) | Always, plainly, without hesitation |
| Returning visitors | Sooner: a pricing link or banner, because they are already primed |

Rules:

- **Discounts show three numbers together**: original price, new price and the
  saving, with the end date. "20% off" alone produces clicks that bounce.
- **Frame premium offers around time and outcome**, not money: "Spend an
  afternoon with us and leave with a plan your board will approve."
- Very low prices lower expectations; price always signals value.

## Visible phone and WhatsApp as a trust signal

A visible way to reach a person reassures visitors because they *could* use
it; most will not, and those who do are usually high-intent buyers. Hiding the
number frustrates buyers and sours the call when it finally comes.

- Show a tap-to-call number and a WhatsApp click-to-chat link in the top band of
  key pages, under pricing tables, and on the contact page.
- State hours and the reply-time promise, and keep it.
- Label a support-only line as support-only.
- Staff it, or do not show it.
- Implementation without third-party scripts: see
  [third-party facades](../../../build/page-builder/references/third-party-facades.md).

## Social-proof hygiene

- **Low numbers are negative proof.** Hide counters (subscribers, likes,
  downloads, reviews) until they are persuasive; remove share-count widgets
  from pages not being promoted.
- **Only genuine proof.** Testimonials and reviews are real, attributable and
  held with consent. Invite genuine early clients and partners to engage as
  themselves.
- **Truthful early-stage framing**: "founding clients" or "pilot cohort" only if
  it is true.
- **Never**: fake names, staff or relatives posing as customers, purchased
  followers, likes or reviews. These deceive buyers, breach platform policies,
  and in some jurisdictions breach consumer law (for example the US FTC rule on
  consumer reviews and testimonials, in force since 21 October 2024; register
  AD-09).

## Endowed progress (loyalty, onboarding, referrals)

People work harder as a goal gets closer. Give a real head start:

- a loyalty card of 12 boxes with 2 genuinely stamped on sign-up, reward after
  10 paid visits;
- an onboarding checklist with step one already done (because it really is);
- a referral form that asks for four names "as a thank-you, not five".

Keep targets reachable (about 10–12 steps), give a completion reward, and never
let earned progress expire without clear notice.

## Exit and switching copy

- **Cancel and unsubscribe must be easy to find and quick to complete.** Use an
  honest action label ("Cancel my membership") and a factual line about what the
  person keeps or loses ("You'll lose your 14-month savings streak"). Offer a
  pause or downgrade.
- **No confirm-shaming** ("No thanks, I don't care about my business"), no hidden
  or looping flows. Obstructive cancellation is treated as a dark pattern by
  regulators in several markets; check the current rules for the client's
  jurisdiction.
- **Switching headlines sell the gain or the loss avoided**, never "quit X":
  "Get back three hours a week: move your invoicing to [Brand]." "Moving payroll
  from spreadsheets? We'll import last month's records for you."

## Audit lens (six dimensions)

When reviewing a page's persuasiveness, score: informativeness, usability,
credibility, inspiration (aesthetic first impression), involvement and
reciprocity. Fix usability and credibility before the others.

## Metrics

| Metric | Use |
|---|---|
| Pricing-page conversion rate | Option-count and order tests |
| Revenue per visitor | Anchor-order test |
| Plan mix | Checks the badge and middle-position effect |
| Contact rate and close rate for calls and WhatsApp | Validates the contact cue (expect low contact, high close) |
| Cancellation save rate, with complaint rate as guardrail | Honest exit framing |
| Programme completion rate | Endowed-progress design |

All tactics here are hypotheses to test with a sample-size plan (see
`experimentation`), not guarantees.

## Ethics filter

Before publishing any cue, ask: is it true; would the buyer feel misled if they
knew how it was produced; does it respect the buyer's right to leave? If any
answer fails, remove the cue.
