# Professional Page and Blog Phrase Bank

Parent skill: [premium-commercial-writing](../SKILL.md). Read when the page structure is already fixed (see [website page writing patterns](website-page-writing-patterns.md)) and you are drafting or revising the sentences themselves: a service hero, a why-us block, a process section, a pricing or investment page, a case study, an About page, a blog introduction or conclusion, or CTA lines.

This file works at sentence level. The patterns file decides which sections a page has; this file decides what each sentence in those sections must do and how to phrase it so that it reads as written by a competent person for a specific buyer.

## 1. The scaffold rule

Every template below is a scaffold, not copy. Before drafting, build a one-page fact sheet for the page: buyer's own words (from calls, emails, reviews, WhatsApp enquiries), counts, dates, places, named comparable clients (with permission), prices, response times, people and their real experience.

1. Fill each slot only from the fact sheet.
2. If a slot has no verified fact, delete the sentence. Never fill a numeric or named slot with an adjective.
3. Mark any inference as inference in the draft notes, and route it to the claim register required by the parent skill.
4. Never publish a template with a slot left in it, and never invent a quotation, figure, client, award or result to complete one.
5. The worked examples in this file use invented businesses and figures to show shape. They are illustrative only and must never be reused as facts.

Slot notation: `[Client]` `[Company]` `[n]` `[%]` `[UGX amount]` `[timeframe]` `[place]` `[named alternative]` `[metric]` `[outcome]` `[their phrase]`.

## 2. The sentence-job grammar

Professional web copy is built from short paragraphs in which each sentence has one job. The grammar below adapts the "power paragraph" used for proposals and plans to web pages.

| Rule | What it means on a web page |
|---|---|
| One job per sentence | Need, then why it matters, then the benefit. Never two jobs in one sentence. |
| Named subject | "Kira clinics lose weekend bookings" beats "Many businesses face challenges". On service pages the subject is usually "you"; on case studies it is the client's name. |
| Numbers early | A count, amount, time or place in the first or second sentence. |
| Cause, then consequence | "[Condition], which [effect on the thing the buyer cares about]." |
| Named alternative | Say what the buyer does today instead ("phoning three suppliers", "a spreadsheet the accountant rebuilds each month"). |
| Forward conclusion | End on the buyer's position after the work, never on a summary of the page. |
| Length | Keep sentences to about 25 words or fewer; keep a lead paragraph to 3 or 4 sentences. |

Person and voice: use second person ("you") on service, pricing and contact pages; first-person plural ("we") on About and process pages; third person (the client's name) in case studies. Keep one choice per page.

## 3. Service page hero and intro

Sentence jobs: (1) the need as the buyer feels it, ideally in their words; (2) why solving it matters now, so that "do nothing" stops looking safe; (3) the longer-term benefit and how the work gets them there.

Templates:

- You are [task] with [current workaround], and it is costing you [time/money/customers] every [period].
- [Buyer type] in [place] tell us the same thing: "[their phrase]".
- Every [period] this stays unsolved, [concrete loss: bookings, invoices, staff hours] goes to [named alternative or nowhere].
- Fixing [problem] now means [immediate result], before [dated event: peak season, audit, launch].
- We [mechanism in plain words] so that you can [longer-term outcome] without [constraint they refuse to give up].
- For [narrow buyer type] who need [outcome] by [timeframe].

Before and after (illustrative):

- Generic: "We deliver innovative digital solutions that help businesses grow in today's competitive landscape."
- Human-professional: "Your weekend clinic slots go empty because bookings arrive by phone and one in three patients never turns up. We set up WhatsApp booking with a reminder the day before, so the front desk confirms every slot and you can keep Saturdays open."

## 4. Why-us and expertise block

Sentence jobs: (1) what this kind of work actually requires; (2) the proof that you meet that requirement (counts, named people, named comparable work); (3) a summary of relevant experience with a link to the full list.

Decision rules:

- State **one** key advantage, tied to a buyer need. A list of five differentiators reads as none.
- Choose the advantage from two or three value themes that score high on all three tests: the buyer rates it important, you can prove it, and competitors do not already say it. Everything else becomes supporting copy or an FAQ answer.
- Expertise is proven by counts, names and comparable projects, not by adjectives such as "experienced" or "passionate".

Templates:

- [Kind of work] needs two things: [requirement 1] and [requirement 2].
- It should be led by someone who has [done the precise thing] before.
- Our [role] spent [n] years as [client-side role], and our [team] has [delivered n comparable projects] for [buyer type] in [place].
- We have done this for [peer 1] and [peer 2]; both [result] within [timeframe]. [See the projects.]
- Unlike [named alternative the buyer considers], we [single difference], which matters because [buyer need].

Before and after (illustrative):

- Generic: "Our experienced team of passionate experts brings unmatched knowledge across many industries."
- Human-professional: "Hospital websites need accurate clinical content and a booking system that talks to the patient records. Our content lead spent six years in communications at a private hospital, and we have connected online booking to three record systems used in Uganda."

## 5. Process and "how we work"

Each step answers five questions in plain words: what happens, who does it, what you provide, what you receive, how long it takes. Put responsibility in active voice, one responsibility per sentence.

Templates:

- Week [n]: we [activity]. You provide [access/content/decision] and receive [artefact] by [day].
- [Named role] is your single contact and replies within [n] working hours.
- Decisions on [scope/brand/budget] stay with you; we recommend, you approve.
- If [common delay] happens, we [agreed fallback] so the launch date holds.

Options-considered block (use when a buyer may prefer a cheaper or faster route; candour about rejected options builds credibility):

- There are [three] common ways to [goal]: [A], [B] and [C].
- [A] is fastest to launch but [drawback tied to their goal].
- [B] costs least up front but leaves you [dependent on/exposed to] [risk].
- We usually recommend [C] because it protects [the buyer's stated priority]. If [constraint] matters more, [A] remains a sound choice and we will say so.

Before and after (illustrative):

- Generic: "Our proven methodology ensures a smooth journey from concept to launch."
- Human-professional: "In week one we interview three of your customers and read your last month of WhatsApp enquiries. You give us two hours of your time and access to the inbox; you receive a one-page summary of what buyers ask before they book."

## 6. Pricing or investment page

Sentence jobs, in this order: (1) the price, stated plainly and first; (2) what the buyer receives for it, linked to their goal; (3) what is also included; (4) who the package is for and not for.

Rules:

- Price first, value beside it. Never make the reader dig for the number.
- No apology or minimising words before the price: "only", "just", "a small investment of".
- Use "fee" for services and "investment" for strategic work, and keep one term throughout the site.
- Core deliverables first, extras as clearly priced options. This protects the base price and gives buyers a way to reduce scope.
- Put the fit case next to the price, because a cheaper alternative always exists.
- Present tiers and anchors using [pricing-page choice architecture](../../sales-copywriting/references/pricing-page-choice-architecture.md); this file supplies the sentences only.

Templates:

- [Package] costs [UGX amount], paid in [n] instalments tied to [milestones].
- It includes [deliverable], [deliverable] and [n] days of support after launch.
- For that fee you get [the one thing the buyer's current setup lacks].
- Also included: [extra with a concrete benefit].
- Optional: [add-on] at [UGX amount], if you need [situation].
- Built for [buyer type] with [size/stage]. If you need [out-of-scope need], [alternative route or referral].

Before and after (illustrative):

- Generic: "Our competitive pricing offers excellent value for money. Contact us for a quote."
- Human-professional: "The Growth site costs UGX 18,500,000 in three milestone payments. It covers discovery, design, build, copy for up to 14 pages and 60 days of support. You get what most dealership sites lack: every enquiry reaches a salesperson on WhatsApp within five minutes. Also included: training for two of your staff to update stock without calling us."

## 7. Proof and case study

The patterns file fixes the section order. Use these sentence jobs inside it: stakes, alternatives considered, what was done, result against a baseline, what it made possible next.

Templates:

- [Client] had [n] [weeks/days] before [deadline], and [problem] was blocking [consequence].
- They weighed [option 1], [option 2] and [option 3].
- They chose [option] because [reason in their terms].
- We [specific actions, including the hardest part].
- Within [period], [metric] moved from [baseline] to [result], measured by [method].
- [Role] said: "[approved quotation]".
- It also gave them [second-order benefit], which [future opportunity].

Credibility checks before publishing: the client approved the text and figures in writing; the baseline and the measurement method are stated; the page says what the client did as well as what you did; confidential metrics appear only as approved ranges or qualitative proof.

Before and after (illustrative):

- Generic: "We successfully transformed the client's digital presence, delivering outstanding results."
- Human-professional: "With six weeks to the new financial year, the SACCO's paper records were holding up loan approvals. It considered temporary clerks, an off-the-shelf system, and phased digitising starting with active borrowers. It chose the phased route; we trained two staff per branch. Approval time fell from 14 days to 3, measured from application to decision."

## 8. About page and team bios

The About page transfers trust. Its lead paragraph states the role the organisation plays for the buyer, the standard it holds, and one piece of proof. For the story spine behind it (brand role, human driver, plot, community as hero) see the [brand story spine reference](../../../brand/brand-storytelling/references/brand-story-spine-roles-drivers-plots.md).

Templates:

- [Company] [role verb: helps, protects, equips, guides] [buyer type] in [place] to [outcome].
- We started in [year] after [verified origin insight].
- Our standard: [specific practice a buyer can check, such as a published reply time].
- Today we [traction: clients, sites, years, places], and [one proof].

Three-line bio (each line one job):

1. Credibility: [Name], [role], [n] years in [field], [one verifiable credential or result].
2. Problem solved: on your project [he/she/they] [specific responsibility].
3. Human detail: [a true, relevant detail: language spoken, community tie, practice outside work].

Before and after (illustrative):

- Generic: "We are a passionate, customer-focused team committed to excellence."
- Human-professional: "Every complaint we receive is answered within four working hours, and we publish our reply times each month. We have kept that standard for 212 clients since 2019."

## 9. Blog introduction

Sentence jobs: (1) a dated, sourced change in the reader's world; (2) the action it forces on the reader; (3) the costly way most people respond; (4) the better route this article offers.

Rule: the change must be real, dated and sourced. If you cannot verify a change, open instead with the reader's observed problem or question. Never open with a broad industry trend.

Templates:

- Since [date], [change] has required [who] to [action].
- Most [readers] are responding by [costly workaround], which [cost].
- There is a simpler route: [one-line preview].
- If you are [reader type] asking "[their question]", this article answers it in [n] steps.

Before and after (illustrative):

- Generic: "In today's rapidly evolving digital world, businesses must adapt to stay ahead."
- Human-professional: "Since [regulator] extended [requirement] to [business type] on [date, verified], wholesalers must issue compliant invoices from every till. Most are hiring clerks to re-key sales. There is a cheaper route, and it starts with the tills you already own."

## 10. Blog conclusion

Sentence jobs: (1) the immediate fix, restated as an action; (2) the second-order benefit the reader also gains; (3) the opportunity that opens next; (4) one next step.

Templates:

- Start with [first action] this week.
- Once [immediate issue] is fixed, you also get [second-order benefit].
- That puts you in a position to [future opportunity] by [timeframe].
- If you want [help/a check/the template], [single next step].

Rules: look forward, never back; no "In conclusion" and no summary of headings; one next step.

Before and after (illustrative):

- Generic: "In conclusion, as we have seen, digital marketing is essential for success. Contact us today!"
- Human-professional: "Send tomorrow's reminders by WhatsApp and track who replies. Within a month you will know which patients need a call, and that list becomes the start of a recall programme for check-ups. If you would like our reminder templates, [download them here]."

## 11. CTA lines

A CTA names the reward and the next step, and states what happens after the click.

Templates:

- [Verb] [the reward]: "Get the price for your site", "Book a 20-minute fit call", "See three dealership sites we built".
- Reassurance line under the button: "We reply within [n] working hours. No payment needed to talk."
- Human route beside the form: "Prefer WhatsApp? Message [number]; we answer between [hours]."
- Transitional CTA for buyers not ready: "Not ready yet? Read what a site like this costs."

Avoid: "Submit", "Learn more", "Click here"; countdowns or "only 2 slots left" unless the constraint is real and stated; confirm-shaming decline links ("No thanks, I don't want more customers"). Write a neutral decline: "Not now".

## 12. Words and habits to avoid on pages and posts

The full slop list lives in the [genuine-writing and AI-slop gate](genuine-writing-and-ai-slop-gate.md). The habits below are the sentence-level failures this bank is designed to prevent.

| Habit | Replace with |
|---|---|
| Writer-centred need ("You need a new website") | The buyer's goal in their terms ("You want to stop losing weekend bookings") |
| Superlatives without proof ("best", "leading", "unmatched", "world-class") | The evidence that would justify the claim, or nothing |
| Benefit words with no object ("efficiency", "growth", "value") | Efficiency of what, by how much, by when |
| Five differentiators | One advantage tied to one buyer need |
| Unbounded audience ("all businesses in East Africa") | The narrow buyer the page is really for |
| Buried or apologetic price | Price first, value beside it |
| Backward conclusions ("As outlined above") | A forward consequence and one next step |
| Visible template reuse ("as we did for X, adapted") | Visible customisation in nouns: their sites, their staff, their customers |
| US hype idioms ("can't-miss", "slam dunk", "game-changer") | Calm, precedent-based reassurance ("we have done this at [n] sites") |
| Customisation claimed in adjectives ("bespoke", "tailored") | Customisation shown in facts about this buyer |

## 13. Register rules for East African and international buyers

- Measured and courteous; formal but not stiff. Reassure rather than boast.
- Name the district, town or estate (Kira, Mukono, Wakiso, Westlands, Kisumu) instead of "the region".
- Currency: `UGX 18,500,000` or `KES 250,000`, with thousand separators; state whether VAT is included.
- Name the real pressure the buyer faces (a compliance deadline, mobile-money reconciliation, power cuts) only when it is verified and dated.
- Use conditional verbs ("would", "could") for benefits you cannot guarantee and indicative verbs for facts. Never promise results you do not control.

## 14. Quick rewrite bank (illustrative)

1. "We offer high-quality services at affordable prices." -> "Monthly bookkeeping for Kampala SMEs costs UGX 450,000 and includes invoice reconciliation, which most firms charge extra for."
2. "We have many years of experience." -> "Since 2019 we have built 37 websites for professional-services firms in Uganda and Kenya, 11 of them for law firms."
3. "Our solution is unique." -> "Two providers in Uganda offer USSD member statements; we are one of them."
4. "We will increase your sales." -> "The target is 40 more qualified enquiries a month by the third quarter, counted through tagged WhatsApp links."
5. "We are passionate about customer satisfaction." -> "Every complaint is answered within four working hours, and we publish the reply time."
6. "This partnership creates synergies." -> "Your 60 outlets gain our product line, and we gain your delivery routes."

## 15. Acceptance checklist

- [ ] Every filled slot traces to the fact sheet; no slot remains in the draft.
- [ ] Each lead paragraph has one job per sentence, in order.
- [ ] A number, name or place appears in the first two sentences of each lead.
- [ ] Service copy states the need in the buyer's words and says why acting now matters.
- [ ] One key advantage, tied to a buyer need, with proof in the same or next paragraph.
- [ ] The price appears first in the pricing paragraph, with no apology words.
- [ ] Case studies state stakes, alternatives, actions and a result against a baseline, all approved by the client.
- [ ] Blog introductions open with a dated, sourced change or the reader's own question.
- [ ] Conclusions look forward and end with one next step.
- [ ] CTAs name the reward, the next step and what happens after the click; no fake urgency or confirm-shaming.
- [ ] The draft passes the genuine-writing and professional quality gates.
