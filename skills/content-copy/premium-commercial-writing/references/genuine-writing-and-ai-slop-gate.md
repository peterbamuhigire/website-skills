# Genuine Writing And AI-Slop Gate

Use this reference before drafting and before final delivery whenever website
copy, blog posts, SEO pages, landing pages, or authority content might sound
generic, synthetic, or under-researched.

## Core Rule

Do not write polished emptiness. A publishable draft must contain real audience
understanding, specific claims, grounded examples, visible judgment, and a
human-readable point of view.

## Pre-Draft Evidence Gate

Before drafting, collect the strongest available inputs:

- business facts: offer, audience, geography, prices or price posture, process,
  constraints, proof, owner/founder notes, and exclusions
- buyer language: interviews, reviews, sales objections, support questions,
  search queries, CRM notes, or credible forum language
- proof: case studies, screenshots, photos, named credentials, results,
  demonstrations, policies, methodology, or original data
- sources: primary sources first, then named experts, regulators, official docs,
  reputable research, and dated articles

If the evidence is thin, write a short evidence-gap note and lower the strength
of the claims. Do not compensate with adjectives.

## Drafting Standard

Every meaningful section should do at least one of these:

- answer a buyer question
- explain a decision, tradeoff, risk, or limitation
- prove a claim with a specific detail
- make the offer easier to understand
- move the reader to the next useful action

Prefer concrete nouns and verbs. Use examples from the actual business, market,
customer, tool, project, country, or sector. Write with a clear editorial stance:
what matters, what does not, who this is for, who it is not for, and why.

## Banned AI-Slop Patterns

Remove or rewrite:

- generic openings such as "In today's fast-paced digital world"
- vague transformation claims with no mechanism
- stacked adjectives: innovative, seamless, robust, cutting-edge, world-class
- empty empathy: "we understand your unique needs" without naming them
- fake balance: "whether you're a small business or large enterprise" when the
  offer has a real best-fit buyer
- unsupported superlatives, rankings, guarantees, or market claims
- generic FAQ padding that no buyer actually asked
- paragraphs that summarize the section heading instead of adding information
- invented anecdotes, testimonials, numbers, awards, credentials, or citations
- anonymous UGC used as proof for high-stakes claims

## Human Texture Pass

After drafting, add what a generic model would usually miss:

- named constraints and tradeoffs
- local/regional context where relevant
- actual process steps, timelines, handoffs, or failure points
- owner, practitioner, or customer judgment
- price, scope, readiness, or fit caveats where useful
- specific examples rather than category summaries

Use restraint. Human texture is not slang, jokes, or forced personality; it is
evidence of lived judgment.

## Source-Integrity Pass

For factual, technical, financial, legal, health, safety, procurement, or
reputation-sensitive claims:

- cite or preserve the source in notes
- prefer primary sources and named researchers/institutions
- verify dates and whether the claim may have changed
- separate user sentiment from verified fact
- do not cite AI summaries as sources
- do not treat Reddit, Quora, Wikipedia comments, reviews, or forum snippets as
  sole proof

## Final Acceptance Checklist

- [ ] The opening names the reader's real situation or question.
- [ ] The piece has a clear point of view, not just neutral filler.
- [ ] Important claims are proved, attributed, qualified, or removed.
- [ ] At least three details could not be pasted onto a competitor's site.
- [ ] The draft includes examples, process, tradeoffs, or constraints.
- [ ] CTAs match the reader's readiness and the page's job.
- [ ] Sources and evidence gaps are visible to the next operator.
- [ ] No banned AI-slop patterns remain.

## Machine-error editorial gate (ME1-ME7)

Apply Digital Research's dated machine-error gate before release. ME1 checks whether adjacent copy
repeats meaning; ME2 whether symmetry is decorative; ME3 whether explanation continues past reader
use; ME4 whether claims inflate consequence; ME5 whether examples are client-specific; ME6 whether
the same rhetorical device has become a tic; and ME7 whether each paragraph earns space with a claim,
evidence, instruction, or decision. Merge or cut copy with no delta. Preserve repeated labels,
accessibility text, and legal notices when their function is explicit.
