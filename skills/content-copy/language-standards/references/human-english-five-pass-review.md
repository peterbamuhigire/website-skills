# Human English Five-Pass Review

Parent skill: [language-standards](../SKILL.md). Read when writing or reviewing any English web page, landing page, blog post, SEO or AI-search asset, CTA, form, error message, email, or support message.

This review is mandatory for English website copy. It makes copy specific, accurate, and easy to act on. Human quality comes from considered, evidence-backed choices, not from ornament, simulated informality, or deliberate imperfection.

## Standard to meet

Write for a visitor who has arrived with a question, a worry, a comparison, or a task. The copy earns trust by stating:

- what is true and how the reader can check it;
- what is included and what is not;
- what happens next, when, and who is responsible;
- what the organisation does not promise.

Default to British English and a neutral East African register unless the brief names another market. Apply the market decision from the parent skill before drafting.

## The five passes

Run the passes in order. Do not start a later pass while an earlier pass has an open blocker.

### Pass 1 — Reader and purpose

Record, before drafting:

1. audience (who is reading, their role, and their prior knowledge);
2. page job (the single outcome this page exists to produce);
3. arrival intent (search query type, referral, campaign, or internal link);
4. emotional temperature (calm research, anxiety, urgency, frustration, celebration);
5. next action (the one step the reader should be able to take).

Blocker: no page job or no next action. Stop and obtain them from the brief owner.

### Pass 2 — Genre and page spine

Choose the genre (service page, product page, guide, policy, form, notification, email) and build the spine:

1. orientation (where the reader is and what this page offers);
2. problem or need, in the reader's words;
3. offer or answer;
4. proof placed next to each claim;
5. process (steps, timing, responsibilities);
6. objections and limits;
7. next step;
8. state copy (loading, empty, error, validation, success, confirmation, privacy).

Check that the genre's conventions are met: a recommendation gives a reason, a procedure gives ordered steps, a policy gives scope and exceptions.

### Pass 3 — Evidence and facts

Verify every material item against a source the owner can produce:

- claims and results;
- testimonials (permission, attribution, date);
- prices, fees, and terms;
- locations, service areas, and opening hours;
- credentials, accreditations, and memberships;
- links and link destinations;
- accessibility promises;
- entity names used for search (organisation, service, product, place, person).

Unverified items are removed, qualified, or marked for the owner. Never invent a figure to fill a gap.

### Pass 4 — Revision

Revise at four levels:

- **Headings**: each heading remains useful when scanned alone; headings follow the reader's questions.
- **Paragraphs**: one job per paragraph; the first sentence carries the point.
- **CTA labels and microcopy**: name the action and, where useful, the result (`Request a stock check` rather than `Learn more`).
- **Grammar, rhythm, and scan order**: vary sentence length naturally; keep proof beside the claim it supports.

### Pass 5 — Proof in context

1. Read the page on a desktop layout and a narrow mobile layout.
2. Read it aloud; mark every place you stumble.
3. Skim headings only; confirm the page still makes sense.
4. Trigger or inspect error, empty, loading, validation, confirmation, and privacy states.
5. Check spelling variety (British by default), numbers, dates, currency, and units against the market convention.

## Grammar and lexical checks

Check each of these explicitly during Pass 4:

| Area | What to check |
|---|---|
| Agreement | subject–verb and pronoun–antecedent agreement, including collective nouns |
| Articles | a / an / the / zero article, especially before abstract and plural nouns |
| Tense | consistent tense within a section; future for promises, present for standing facts |
| Prepositions | the preposition the verb or adjective takes (`comply with`, `responsible for`) |
| Pronouns | a clear referent for every `it`, `this`, `they` |
| Relative clauses | `that` / `which` / `who` choice and comma use |
| Parallelism | list items share one grammatical form |
| Modifiers | no dangling or misplaced modifiers |
| Punctuation | sentence boundaries, serial-comma policy, apostrophes, hyphenated compounds |
| Spelling | one variety throughout; confusable words (`affect`/`effect`, `complement`/`compliment`, `principal`/`principle`) |
| Cohesion | connectors show the logical relation (cause, contrast, sequence) |

## Word-choice rules

- Prefer the exact common word. Use an advanced word only when its meaning, connotation, collocation, and register are all right for this reader.
- Check word partnerships in context; apply the collocation overlay in [english-collocations-and-lexical-precision-2026-09-02.md](english-collocations-and-lexical-precision-2026-09-02.md).
- Replace vague praise with a service detail, a result with evidence, a process step, a limit, a comparison, or an honest qualification.
- Give each page and paragraph one clear job.
- Vary rhythm naturally. Do not fill every section with the same three-item pattern.
- Do not add typos, fake colloquialisms, or inflated warmth to make copy seem human.
- Treat intensifiers (`very`, `truly`, `world-class`) as claims that need evidence; cut them when none exists.

## State-copy guidance

For every interactive element, write the states as part of the page, not as an afterthought:

| State | The copy must say |
|---|---|
| Loading | what is happening, without false precision about time |
| Empty | why it is empty and the first useful action |
| Validation | which field, what is wrong, and how to fix it, in plain words |
| Error | what happened, whether data was kept, what to do next, and where to get help |
| Success / confirmation | what was received, what happens next, when, and a reference where relevant |
| Privacy | what is collected, why, and where the reader can read more |

## Anti-slop tests

Apply these questions to the finished draft. Any "no" requires a revision.

1. Could this page belong to ten unrelated companies? If yes, add the real service, audience, place, process, proof, boundary, or next action — or cut the passage.
2. Does the first screen orient the visitor?
3. Do headings remain useful when scanned alone?
4. Are claims honest and placed close to their evidence?
5. Are all interaction states covered?
6. Does the page make a good argument, rather than relying on SEO structure to carry it?

## Review record

Record these fields for every reviewed page or message:

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| `page_job` | the single outcome |
| `audience` | who reads and acts |
| `source_proof_map` | each material claim and its source |
| `market_language` | market, English variety, register |
| `terminology_checks` | entity names, product terms, confusables checked |
| `state_copy_review` | states inspected and result |
| `read_aloud_proof` | done / not done, with notes |
| `unresolved_gaps` | missing proof, owner questions |
| `reviewer` | name or role |
| `date` | review date |

## Use of source genres

- A controlled technical manual teaches observation, consistent classification, and evidence before interpretation; apply those habits to product and service descriptions, not to tone.
- Vocabulary study supports selective word choice and confusable-word checks; it does not justify rare words.
- Error-correction practice supports the grammar checklist above; do not treat simplified exam answer keys as usage authority.
- Advanced examination writing models teach genre, audience, register, task coverage, paragraphing, and reasoned recommendation; they are not page templates.
