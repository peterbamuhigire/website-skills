# Consultant and Creator Site Kit

Parent skill: [content-writing](../SKILL.md). Read when the site belongs to one person rather than a firm: an independent consultant, expert, speaker, coach, author or content creator whose clients, brands and event organisers need to decide quickly whether to book them. Complements the [standard page kit](standard-page-kit.md), which covers business pages; this file covers what changes when the product is a person.

People who book consultants, speakers and creators are usually screening a list. They skip anyone whose basics are hard to find: a clear photo, what the person does, where they are based, how to reach them, and evidence of range. The site's first job is to make the person easy to shortlist.

## 1. Minimum page set

| Page | Job | Must contain | Avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Home | Say who, what, for whom, where, and route to the three pages below | Name, one-line description of the work, city, headshot, one proof, two routes (work with me, contact) | Slogans with no description; splash pages |
| About | Build the relationship with the audience or clients | Current headshot; a few lines written to the reader; a short talking-to-camera video or sample of work; 3 to 5 best pieces showing range | A full CV; a life story unrelated to the reader |
| Work with me (partnership or services) | Act as the cover letter for buyers | A different headshot; name, what you do and three precise descriptors; where from and where based; relevant background; searchable specifics (section 3); types of engagement offered; how pricing is set; a link to past work | A wall of past-client logos; published fixed prices (section 4) |
| Contact | Make contact certain | A direct email for business enquiries, a separate one for press or readers, WhatsApp or phone with hours, expected reply time, location or time zone | A contact form as the only route |
| Press or media kit | Serve journalists, event organisers and partners | Downloadable one-sheet and press kit (section 5), approved bios in three lengths, high-resolution photos with credits, topics | Waiting until someone asks |
| Speaking (if relevant) | Help organisers book | Topics with one-line outcomes, formats (keynote, panel, workshop, virtual), past events with dates, a video extract, technical needs | Topics described only by title |
| Work or case archive | Show results and range | Projects or sponsored work grouped by type, each with date, client (with permission), what was done and the result | Undated or unattributed work |

## 2. Bio formulas

Profile or header bio (one or two lines): what you create or do, for whom, city, contact. Example (illustrative): "Kampala style creator: office wear that works in 28°C. English and Luganda. Collaborations: amina@example.com".

Three-line professional bio:

1. Credibility: role, years, one verifiable credential or result.
2. The problem you solve and for whom.
3. A true human detail relevant to the reader (languages, community, practice).

Provide the approved bio in three lengths (about 30, 80 and 200 words) on the press page, so organisers do not rewrite it.

## 3. Searchable specifics

Buyers and organisers search saved kits and pages by keyword. Make the specifics explicit, in text rather than images:

- specialisms and sectors, in the words buyers use;
- languages spoken and written;
- places served: districts, counties, cities, countries;
- formats offered (workshops, audits, retainers, keynotes, video series);
- relevant memberships, registrations and qualifications (current and verifiable);
- causes or community affiliations the person genuinely supports;
- for creators: audience description as reported by the platforms' own analytics, with the date the figures were taken.

Never state an audience figure, reach number or rating without its source and date.

## 4. Pricing and past clients

- Do not publish fixed prices on the partnership page, one-sheet or press kit. Rates depend on scope, usage rights, exclusivity and timing. Keep a separate rate sheet sent on request, and state the pricing basis on the page: "Rates depend on scope, usage and exclusivity. Tell me about your budget and I will propose what fits."
- For consultants whose market expects a price signal, a "from" figure or a price range with its scope is acceptable; follow the [pricing-page choice architecture](../../sales-copywriting/references/pricing-page-choice-architecture.md).
- Avoid a wall of past-client or sponsor logos on the partnership page: it can put off a competitor of a past client, and logos without context prove little. Instead link to an archive of named work, each item shown with the client's permission.
- Where the person sells to firms that are not competitors of each other (for example, a speaker at industry events), a small set of permitted logos with context is acceptable.

## 5. One-sheet and press kit

- **One-sheet**: one page with headshot, three-line bio, what you offer, searchable specifics, two or three proof points, and contact details. No prices.
- **Press kit**: the About, Work with me and Contact content as a designed PDF, plus photos and bios. No prices.
- Build both from the site's approved text so the three never disagree, and date them. Visual design and typography are routed to `design-system-skills`.

## 6. Contact rules

- Offer a direct business email; a form may sit alongside it but never replaces it.
- Separate business enquiries from press or reader messages.
- State the reply time and meet it; set an out-of-office message with an urgent route when away ("I am travelling until [date] and replying within [n] hours. For time-sensitive work, email [address] or WhatsApp [number].").
- Show the time zone for international buyers.

## 7. Consistency and home base

- The site is the home base the person controls; social profiles can be lost or restricted.
- Use the same name and handle on the site and every active profile, and check that every profile link on the site works.
- Search the person's name each quarter: the first results page should show their own site, profiles and interviews.
- Keep the headshot and video current; replace anything older than about a year or no longer accurate.

## 8. Disclosure and safeguarding

- Mark sponsored or paid work as such in the archive and in any post embedded on the site. No influencer-specific disclosure law was found for Uganda or Kenya as of September 2026, so apply clear "Ad" or "Paid partnership" labelling as good practice and check the current position before a campaign (register AD-10); in the United States and United Kingdom specific rules apply (register AD-09).
- If family members or children appear, never show the home exterior or a child's school, post location details only after leaving, and have a parent or guardian's written consent (and the child's agreement where old enough).
- Contact forms and mailing lists collect personal data; the privacy notice comes from `policy-pages` (registers PL-01, PL-02).

## 9. Before and after (illustrative)

- Generic header: "Lover of fashion, coffee and good vibes. Living my best life."
- Bookable header: "Nairobi food creator: 20-minute family suppers. English and Swahili. Brand work: wanjiru@example.com"
- Generic partnership line: "Let's collaborate! DM me for rates."
- Professional: "I work with brands on sponsored videos, recipe development, live cooking at events and long-term ambassadorships. Rates depend on scope, usage and exclusivity. Email wanjiru@example.com with your dates and budget."

## 10. Handoff

| Handed over | Artefact | Owner |
|---|---|---|
| Personal-brand strategy, social growth, rate setting and creator deal negotiation | Positioning notes and rate basis | The social media engine (`social-media-skills`), in its personal-brand and creator-monetisation skills |
| Speaking or consulting proposals | Approved bios, topics and proof list | `proposal-skills` |
| Headshot direction, one-sheet and press-kit layout | Content and specifics from this kit | `design-system-skills` |

This engine owns the site pages, their copy and the downloadable kit content.

## 11. Acceptance checklist

- [ ] Home, About, Work with me and Contact pages exist and route to each other.
- [ ] Headshot, city, what the person does and a direct email are visible without scrolling on a phone.
- [ ] Searchable specifics are written as text, current and verifiable.
- [ ] No fixed prices in the partnership page, one-sheet or press kit; the pricing basis is stated.
- [ ] No logo wall on the partnership page; past work is shown with permission and dates.
- [ ] Contact is never form-only; reply time is stated and tested.
- [ ] Every audience or reach figure has a source and date.
- [ ] Sponsored work is labelled; family and child safeguarding rules are met.
