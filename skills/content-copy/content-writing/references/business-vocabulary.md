# Business Vocabulary Reference

Parent skill: [content-writing](../SKILL.md). Read when choosing register, transitions, data phrasing, business-letter wording or persuasive phrasing for any content-generating skill (content-writing, page-builder, sales-copywriting, blog-writer).

## 1. Choosing the Register

Decide the register first, then choose words. Default to formal for professional, institutional and B2B brands; allow informal only for conversational B2C copy.

Formal-writing rules:
1. Understate feeling: "rather disappointed", "somewhat surprised".
2. Avoid exclamation marks.
3. Use the passive when the action matters more than the actor.
4. Avoid contractions.
5. Replace phrasal verbs and idioms with single formal verbs.
6. Vary sentence openings with concessive structures ("Although A, B").

### Formal and informal pairs

| Formal | Informal |
|---|---|
| depart, carry out, provide, retain | go, do, give, keep |
| cease, seek, assist, obtain | stop, look for, help, get |
| desire, request, function, demonstrate | want, ask for, work, show |
| reside, require, compensate for, persuade | live, need, make up for, talk into |
| subsequently, sufficient, further | later, enough, more |
| hence / therefore, inexpensive | so, cheap |
| furthermore, in addition, a number of | what is more, also, several |
| in my view, in conclusion | I think, to finish |
| lack of / deficiency | there is no / little |

## 2. Linking and Sequencing

| Job | Words |
|---|---|
| Start a sequence | Firstly, Initially, At first |
| Continue | Next, Following that, Then, Once, As soon as |
| Simultaneous | At the same time, Simultaneously |
| Before / after | Prior to, Following |
| Finish | Finally |
| Add | In addition, Furthermore, Moreover |
| Contrast | However, Nevertheless, On the other hand, Whereas, Although |
| Cause and effect | Therefore, Hence, As a result, Consequently |
| Example | For example, For instance, Such as |
| Similarity | Likewise, Similarly |
| Emphasis | In particular, Notably, Especially |
| Conclude | Overall, In conclusion |

Subordinators for complex sentences: although, whereas, whenever, until, since, rather than, even though, as long as, by the time, now that, whichever.

### Cohesion by reference
Point back with "this" or "these" plus a summarising noun (this approach, these factors, this process, this evidence, this policy). Slot: "Late invoicing drives most cash-flow problems. This pattern is common among small distributors."

Test: underline the key sentence of each paragraph. Read only those; the argument must still make sense. If not, rewrite.

## 3. Describing Data

### Trend verbs

| Pattern | Phrases |
|---|---|
| Sharp rise | soared, rose dramatically |
| Steady rise | rose gradually, increased steadily |
| Sharp fall | plunged, dropped sharply |
| Steady fall | declined gradually, fell steadily |
| No change | remained stable, remained flat at |
| Levelling | levelled off at, evened out at |
| Top / bottom | peaked at, reached a high of / bottomed out at, hit a low of |
| Unstable | fluctuated, varied, was erratic |
| Dip and recover | dipped slightly before recovering |

### Proportions in words

5% one in twenty; 10% one in ten; 20% one fifth; 25% one quarter; 33% one third; 40% two fifths; 50% half; 60% three fifths; 66% two thirds; 70% seven in ten; 75% three quarters; 80% four fifths.

Approximators: just over, just under, almost, nearly, roughly, approximately. Example: 74% becomes "nearly three quarters"; 48% becomes "just under half".

### Comparison phrases
- Share: the bulk of, the largest proportion of, a smaller proportion of, the majority of.
- Multiples: three times the number of, a third of the number of.
- Gaps: significantly higher, noticeably higher, slightly higher.
- Minimising: a mere, only, just.

Rule: state only figures you can source. Attribute every figure to its owner and date.

## 4. Persuasive Writing

### Four high-yield words
1. "You": address the reader; use it more than "we".
2. The reader's name where the medium allows (email, proposals).
3. "Free": use only when true, and state exactly what is free.
4. "Because": attach a reason to every request. Test the effect on your own CTAs rather than assuming it.

### Principles
- Prefer facts to opinions. Weak: "Customers love it." Stronger: "Rated 4.7 out of 5 by 312 verified customers" (only if true).
- Be specific: numbers, named clients (with permission), dated evidence.
- Group points in threes where content allows; do not pad to reach three.
- Audience first: before each persuasive section, write what the reader believes, fears and wants.
- Order: current reality, then problem or opportunity, then solution.

### Stating opinions formally

| Function | Phrases |
|---|---|
| Own stance | In my view; We hold that; There can be little doubt that |
| Others' views | It has been suggested that; Some argue that; It is often claimed that |
| Consensus | It is generally agreed that; Few would dispute that |
| Evidence | Research suggests that; The evidence indicates that |
| Concession | However; Nevertheless |

## 5. Letters and Emails

### Structure
1. Greeting: "Dear Mr Kato" if known; "Dear Sir/Madam" if not.
2. Reason for writing.
3. Detail, in short paragraphs; questions phrased indirectly.
4. Closing action.
5. Sign-off: named greeting takes "Yours sincerely"; Sir/Madam takes "Yours faithfully".

Pre-send checklist: formal register throughout; all needed information present; indirect questions; paragraphs; proofread.

### Openers
- I am writing to / with regard to / on behalf of ...
- I am writing in reply to your ...
- I would like to apply for the position of ...

### Requests
- I would be grateful if ... / I wonder if you could ...
- Please find my CV attached (email) or enclosed (letter).
- I am available for interview at any time convenient to you.
- With regard to / Regarding / Concerning ...

### Complaints
- Openers by strength: "I am writing to complain about ..."; "I wish to express my dissatisfaction with ..."; "I wish to complain in the strongest terms about ..."; "I am writing to inform you of an apparent error in ...".
- Topic words: poor service, slow service, faulty item, damaged packaging, replacement, refund, repair, inspection, restore to working order.
- Closers: "I look forward to a prompt reply."; "I would be grateful for a full refund by [date]."; "I suggest immediate steps be taken."; "I wish to draw your attention to ...".

### References
Slots: "I have known [name] for [period]." / "[Name] is reliable, organised and at ease with clients." / "I recommend [name] without reservation." / "Please contact me if you have questions."

### General closers
- Formal: "Should you require further information, please do not hesitate to contact me."
- Informal: "Tell me if you need anything else."

## 7. Process, Change and Growth Language

### Noun and verb families for process descriptions
storage/store; delivery/deliver; assembly/assemble; packaging/package; construction/construct; development/develop; conversion/convert; transformation/transform; installation/install; distribution/distribute; production/produce; harvest/harvest.

### Before-and-after phrases
replaced; took the place of; was demolished to make way for; was converted into; was completely transformed; was expanded; was reduced.

### Market and growth phrases (fill only with sourced evidence)
- "Demand is growing, led by [segment] in [region]."
- "[Industry] is forecast by [named body] to reach [figure] by [year]."
- "Customers have asked us directly for [solution]."
- "A [year] study by [body] found that [group] regularly [action]."

Avoid unsourced hype; every slot needs a checkable source.

## 9. Building a Client Vocabulary

- Record sentences, not isolated words: term, definition, example sentence.
- Learn word families (tradition, traditional, traditionally).
- Suffix signals: -tion/-ment/-ity/-ness make nouns; -al makes adjectives; -ly makes adverbs; -ise makes verbs; -er/-or names the doer.
- Learn clusters (all verbs of financial change together) rather than random lists.
