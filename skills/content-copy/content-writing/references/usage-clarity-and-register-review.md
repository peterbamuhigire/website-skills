# Usage, Clarity, and Register Review for Search-Aware Content

Parent skill: [content-writing](../SKILL.md). Read when planning or reviewing a page or content cluster that must use the reader's own language, keep a distinctive human voice, and connect search, social, and on-site journeys without keyword stuffing.

This reference joins three lenses into one working brief: the reader's journey across channels, the language readers actually use, and a recognisable, specific voice. It supports the SEO and AI-search skills; it does not create an AI-only content format.

## 1. Journey before channel

A reader's experience starts before the website visit and continues after it: search, social conversation, the owned destination, response, and follow-up. Plan all of these as one path.

Before choosing a channel or format, decide:

- the audience job (what the reader is trying to get done);
- the organisation's purpose for this piece;
- voice, proof, destination page, and intended outcome.

Operating rules:

1. Combine evergreen pages with genuinely timely updates. Give every item a page job, owner, source, approval state, expiry or review date, and recovery path.
2. Adapt one core idea to the conventions of each destination. Do not copy wording mechanically across channels, and do not treat people as clicks or impressions.
3. Keep community participation conversational and useful, and subject to house rules, consent, moderation, and rights checks.
4. Connect content, SEO, social, sales, support, legal or compliance, and analytics through explicit handoffs with named owners.

## 2. Language map: plan, research, implement, review

Keywords are labels that help people and systems understand an offer. They never replace a real page purpose or a useful answer. Run this loop:

1. **Plan.** Start with the customer's vocabulary, problem, place, offer, and decision.
2. **Classify.** Assign intent and page role before collecting long keyword lists.
3. **Group.** Build a topic and entity map. Record ambiguous terms and the context that disambiguates them.
4. **Record.** Keep a dated language resource file (template below).
5. **Triangulate.** Gather language from customer evidence, Search Console or equivalent first-party data, live search observation, competitor discovery, and keyword tools. Note what each source can and cannot establish.
6. **Review.** Judge qualified actions and reader usefulness, not rankings or traffic alone. Change one meaningful variable at a time where practical.

### Language resource file template

| Field | Entry |
|---|---|
| `term` | the phrase as readers use it |
| `source` | where it was observed (interview, query data, support log, tool) |
| `date` | observation date |
| `locale` | market and language variety |
| `intent` | informational, commercial investigation, transactional, navigational, support |
| `page` | the one page that owns the term |
| `proof_burden` | evidence the page must show to deserve the term |
| `owner` | person responsible for the page |
| `next_review` | date to re-check the term |

### What each language source can establish

| Source | Establishes | Cannot establish |
|---|---|---|
| Customer interviews, sales and support logs | real vocabulary, objections, decision words | volume or trend |
| First-party search data | queries that already reach the site | demand the site does not yet reach |
| Live search observation | current result types and competing page roles | personalised or future results |
| Competitor discovery | how the category describes itself | whether readers use those words |
| Keyword tools | relative demand estimates | exact volume, intent, or conversion |

## 3. Voice as a quality control

Reader-friendly does not mean bland. Voice comes from deliberate choices of word, syntax, rhythm, mood, tone, point of view, and the values a text makes recognisable.

1. Define three to five voice commitments and matching anti-commitments (for example, "plain and exact" / "never vague praise"). Test them against real audience language and the brand's evidence.
2. Use plain language and clear structure without removing specificity, local texture, or a defensible point of view.
3. Use editing to improve clarity, accuracy, inclusion, and usefulness — not to make every contributor sound identical.
4. Treat imitation and templates as learning scaffolds only. Never mimic another organisation or manufacture a personality.
5. Keep uncertainty, disagreement, and limits visible when they matter to the reader's decision.

### Register check

For each page, record the register (formal, neutral, conversational) and confirm:

- the register fits the audience's expectation for this task (payments and policies stay formal or neutral);
- the register is held consistently across headings, body, CTAs, and state copy;
- idiom and humour are absent from navigation, forms, errors, privacy, and payment text.

## 4. Six-layer page brief

Complete this brief for any page or cluster that depends on search or social discovery.

| Layer | Decision required | Evidence or gate |
|---|---|---|
| Journey | Where did the reader come from, what job are they doing now, and what is the safe next action? | journey map, route, CTA, failure path |
| Language | Which customer terms, synonyms, entities, places, and ambiguities must be understood? | dated resource file and intent map |
| Value | What original proof, explanation, experience, or decision support does the page add? | claim–proof map and named author or owner |
| Voice | What should be recognisably human and specific without obscuring the answer? | voice notes, read-aloud edit, accessibility review |
| Distribution | Which owned, earned, or authorised social or community path can help a real person find it? | rights, moderation, canonical-link, and approval checks |
| Learning | What will be measured separately for visibility, referral, qualified action, and revenue? | instrumentation map, baseline, guardrail, review date |

Current search guidance governs implementation: helpful, reliable, people-first, crawlable, understandable content; readers' words in prominent, natural places; no keyword stuffing; no assumed preferred word count; no special AI markup. Check the current Google Search Essentials, SEO Starter Guide, and AI optimisation guide before stating any platform rule.

## 5. Claims to quarantine

Do not carry these forward from older marketing or SEO material without a fresh primary-source check:

- named network rosters, interface steps, product availability, or analytics screens;
- claims that likes, shares, velocity, or any social network directly produce search rankings or AI citations;
- fixed keyword counts, keyword density, head/body/long-tail volume assumptions, or universal conversion promises;
- references to specific algorithms, ranking updates, or retired analytics products presented as current rules;
- any benchmark, statistic, platform rule, local fact, legal claim, or rights assertion without a current authoritative source.

## 6. Reversible pilot

Use this when introducing the brief to a team.

- **Hypothesis:** a brief that joins journey stage, intent language, original proof, distinctive voice, distribution permissions, and separate outcome measures produces fewer generic pages and clearer handoffs than a keyword list alone.
- **Pilot:** apply it to one real page or cluster. Record the baseline, source map, content change, approval state, and route.
- **Measure:** search visibility or citation observation separately from referral, qualified action, and revenue.
- **Guardrails:** no fabricated proof, no rights or privacy breach, no keyword stuffing, no manufactured mentions, no accessibility regression.
- **Stop and roll back** if evidence becomes contradictory, the page job is unclear, or a material claim cannot be sourced.
- **Standardise** only after the owner reviews the result and records a re-audit date.
