# Standard Page Kit and Lean Home-Page Policy

Parent skill: [content-writing](../SKILL.md). Read when scoping a site's page
inventory, briefing content for core pages, or reviewing a home page that has
become crowded. `page-builder` implements these pages; `policy-pages` owns
privacy and terms.

## Lean home-page policy

The home page helps visitors decide where to start. It is not the About page
and not a storage area.

1. **Header block** on every page says who you are, what you do, where, and
   gives the main routes, because visitors land on any page.
2. **At most two primary actions** on the home page. Everything else is a
   route to a single-focus page.
3. **One in, one out.** Add a home-page section only if one leaves. Record the
   decision in the owner decision register.
4. **A featured slot** that the client can change (new service, event,
   seasonal offer) instead of permanent "What's new" clutter.
5. **Summary, not biography.** At most a short About summary with a link.
6. **Local belonging.** For local businesses, show place through copy and
   imagery: neighbourhood, landmarks, local clients (with permission), not only
   an address.
7. **No splash or intro pages.** Language or audience choices go in the real
   home page or header.
8. **Header height.** The first screen on a phone must show the offer and a
   next step, not only a tall photograph.

## Page kit

| Page | Job | Must contain | Avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Service or product (one per offer) | Answer one need completely | Who it is for, what is included, process, price or price guidance, proof, next step; location where local | A single "Services" page that makes visitors guess |
| Services overview | Route to the right detail page | Two or three sentences per offer, each linking to its page | Full detail duplicated |
| About | Build the relationship | Clear description and the story of why the business exists; real photos, preferably in action; team write-ups; related links | CV, history lecture, sales pitch |
| Contact | Make contact easy and certain | All routes on one page: WhatsApp link and number, phone with hours, form, address with landmark directions, map (facade or static), premises photo from the road, expected reply time, confirmation after submit | Email address as the only route; unclear reply expectations |
| Quote or booking | Capture a qualified request | Short form, what happens next, response time | Asking for data not needed yet |
| FAQ | Answer genuinely frequent questions | Short answers linking to detail; topic groups past about 20 questions; a route to ask a new question; new questions fed back | Marketing copy phrased as questions; promising a search rich result (FAQ rich results stopped showing in Google on 7 May 2026, register CW-09) |
| Proof (testimonials, reviews, case studies) | Show real results | Name, role, organisation and photo with permission; reviews quoted fairly and linked; named case studies | Anonymous cases; testimonials styled to look like social posts they never were |
| Media or press | Serve journalists, partners, tender panels | Current logos, bios, photos, profile PDF, registrations and certificates where relevant | Waiting until someone asks |
| Resources | Keep useful documents findable | Described, current links and downloads | Dead links |
| Privacy, terms, cookies | Trust and legal basis | Via `policy-pages`; reviewed by a lawyer where required | Copied templates from another jurisdiction |
| Custom 404 | Recover lost visitors | Site navigation, short apology in brand voice, search or site map link, most-wanted content | "Under construction" pages anywhere |
| Site map (HTML) | Orientation on larger sites | Hierarchical list; linked from 404 | — |

## Testimonial drafting for approval

Clients often agree to give a testimonial but never write it. Offer to draft it
from what they already said:

> "Could we quote you on the website? Here is a short draft based on what you
> told us. Change anything you like, or reply 'approved'."

Keep the client's words and claims; never invent results. Record approval with
the date.

## Decisive details

Ask the owner, a knowledgeable colleague and a few customers to list the
details that decide a purchase (payment methods such as mobile money, insurers
accepted, delivery areas, parking, opening hours, registrations). Merge, let
customers rank them, then place each by importance: every page, section
sidebar, FAQ or contextual link. For recurring temporary information, design a
slot that appears only when relevant.

## Worked rewrite

- Generic: "Welcome to ABC Ltd, the leading provider of quality solutions."
- Specific: "ABC repairs commercial air conditioning in Kampala. Same-day
  call-outs for offices, clinics and hotels. [Book a site visit]"
