## Quality standards
- Recommendations must be concrete enough to apply immediately.
- Changes should improve consistency, usability, or credibility without flattening the brand.
- The standard should support downstream implementation rather than slow it down.
- Each page should have a job: rank, educate, compare, prove, convert, reassure, onboard, or retain. If the job is unclear, do not polish the copy; fix the page strategy.
- Website copy must contain evidence of the actual business: specific offer,
  audience, proof, process, market, geography, constraints, examples, or owner
  judgment. Generic copy that could fit a competitor fails.
- If research or proof is missing, surface the gap instead of filling it with
  vague claims, synthetic examples, or polished filler.

## Anti-patterns
- Do not apply every rule mechanically when only a subset is relevant.
- Do not give generic critique with no change implications.
- Do not override project reality with taste-based preferences alone.
- Do not publish grammatically clean but empty AI-like copy.
- Do not invent testimonials, numbers, credentials, case details, customer
  quotes, citations, local facts, or market claims.
