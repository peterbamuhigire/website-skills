# Search and Answer-Friendly Content Writing

Parent skill: [`../SKILL.md`](../SKILL.md)

Write for a real reader and page job. Search and AI-answer usefulness comes from
clear intent, original value, verifiable claims, accessible structure, and a
satisfactory next step—not keyword density, arbitrary length, or AI-only prose.

## Required brief

- audience, market, language, and task;
- primary and compatible secondary intent;
- page role and canonical route;
- entity and fact source;
- proof burden and claim owner;
- current source register;
- internal-link role and next action;
- measurement event and update trigger.

Stop when material business, product, legal, health, financial, location,
pricing, review, or performance claims lack evidence.

## Workflow

1. State the page job and the question or decision it must resolve.
2. Map claims to primary, current, and appropriately scoped sources.
3. Identify the non-commodity contribution: original data, practitioner
   experience, method, example, specification, case evidence, or useful synthesis.
4. Draft the shortest complete answer appropriate to the task; preserve
   conditions, exceptions, jurisdiction, version, and uncertainty.
5. Organise the reading path with natural headings, paragraphs, lists, tables,
   media, and links. Questions are optional; no machine-chunking quota applies.
6. Make each important fact understandable when extracted: name the subject,
   scope, period, method, and limitation.
7. Add a next action that matches visitor readiness and disclose what happens
   after the action.
8. Review accuracy, source support, grammar, accessibility, originality,
   commercial honesty, and machine-error repetition.
9. Record publication and substantive modification dates plus the next review
   trigger; do not refresh dates cosmetically.

## Keyword and language discipline

- Use language real users employ when it remains accurate and natural.
- Research each locale natively; do not translate an English keyword list.
- Cover concepts, entities, synonyms, and necessary terminology through useful
  explanation, not repetition targets.
- Do not require a keyword in the H1, first sentence, first H2, conclusion, or a
  fixed percentage of words.
- Keep one primary page job while supporting compatible questions naturally.
- Create a new page only when intent, audience, offer, location, or task is
  materially distinct.

## Answer patterns

Use a pattern only when it fits the user task:

- **Definition:** direct definition, scope, boundary, example, source.
- **Procedure:** prerequisites, ordered steps, failure/recovery, result.
- **Comparison:** criteria, evidence, trade-offs, fit, decision method.
- **Product/service:** audience, problem, operation, requirements, proof,
  limitations, price posture, support, next step.
- **Current fact:** answer, as-of date, jurisdiction/version, primary source,
  next verification trigger.
- **Case evidence:** baseline, intervention, period, sample, result, method,
  limitation, client approval.

There is no universal 30-, 40-, or 60-word answer rule. Use the shortest wording
that remains complete and responsible.

## Authority and entity clarity

- Identify the responsible organisation, author, reviewer, product, service,
  location, and source where relevant.
- Keep names and facts consistent with authoritative owned pages and legitimate
  profiles.
- Cite primary sources for current regulations, standards, prices, product
  behaviour, and other time-sensitive claims.
- Do not fabricate expertise, first-hand experience, statistics, reviews,
  clients, partnerships, or independent coverage.
- Treat Wikipedia and community content as governed third-party environments,
  not controllable marketing profiles.

## Structured data handoff

Give `seo` a fact map and candidate types, not invented JSON-LD. Structured data
must match visible content and a current consumer. Google no longer shows HowTo
or FAQ rich results; visible procedures and FAQs may still serve users.

## SXO handoff

The page should make fit, evidence, limitations, next action, and post-action
expectations clear. Measure task completion and qualified outcomes according to
the page job. Do not use universal bounce, time-on-page, conversion, or visitor-
readiness percentages as quality rules.

## Acceptance checklist

- [ ] Page job, audience, market, and language are explicit.
- [ ] Material claims have current sources, scope, dates, and limitations.
- [ ] The page adds original or practitioner value.
- [ ] The opening is useful without obeying a fixed length formula.
- [ ] Headings and sections follow human comprehension, not artificial chunking.
- [ ] Entity names and public facts are consistent.
- [ ] Internal links help the task and expose related canonical pages.
- [ ] CTA and post-action expectations match visitor readiness.
- [ ] Publication, substantive modification, and review dates are truthful.
- [ ] AI assistance did not introduce unsupported claims, generic filler, or
      near-duplicate pages.
