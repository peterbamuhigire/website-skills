# Keyword Research Procedure

Parent skill: [seo-audit](../SKILL.md). Read when choosing, scoring, mapping or measuring target keywords for a page, an article or a whole site, particularly for East African and Francophone African ICT, software and professional-services markets.

Organised by task: understand what a keyword is, gather buyer language, study the results, score and prioritise, map to pages, then measure. Every figure below is a planning assumption or a check to run, not a fact: volumes, difficulty and trends must come from current tools and the site's own data.

---

## 1. Principles that govern every choice

1. **A keyword is a compressed expectation.** The searcher condenses a mental picture (a reliable local professional who can fix their problem) into a few words. Each target keyword is a hypothesis about how buyers express a need; wrong words make the page invisible however good the service is.
2. **Buyer vocabulary beats seller vocabulary.** Words belong to communities. A Ugandan procurement officer, a Nairobi start-up founder and a Dakar business owner searching in French describe the same need differently. Use the words the buyer uses for the problem, not the words the agency uses for its solution.
3. **Right keyword plus relevant destination equals conversion.** The keyword must match what the searcher expects, and the page must deliver it. The snippet, title, H1, opening paragraph and call to action must all reinforce one theme. A page that ranks for a term it does not answer earns clicks and loses visitors, and high bounce is a sign of mismatch. Relevance is necessary but no wording pattern guarantees ranking or durability.
4. **Keywords reveal the stage of the buying journey.** Match each keyword to a stage and give the page a matching goal: no consultation call to action on an early informational article; put it on a decision-stage service page.
5. **Vocabulary is a trust signal.** Using the exact terms a community uses internally signals membership ("ICT consulting" in East African public-sector and NGO contexts; "système d'information" in Francophone business). Wrong vocabulary produces an unexplained sense of mismatch.
6. **Research is continuous.** Analytics show which keywords bring qualified visitors, which bring bounces, which reveal demand for services not yet built, and which unexpected phrases real buyers use. Feed these back quarterly.

### Close the vocabulary gap

| Seller wording | Buyer wording |
|---|---|
| ICT advisory services | IT help for my business |
| Digital transformation consulting | How to computerise my company |
| Software engineering solutions | Someone to build a system for me |
| Business process reengineering | Fix how my company operates |
| PropTech solutions | Software for managing my rental properties |
| ERP implementation | Connecting my accounts and inventory |

Method: before opening any tool, interview five buyers or read five real enquiry emails and record every word they use. These are the seed keywords.

### Choose jargon level by funnel stage
Jargon excludes; plain professional wording includes. Awareness-stage terms use plain buyer language; decision-stage terms may use technical vocabulary because the buyer has learned it and uses it to filter providers.
- Awareness: "how to improve my business with technology Uganda" (plain).
- Consideration: "ERP system vs custom software Africa" (intermediate).
- Decision: "ERP implementation company Uganda" plus a named integration (technical).

Also read the sophistication behind variants: "ICT consultant" versus "IT consultant"; "website designer" (aesthetics) versus "web developer" (functionality); "logiciel sur mesure" (a buyer who wants to be addressed as a peer); "how do I get a website for my business in Uganda" (a first-time buyer who needs guidance, not jargon).

## 2. Gather seed keywords

Start inside the business, then translate to buyer language. List every service, every problem solved, every client type, then add geography.

Sources:
- Service descriptions (translated to buyer words).
- Enquiry emails and calls, verbatim.
- Competitor page titles.
- Industry associations and publications.
- Social conversations where buyers describe challenges.
- Search autocomplete suggestions for each seed.
- "People also ask" boxes (question-form long-tail).
- Related searches at the foot of results pages.

### Mandatory three-wave study for articles and long-form pages
Pair keyword research with the digital-research-engine's three-wave study for every article. It is a content and evidence workflow, not a promise of rankings or volume.

- **Wave 1, map intent.** Define audience, decision, market and language, then group buyer language into 3 to 7 related query clusters. Record exact queries, provider, access date and geography. Use an approved search or API tool; do not scrape result pages or invent volume figures.
- **Wave 2, read the set.** Read the accessible top five results per cluster. Capture title, headings, format, audience, evidence, sources, localisation, call to action, strengths and missing reader questions. Mark blocked or unread pages `UNASSESSED`. A snippet is a discovery lead, not evidence.
- **Wave 3, design the gap.** Compare what the set answers, repeats and omits. Select one defensible article job, primary and secondary terms, answer sections, internal links and authoritative sources. Verify material claims at the original source and record unresolved checks. For English and French, map intent separately and adapt wording to the market rather than translating the keyword literally.

Keep a dated record: queries, five-result URLs and read status, competitor patterns, content and AI-answer gaps, source map, chosen terms, outline, limitations and measurement plan. Competitor patterns inform the editorial opportunity but do not prove market facts. Claim an outcome only with Search Console or other performance data.

## 3. Expand, classify and build patterns

Expand each seed into a cluster using tools, then group by theme, intent and geography. A cluster usually corresponds to one page.

### Head terms, specific terms and the long tail
- Head terms: high volume, high competition, early-stage visitors. Specific terms (long tail): lower volume, higher conversion because the searcher knows what they want.
- Strategies: branding (broad terms, many impressions, low conversion), conversion (specific terms, fewer impressions, high conversion), hybrid (both).
- New sites with low authority should lead with specific terms, build rankings there, then compete on broader terms.
- In aggregate the long tail often outweighs the head. Its benefits: less competition, cheaper paid clicks, higher conversion, precise page matching, and topical authority from covering many related terms. Global results tend to dominate broad terms like "IT consultant"; geo-modified terms like "IT consultant Kampala small business" usually face little competition (verify per query).

### Construction patterns (slot templates)
- Service plus specificity plus place: "[service] for [client type] [country]" (for example "ERP implementation for SMEs Uganda").
- Problem plus place: "how to [outcome] in [country]"; "why my website is not getting customers [country]"; "how much does [service] cost in [country]".
- Comparison plus place: "[option A] vs [option B] [region]".
- Urgency plus place: "[role] available [city]"; "urgent [deliverable] [country]".
- Vertical plus service plus place: "[service] for [industry] [country]" (for example property management software, software for NGOs, ERP for manufacturing in East Africa).
- Conversational form: "Who is the best [role] in [city]?"; "Where can I find [role] in [city]?"; "Is it worth getting [service] for my business in [region]?" Include such prompts only where user evidence supports them.

### Geographic modifiers
- Uganda (primary): `[service] Uganda`, `[service] Kampala`, `[service] company Uganda`, `hire [role] Uganda`, `best [role] in Uganda`, `affordable [service] Uganda`, `how much does [service] cost in Uganda`; secondary towns where the client base exists (Entebbe, Jinja, Mbarara, Gulu).
- East Africa: `[service] East Africa`, plus Kenya/Nairobi, Tanzania/Dar es Salaam, Rwanda/Kigali, Ethiopia/Addis Ababa, and EAC.
- Africa broadly: `[service] Africa`, `African [role]`, `[service] for African businesses`, `pan-African [service]`.
- Francophone Africa: see section 8.

Use local terms and named locations only where they match genuine service relevance; do not add them merely to target voice interfaces.

### Service cluster starter lists
Adapt as slot lists; verify each in a current tool.
- IT and ICT consulting: head terms "IT consultant [country]", "ICT consultant [city]", "technology consulting services"; long tail on choosing a consultant, small-business and NGO consulting, cost, managed IT and support, technology strategy, digital transformation.
- Software development: "software developer [country]", "custom software development [city]", "app developer"; long tail on SME software, ERP implementation and developers, mobile and web applications, cost, finding a developer, SaaS, database systems, inventory, HR, payroll, school and hospital management.
- Website development: "website developer [country]", "web designer [city]"; long tail on cost, affordable design, small business, e-commerce, NGO, church, restaurant, WordPress, redesign, maintenance, "who is the best website designer in [country]".
- Business planning: "business plan writer [country]", "business consultant [city]"; long tail on plans for bank loans, development-finance applications, start-ups and business proposals, cost, SME consulting, feasibility studies, market research, registration guidance.
- Social media management: "social media manager [country]"; long tail on Facebook and Instagram management, agency, strategy, content creation, growing followers, advertising.
- Property technology: "property management software [country]"; long tail on landlords, rental tracking, tenants, real estate management, portfolio management.
- French equivalents for each: "consultant informatique", "conseil en systèmes d'information", "développeur logiciel", "développement sur mesure", "création site web", "agence web", "rédacteur plan d'affaires", combined with a target country or city.

## 4. Study competition and find gaps

Answer three questions: which terms do competitors rank for that we do not target; what weaknesses do their pages have; which clusters are uncontested.

1. **Identify real competitors**: local companies and freelancers, regional consultancies, French-language firms serving Africa, and indirect ones such as freelancer marketplaces that rank for hiring terms.
2. **Assess each ranking page**: keyword in title, URL, H1 and description; content depth; keyword in the first sentence and subheadings; backlink count, source and anchors.
3. **Look for exploitable weaknesses**: no geographic modifier; thin content on important terms; no structured data; no local signals (address, phone, map); slow mobile pages.
4. **Find uncontested terms**: results with no well-optimised page in the top five, dominated by directories, or showing thin, poorly formatted pages. A well-structured, well-written page can move quickly there.
5. **Gap analysis**: list about ten competitor URLs; export their ranking keywords using a paid or free tool; filter to terms where the site is outside the top 20; cross-check against services and geographies; prioritise by (commercial intent times volume) divided by competition strength.

To judge how many pages truly compete, test the deeper results pages of the query and compare with the headline total; the real count is usually much lower. Confirm the current method, since search engines change how deep results pages go.

## 5. Score and prioritise

### Qualifying test (a golden keyword passes all four)
1. **Adequate volume.** Enough real searches to justify content. For professional services in smaller markets a modest figure for a high-intent geo term can be excellent; use relative volume, not absolute.
2. **Commercial intent.** Words such as hire, cost, price, services, company, agency, consultant, developer; several advertisers bidding; specific service plus place plus modifier. Check by counting ads on the results page, reading estimated cost-per-click, and seeing whether the phrase appears in real enquiries.
3. **Stable or rising trend.** Check a trends tool; never invest in a declining term (for example one tied to software being phased out).
4. **Weak competition.** Few truly competing pages, weakly optimised top results, thin content, few or low-quality backlinks.

Sequence: no volume, discard; no commercial intent, discard or deprioritise; declining, discard; strong competition, deprioritise or find a longer-tail variant; all four present, target it.

### Composite score
Score each candidate 1 to 5 on four dimensions; the total is out of 20.

| Dimension | 5 means | 1 means |
|---|---|---|
| Commercial intent | Strong buying intent ("hire", "cost", service plus place) | Pure information seeking |
| Relevance to services | Exact match to an offered service | Tangential |
| Competition | Very weak (thin pages, low authority) | Dominated by large authoritative sites |
| Volume | Solid for the market (relative) | Near zero |

A weighted alternative when relevance and competition matter most: (relevance times 2) plus specificity plus (competition times 1.5) plus popularity. Discard any term the page cannot credibly answer.

| Score | Tier | Action |
|---|---|---|
| 16 to 20 | Tier 1, immediate | Dedicated page, fully optimised, within 30 days |
| 12 to 15 | Tier 2, important | Page or section within 60 days |
| 8 to 11 | Tier 3, develop | Blog content within 90 days |
| 4 to 7 | Tier 4, opportunistic | Secondary keyword in existing content |
| 1 to 3 | Tier 5, deprioritise | Monitor only |

Quick-win multiplier: multiply the score by 1.5 where no well-optimised page holds positions 1 to 5 for the specific geo-modified term. A 14 that is uncontested becomes 21 and outranks any standard Tier 1.

### Small-market caution
Keyword tools often undercount search volume in smaller and African markets, so a term showing near-zero may still generate qualified leads. Compare with Search Console data (actual performance) and trust that over tool estimates. One qualified visitor from "IT consultant Uganda" is worth more than a hundred from a broad international term; quality and intent outweigh volume.

## 6. Map to the funnel and to pages

### Search Funnel Mapping

| Stage | Buyer state | Keyword pattern | Intent | Content |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Awareness | Unaware of problem or of solution category | Trends, technology names, broad problems | Informational | Blog posts, explainers |
| 2 Research | Knows the problem, seeking information | "what is", "how does", comparisons | Informational | Guides, comparisons, FAQs |
| 3 Consideration | Weighing options | "best", "[A] vs [B]", "how to choose", "cost of" | Commercial | Service pages, case studies, pricing guides |
| 4 Intent | Selecting a provider | "[service] [city]", "[service] company", "hire [role]" | Transactional | Landing and service pages |
| 5 Action | Ready to contact | "[service] quote", "contact [company]", brand name | Transactional | Contact, quote, pricing |

East African adjustments (validate with your own analytics):
- Referrals often start searches: a WhatsApp recommendation leads to a search for a name or company, so branded terms and a strong business profile matter, and the home page should rank for the brand name.
- Online research often validates a referral rather than creating one, so pages must confirm credibility.
- Buyers spend longer in consideration: portfolio, industry evidence and local experience content speed conversion. Create validation content ("reviews", "testimonials", "case studies").
- Industry-specific terms (ERP for healthcare, software for NGOs) usually convert better than generic service terms.
- Verify device mix and input behaviour from the project's analytics; do not import regional voice-search assumptions without current market evidence.

### Assignment template
For every keyword record: funnel stage (1 to 5), intent (informational, commercial, transactional or navigational), content home (page or article), and conversion goal (read more, enquire, call, download). One primary keyword per page, several secondary and long-tail terms, and no two pages sharing a primary keyword (cannibalisation). Interpret each final keyword: what the searcher is really asking, what would satisfy them, and what would make them convert.

### Segment first
Group keywords by audience segment (for example "ERP for manufacturing" and "business plan writer" serve different buyers); each segment gets its own cluster and, ideally, its own content.

## 7. Target voice and AI answers

- Voice and assistant queries tend to be longer, question-shaped, conversational and locally framed, but length and behaviour vary by user, language, device and platform. Use observed queries, not a universal word range.
- Typed versus spoken forms: "IT consultant Uganda" versus "Who is the best IT consultant in Kampala?"; "website designer Kampala" versus "How much does it cost to get a website built in Uganda?"; "property management software Uganda" versus "What is the best software for managing rental properties in Uganda?"
- AI answer tools and featured snippets tend to draw from content that answers a specific question directly within the first fifty words or so, uses the question or a close variant as a heading, sits on a credible, well-structured page with suitable schema, and covers the topic in clear sections. Format: question as H2, a direct answer of about 40 to 60 words, then detailed explanation. Example: a page headed "What does an IT consultant cost in Uganda?" that states the answer first. Treat this as a hypothesis to test, since these systems change.

## 8. Research multilingual and Francophone markets

International research differs from single-market research: the same concept can use different words across markets and even cities; volume data is unreliable in many non-English markets; search behaviour varies; and cultural context decides which terms signal trust.

Francophone Africa is an opportunity to test: professionally structured French-language sites for ICT services are often scarce in West and Central Africa, the internet audience is large and growing, and vocabulary is regional (OHADA law, SYSCOHADA accounting, central-bank regulation terms). Verify the competition for each query before assuming an easy win.

Rules:
- Do not machine-translate keywords. "IT consultant" is not "consultant IT". Standard forms include consultant informatique, conseil en systèmes d'information, expert informatique, consultant en transformation numérique, ingénieur conseil.
- Do not use France-centric vocabulary by default; research the target country's variants.
- Geo-modify with target cities such as Abidjan, Dakar, Kinshasa, Douala, Lomé, Cotonou and Bamako.
- Conversational French examples: "Où trouver un bon consultant informatique à Abidjan ?"; "Quel est le prix d'un site web en Côte d'Ivoire ?"; "Comment choisir un développeur logiciel en Afrique ?"; "Qui peut m'aider à informatiser mon entreprise à Dakar ?"
- Pattern set: `[service en français] [city]`, `... Afrique de l'Ouest`, `... Afrique centrale`, `consultant [domaine] Afrique`.

## 9. Exclude wrong-fit queries (negative keywords)

Filter out queries that attract visitors who will never buy:
- "free", "template", "DIY", "download", "tutorial", "online course" (self-serve seekers).
- "crack", "torrent", "pirated".
- "job", "vacancy", "internship", "salary" (job seekers, for example "IT consultant salary").
- Academic-research phrasing; countries not served; government-tender terms unless procurement is a target.

Use them in paid campaigns and to prune content targets.

## 10. Measure and iterate

Rankings are an intermediate metric; traffic matters only if it converts. Ultimate metrics:
1. Organic sessions from the target keyword cluster (analytics plus Search Console).
2. Conversion rate from organic traffic (form fills, calls, WhatsApp starts).
3. Revenue influenced (did the person who found you via search become a client?).

Cadence:
- Weekly: Search Console impressions and clicks; note new unplanned queries as content opportunities.
- Monthly: rankings for Tier 1 and 2 terms; organic traffic trends; trace article traffic to its keywords.
- Quarterly: full performance review against baseline; new opportunities from Search Console; update the priority list; check competitor changes.
- Annually: full competitive re-analysis; new entrants; shifts in volume; new vocabulary buyers have started using.

Set benchmarks per tier from your own baseline (for example a Tier 1 Uganda cluster, a Tier 2 East Africa cluster, a Tier 4 Francophone cluster) and judge value by enquiry quality rather than raw visits; a handful of visits from an uncontested high-intent French term can produce valuable enquiries.

## 11. Tools

| Tool | Purpose | Note for smaller markets |
|---|---|---|
| Search Console | Actual queries, rankings, click-through | Real data for your own site; free |
| Keyword planner (ads platform) | Volume estimates | Treat with scepticism; may undercount |
| Trends tool | Trend direction, relative comparison | Useful for direction |
| Autocomplete and People Also Ask | Buyer vocabulary and questions | Reflect real behaviour |
| Question-mapping tools | Broad question sets | Free tiers are limited |
| Commercial suites (Ahrefs, SEMrush) | Competitor keywords, backlinks, audits | Regional data can be thin |
| Ubersuggest, Moz, Majestic | Ideas, difficulty, backlinks | Free tiers limited; check regional coverage |

Check current pricing, free-tier limits and regional coverage before recommending a tool.

## 12. New-keyword checklist

Before targeting a keyword, answer yes or no:
- [ ] Does it match how a real buyer in the target market describes the problem?
- [ ] Is there genuine search intent, not academic curiosity?
- [ ] Can we write content that answers the implied question?
- [ ] Does it map to a specific page or piece of content?
- [ ] Is competition weak enough to rank realistically?
- [ ] Is the trend stable or rising?
- [ ] Will it attract the right buyer, not wrong-fit visitors?
- [ ] Have commercial-intent signals been checked (ads, cost-per-click)?
- [ ] Is it distinct from other targets, avoiding cannibalisation?
- [ ] Can a title, H1 and description include it naturally?

More than two "no" answers means reconsider the keyword before writing content.
