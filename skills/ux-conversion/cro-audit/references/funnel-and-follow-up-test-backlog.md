# Funnel And Follow-Up Test Backlog

Parent skill: [cro-audit](../SKILL.md). Read when building a test backlog for a sales funnel, VSL page, webinar, order page, advertising creative, or follow-up email and call programme, after the core rules in [split-test-rules.md](split-test-rules.md) have been applied.

These are hypotheses that won in historical split tests on direct-response funnels. They are not rules. Past lifts do not transfer between markets, eras, or platforms, so none are quoted here. Log every idea using the experiment record in `content-copy/sales-copywriting/references/buyer-psychology-and-ethical-persuasion.md`, with guardrails for refunds, complaints, and lead quality.

## Testing discipline

- Look at the whole funnel. One weak step can erase the gains of strong steps elsewhere.
- Priority order for tests: the offer, the headline, CTA and order-button timing, price point, then format and layout.
- Test each traffic source separately; warm and cold visitors respond differently.
- Model what already works in the market before inventing variations; build a control, then try to beat it.
- Run to an adequate sample before declaring a winner.

## Headline and creative hypotheses

| # | Hypothesis | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| H1 | A headline stressing ease of starting and a trial outperforms one listing features. | Benefit and ease over description. |
| H2 | Name the condition or diagnosis the prospect already identifies with, rather than a symptom or general category. | Self-identification drives response in problem-led ads. |
| H3 | A specific ad naming a real person's verified result outperforms a generic curiosity ad. | Curiosity alone rarely converts; pair it with specificity. Permission required. |
| H4 | Story-led ad copy with a curiosity gap and a promised demonstration ("see how") outperforms feature-led copy. | Avoid clickbait that the landing page does not honour. |
| H5 | Plain, editorial-looking display ads outperform polished ad designs. | Must be clearly labelled as advertising where law or platform requires; never disguise ads as news. |
| H6 | Dark-background display ads stand out against typical page backgrounds. | Test per placement. |
| H7 | Humorous, relevant images on promotional social posts increase engagement. | Keep humour on-brand and inoffensive. |
| H8 | Aspirational or attention-holding images relevant to the audience outperform topical or political imagery. | Never use sexualised or stereotyping imagery; test images the audience finds relevant and respectful. |

## Video sales page hypotheses

| # | Hypothesis | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| V1 | A short FAQ at the end of the sales video, answering the top five support questions positively, lifts conversion. | Source the questions from support tickets. |
| V2 | Validate a simple slide-based video before paying for animation or illustration; then try to beat the control with higher production. | Cheap first, expensive only to beat a proven control. |
| V3 | Presenter voice characteristics (gender, accent, pace) affect response in some niches. | Test; do not assume. Avoid stereotyped casting decisions. |
| V4 | Video player size and placement affect completion; test a larger default width. | Check mobile rendering and page speed. |
| V5 | On one-time-offer pages, a genuine lesson preview video with the order menu appearing after a short viewing period outperforms long copy. | Always keep a visible decline link. |
| V6 | Copy length optimum shifts with context: a large customer photograph and testimonial headline can make shorter copy win. | Retest length whenever the hero changes. |

## Webinar hypotheses

| # | Hypothesis | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| W1 | Offer an immediate "watch the recording now" option on the registration page, placed first. | Many registrants prefer immediate access. |
| W2 | After a webinar, an order page with a recap of what was covered and the guarantee outperforms a bare order form. | Attendees forget; remind them. |
| W3 | A low-commitment "hold my place" step for undecided attendees (contact details now, decision later) increases eventual sales. | Must not take payment details without clear consent and terms. |

## Offer and order hypotheses

| # | Hypothesis | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| O1 | Offering a free trial beside the paid option increases sign-ups. | Measure paid conversion, not just trials. |
| O2 | A short name-and-email capture step between the sales video and the order page ("hot list") captures most interested buyers for follow-up without reducing sales. | Consent and privacy notice required. |
| O3 | Test lower and higher price points; a lower price can multiply volume enough to exceed higher-price revenue, and the reverse can also hold. | Compare revenue and margin per visitor. |
| O4 | Financing or pay-later options on higher-value offers increase sales, including when offered in follow-up. | Credit regulation and full cost disclosure apply. |
| O5 | A coupon-style design on social landing pages feels personally targeted. | Only for real discounts; no fake expiry. |
| O6 | A visible affiliate or partner sign-up link on the sales page recruits partners without hurting sales. | Measure both. |
| O7 | Countdown timers on order forms reached from follow-up emails can lift response. | Only when the deadline is real and the offer truly changes after it. |

## Phone and follow-up hypotheses

| # | Hypothesis | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| F1 | Show a phone number in the header for older or higher-anxiety audiences. | Staff the line during stated hours. |
| F2 | An exit prompt that addresses anxiety ("Prefer to order by phone? Call us") outperforms a discount exit prompt. | Framing the real concern beats price cuts. |
| F3 | Mechanisms that make buyers call you (a free resource claimed by phone) produce more valuable calls than outbound calling. | Inbound intent is stronger. |
| F4 | Call new buyers after the product has arrived and been used, but before they forget the purchase (historically around two to four weeks after purchase). | Tune the window to delivery and usage time. |
| F5 | Send marketing email from a named person at the company rather than the company name alone. | Keep the sender consistent. |
| F6 | Text-dominant emails outperform image-heavy emails for deliverability and response. | Many clients block images by default; check current client behaviour before stating figures. |
| F7 | For promotional emails, a direct, offer-first subject line can earn more revenue than a curiosity subject line despite fewer opens. | Judge by revenue, not opens. |
| F8 | Newspaper-article-style direct mail outperforms postcards for credibility. | Must be clearly identifiable as advertising; never imitate real news outlets. |

## Advertising delivery hypotheses (verify current platform behaviour first)

Platform placements, bidding models, device targeting, and frequency settings change often. Treat these as questions to test on the current platform, never as facts:

- Does in-feed placement outperform side-column or secondary placement for this offer?
- Does capping frequency per person per day improve conversion and reduce fatigue?
- Does excluding devices where the landing page or checkout performs poorly lower cost per lead?
- Which bidding model yields the lowest cost per qualified lead at this budget?
- Does sending ad traffic to a platform-native destination beat an external landing page for this offer?
- Do email opt-ins linked to a social or professional identity show higher long-term value than bare email addresses?

## Rejected patterns

Do not test or ship:

- Testimonials styled to look like social media posts that were never posted, or any invented or unattributed testimonial.
- Fake scarcity, fake timers, or deadlines that reset.
- Disguised advertising, or formats that imitate official or legal notices.
- Images or copy that sexualise, stereotype, or demean.
