# Quick Comprehension Tests and User-Testing Etiquette

Parent skill: [cro-audit](../SKILL.md). Read when a mockup, prototype or live
page needs a fast human check of whether visitors understand it, or when
planning small moderated tests with real customers.

These tests are cheap and fast. They do not replace analytics or experiments;
they catch comprehension failures before money is spent on traffic.

## Test battery

| Test | Method | Pass condition |
|---|---|---|
| Five-second recall | Show the page for about five seconds, hide it, ask "What is this site about? Who is it for? What can you do here?" | Most testers name the offer, audience and main action. Do not quote attention-span statistics; keep the test, drop the folklore |
| Point-to-it | Ask testers to point, as fast as they can, to the phone number, the main service and the contact route | Found without searching |
| Squint or distance | View the page from a distance or blurred | Key regions (header, main message, primary action) remain distinct |
| Unfamiliar-language | Machine-translate the page into a language the tester cannot read; ask them to find the contact information | Layout conventions alone lead them there |
| Reference-site lenses | Review admired sites three ways: first-impression like or dislike, time to find the phone number, easiest text to read | Findings recorded per lens in the design-reference log |
| First-sentence scan | Read only the first sentence of each paragraph | The page's argument survives |

## Moderated test etiquette

1. Test early and often: a rough sitemap, a mockup and the first working
   version are three separate test stages.
2. Recruit people who genuinely match the audience; thank them with a small
   incentive (in East African markets, airtime, data bundles or mobile money
   are practical). Record consent for any recording and say how it will be used.
3. Give scenario tasks ("You need a cleaner for your office every Friday
   morning; arrange a quote"), not instructions ("Click Services").
4. Do not reveal what you are testing. Give no verbal or non-verbal cues; even
   nodding steers people.
5. Ask general questions first, then specific ones. Never lead ("Did it load
   quickly?" is leading; "Tell me about that step" is not).
6. Watching is not understanding: follow observations with open questions.
7. Triangulate with a second method (analytics, session recordings where
   consented, or a second test round).
8. Test on low- to mid-range Android phones on real mobile data when that is
   the audience's reality.

## Output

A findings table: test, participant profile, task, observed behaviour, quote,
severity, recommended fix, owner. Mark any test not run as `NOT_ASSESSED`.
