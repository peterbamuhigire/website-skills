# Show Your Work: Authority-Building Practices

Parent skill: [they-ask-you-answer](../SKILL.md). Read when planning how the agency shows up publicly, week after week, without posturing as a guru or burning out.

The premise: self-promotion is the wrong frame; being findable is the right one. Share process, influences and learning generously, on ground the agency owns, and the audience finds the agency. For a premium engagement priced in the region of UGX 40m upwards, this file sits beside `legacy-guidance.md` (what to publish: the questions buyers already ask) and `content-inc-audience-model.md` (who to publish for). This file covers how to show up.

---

## 1. Decide the posture

- Position the agency inside a visible community of peers (designers, strategists, SEO practitioners, developers, writers), not as a lone expert. Follow them, read them, credit them and fill the gaps they leave.
- Take the learner's stance: curious, generous, unafraid to learn in public. Authority comes from visible curiosity, not claims of genius.
- Put the work online. Work that cannot be found does not count as evidence.

## 2. Decide what to publish

### Publish the process, not only the product
The finished site is a small part of the value. The inspirations, sketches, decisions, mistakes and iterations are where trust forms. Turn the invisible into something a buyer can see.

Artefacts to capture during every live build:
- Sketches, wireframes, IA diagrams, moodboards, influence boards.
- Drafts, including abandoned ones; the three directions considered and the two rejected.
- Screen recordings and screenshots of intermediate stages; pre-launch Lighthouse scores.
- The sector research brief written before the engagement; a redacted strategy memo that committed to a bet.
- Reading highlights, tool shots, desk photographs.
- Post-mortems: what was learned, what would change.

Publish these as they happen, not in a case study written six months late. They feed the proof library (`proof-library.md`, Proof of Strategic Rigour) and the short-post rhythm.

### Teach openly
Teaching adds value; it does not create instant competitors, because knowing a method is not the same as executing it. Publish playbooks: how a sector palette is chosen, how a conversion audit runs, how a bilingual site is structured, how a headline is written, how an engagement is priced, how a discovery call runs. This is the heart of the authority engine and matches the Big 5 approach in `big-5-article-templates.md`. Prospects who learn from the agency arrive pre-sold. Treat "trade secrets" sceptically: the method is rarely the moat.

### Share tastes and influences
Publish the reading list (with a line on why each item matters), the designers and typographers learned from, the sector books behind current engagements, and teardowns of admired sites. This attracts clients whose taste aligns and repels mismatches before a call. Credit like a museum label: what it is, who made it, when, why it matters, where to find more. If it cannot be credited, do not share it. Link out for what others do better.

### Tell stories, not adjectives
Work does not speak for itself; the story attached decides how it is valued. Use a three-act shape: where we have been, where we are, where we are going. In proposals and case studies (`case-study-template.md`), the last act hands the prospect the ending, so the prospect is the hero and the agency is the guide. Keep bios factual and short. Ban "aspiring", "amazing", "ninja", "guru", "rock star".

## 3. Filters before posting

Share: influences, tools, works in progress, methods, failures with the lesson, the work journal, current struggles.

Hold back: anything too personal or unhealed for public critique; anything that cannot be credited; anything failing the "so what?" test (is it helpful, entertaining or revealing?); anything likely to be regretted in the morning (leave it in the drawer for 24 hours).

Working rule: write as though every reader has the power to end the relationship.

## 4. Behave as a good citizen

- Read before writing; listen before speaking; be a fan before seeking fans; be interested to be interesting.
- Never open outreach with "see our latest post". Open with a specific reaction to the other person's work.
- Aim for roughly 70 per cent amplifying and engaging with others, 30 per cent own work. Comments must be substantive, never emoji-only. Most Dream 100 touches (see `expert-authority-framework.md`) should be acts of genuine interest, not pitches.
- Never ask for follow-backs. Drop contacts who consistently drain the conversation.

## 5. Handle criticism

Public work draws criticism, so condition for it.
1. Stay calm; a bad comment is not fatal.
2. Build tolerance by publishing often.
3. Treat criticism as material for the next piece.
4. Keep overly personal work offline.
5. Separate the work from identity.

Block trolls and delete abuse. Close comments on the agency site and moderate hard elsewhere. Treat "too expensive" and "I could do that on a free builder" as a filter, not a wound, and keep the wall of testimonials (`proof-library.md`) visible.

## 6. Charge properly, and capture email

Generosity is upstream (teaching, process). Pricing is downstream and needs no apology. Give freely, then offer a specific thing to buy, with a clear hire-us route. Always collect email addresses; email outlasts every platform. Accept opportunities that allow more of the right work and refuse those where more money means less of it.

## 7. Sustain the rhythm

Daily posting is aspirational for a busy founder. Use the achievable cadence:

| Frequency | Format | Channel | Founder time per week |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4 to 5 per week | Short post, 100 to 250 words | LinkedIn and site updates | About 2 hours |
| Weekly | Long-form anchor, 1,200 to 2,500 words | Site and newsletter | 4 to 6 hours |
| Monthly | Signature talk or roundtable recording | Site and video channel | About 3 hours |
| Quarterly | Cornerstone asset (benchmark report, teardown compilation) | Site and gated newsletter | About 20 hours spread over the quarter |

Total: 8 to 12 hours per week. Rules:
- Work first, then share. Sharing must not displace the work; if it does, set a 30-minute timer.
- Flow feeds stock: social posts become blog posts, then cornerstone reports, then eventually a book.
- Keep going. Year 1 is invisible, year 3 is a library, year 5 is a moat. Start the case study the day a build ships, and schedule rest periods, because burnout silently ends most content programmes.
- Finish work and begin the next piece without waiting for permission or feedback. Anyone unembarrassed by last year's output is probably not learning.

## 8. Quarterly audit

Answer yes or no:
- [ ] At least 4 short posts per week on the primary platform?
- [ ] A weekly long-form anchor published consistently?
- [ ] At least one process artefact shared this week?
- [ ] Every shared source credited?
- [ ] About page free of adjectives and shaped as three acts?
- [ ] At least one technique taught in detail this month?
- [ ] At least half of social engagement amplifying others?
- [ ] A mailing list in place and growing?
- [ ] Trend-chasing outside the niche refused?
- [ ] Still publishing at months 18, 24 and 36?

Fewer than 7 yes answers means the authority engine has stalled; diagnose the gap before the following review.
