# Sent-Items, FAQ and SOP Mining Sprint

Parent skill: [they-ask-you-answer](../SKILL.md). Read when a client (or the agency itself) needs its first evidence-backed buyer-question register quickly, or when the register has gone stale, and the richest evidence sits in answers the team has already written: sent emails, WhatsApp Business replies, support tickets, proposal clarifications, FAQs and internal procedures.

Complements [buyer question research method](buyer-question-research-method.md), which defines the research table and the strategic uses of buyer questions, and the idea-level "owned-material mining" section of `blog-idea-generator` (`references/topic-prompts-segments-events-and-owned-material.md`). This file adds a timed, repeatable sprint with provenance, confidentiality controls and a decision rule that turns each frequent question into the right page, series or sales asset.

## 1. Why sent items

Teams have usually answered their buyers' most important questions many times, in writing, at length. Those answers are first-party evidence of what buyers ask and a first draft of what the website should say. Mining them is faster and more reliable than inventing topics.

## 2. Authority and confidentiality gate (before anyone opens an inbox)

1. The account owner, not the agency, searches their own mailbox and WhatsApp Business history, unless the client has given the agency written authority to do so.
2. Export only the questions and the team's answers. Remove names, phone numbers, email addresses, account numbers, prices agreed with individual clients, and any health, financial or legal details about identifiable people.
3. Store the export in the client's approved location, with access limited to the sprint team, and delete working copies when the register is complete.
4. Personal data is involved: Uganda requires consent for collection and registration of data collectors (register PL-01); Kenya's Data Protection Act 2019 applies (register PL-02). Use the questions as themes; never publish a buyer's words in a way that identifies them without consent.
5. Internal procedures (SOPs) may contain security, pricing or supplier details. The process owner approves each SOP before it becomes public content.

If any item cannot be met, stop and use the interview and search methods in the research method file instead.

## 3. Sources to pull

| Source | What to look for |
|---|---|
| Sent email (last 6 to 12 months) | The longest replies; replies to questions beginning "how", "how much", "how long", "can you", "what is the difference", "do you" |
| WhatsApp Business chats and saved quick replies | Quick replies are a ready list of frequent questions; long typed answers show where quick replies fail |
| Support tickets or help-desk macros | Repeated issues and the macros used to answer them |
| Proposal and tender clarification questions | Questions procurement panels and buyers ask before deciding |
| Sales call notes and CRM "lost reason" fields | Objections and the questions behind them |
| Existing FAQ page and brochure | What the business already claims to answer, to compare with what buyers actually ask |
| Onboarding documents and SOPs | Steps buyers ask about ("what happens after I pay?") and candidates for "how we work" pages |

## 4. The two-hour sprint (after a 30-minute export)

| Time | Step | Output |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00 to 0:20 | Pull the 30 longest sent replies and the saved quick replies; list the question each one answers, in the buyer's wording | Raw question list with source and date |
| 0:20 to 0:45 | Cluster similar questions; count how many times each cluster appears and in how many separate conversations | Clustered list with frequency |
| 0:45 to 1:05 | Tag each cluster: buyer stage (first look, comparing, ready to buy, after purchase), Big 5 category (price, problems, comparisons, reviews, best-fit), and the fear behind it | Tagged list |
| 1:05 to 1:25 | Apply the decision rule in section 5 to each of the top clusters | Asset decision per cluster |
| 1:25 to 1:45 | Mark SOPs suitable for "how we work" content and note what must be removed | SOP shortlist |
| 1:45 to 2:00 | Enter the top five to ten items in the register with owner, proof needed and date | Register entries |

Repeat quarterly, or after any change to price, offer or process.

## 5. Decision rule: frequent question to page, series or asset

Apply in order; stop at the first match.

| If the question ... | Then create ... | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| is about price or cost | A pricing article or pricing-page section using the Big 5 pricing template | Use real price ranges or cost drivers only; see [Big 5 article templates](big-5-article-templates.md) |
| needs one short, stable answer (a fact, a policy, hours, payment methods) | An FAQ entry that links to the relevant detail page | Do not promise an FAQ rich result; those stopped showing in Google on 7 May 2026 (register CW-09) |
| is about "what happens next" or how the work is done | A "how we work" process page or section built from the approved SOP | Show steps, who does what and timings; remove security and supplier detail |
| has several sub-questions, or buyers ask follow-ups after the first answer | A series (a pillar page plus two to five linked articles) rather than a single post | Name the series and plan the order of reading |
| is asked mainly before a sales call or after a proposal | A sales-enablement asset used in assignment selling | Use the [assignment selling templates](assignment-selling-templates.md) and name the sales step it shortens |
| compares the business with an alternative or competitor | A comparison article that treats the alternative fairly | Legal review before naming any competitor |
| is about a problem or limitation of the offer | A "who this is not for" or problems article | Candour here builds trust; do not hide the limitation |

Frequency threshold: agree a practical threshold with the client (for example, a question that appears in at least three separate conversations in six months). Treat the threshold as a working rule and record it in the register; it is not a benchmark.

## 6. Register entry fields

Use the provenance fields the parent skill requires, plus the sprint fields:

| Field | Example (illustrative) |
|---|---|
| Question (buyer's wording, anonymised) | "Do you accept insurance for scans?" |
| Source and date range | WhatsApp Business chats, Mar to Aug 2026 |
| Frequency | 14 conversations |
| Direct language or inference | Direct |
| Buyer stage and Big 5 category | Ready to buy; price |
| Fear behind it | Unexpected cost at the counter |
| Asset decision (section 5) | FAQ entry plus insurer list on the pricing page |
| Proof or fact needed | Current insurer list from the finance office, dated |
| Owner and due date | Front-desk lead; 30 Sept |
| Confidence | High |

## 7. SOP to "how we work" content

1. Choose procedures buyers ask about: onboarding, what happens after payment, how a complaint is handled, how data is protected, how a site is tested before launch.
2. Keep the steps, the order, who is responsible and how long each step takes.
3. Remove internal tools, credentials, supplier terms, margins and anything the process owner marks confidential.
4. Write in first-person plural and active voice: "Within one working day, your project lead sends ..."
5. The process owner approves the public version, and the page is reviewed whenever the procedure changes.

Title patterns: "How we [process] for a new [client type], step by step"; "The checklist we run before every [deliverable] goes live"; "Why we [unusual practice], and what it saves you".

## 8. Worked example (illustrative)

A clinic group in Kampala exports six months of WhatsApp Business chats. The front-desk lead removes names and numbers. The sprint finds five clusters that appear in more than ten conversations each: the cost of an ultrasound scan, which insurers are accepted, weekend opening hours, paediatric services, and parking. Decisions: price goes to a pricing section with cost ranges approved by finance; insurers and weekend hours become FAQ entries linked from the contact page; paediatric services become a two-part series (what to expect, how to prepare a child); parking becomes a line on the contact page with landmark directions. The registration SOP becomes a "your first visit, step by step" page after the operations manager removes the internal system names.

## 9. Anti-patterns

- Copying a buyer's email into public content. Fix: use the question as a theme, anonymise, or ask consent.
- Counting one long thread as many questions. Fix: count separate conversations.
- Writing one article for a question that needs a series. Fix: apply the section 5 rule.
- Publishing an SOP with internal detail. Fix: process-owner approval before publishing.
- Treating the sprint output as the whole research base. Fix: add search, sales-call and interview evidence using the research method file.
