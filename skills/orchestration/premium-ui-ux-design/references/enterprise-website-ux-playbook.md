# Enterprise Website UX Playbook

This reference is self-contained. It distills the user's supplied Design Studio UI/UX
enterprise website article into website-engine rules.

Source used:

- https://www.designstudiouiux.com/blog/enterprise-website-design/

## Core Rule

Enterprise websites must help different stakeholders reach trustworthy proof quickly.
They are not larger brochure sites; they are decision-support systems for buyers,
partners, recruits, investors, procurement, regulators, and internal teams.

## Enterprise UX Inputs

Capture before design:

- Stakeholder groups and their decision jobs.
- Primary conversion paths: demo, inquiry, tender, application, booking, download, partner contact.
- Proof burden: case studies, certifications, compliance, team credibility, security, sector expertise.
- Content ownership and approval workflow.
- Languages, regions, and accessibility expectations.
- Performance and device constraints for target markets.
- Integration needs: CRM, analytics, CMS, search, forms, careers, investor relations, portals.

## Page System

| Page / Section | UX Job | Required Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Home | Route stakeholders and establish credibility | Category, value promise, proof, next actions |
| Solutions / Services | Explain fit by buyer problem | Outcomes, process, differentiators, FAQs |
| Industry / Segment pages | Show domain relevance | Sector proof, terminology, use cases |
| Case studies | Reduce buyer risk | Problem, approach, result, measurable proof |
| Resources / insights | Build authority and search depth | Original thinking, structured topics, internal links |
| Trust / compliance | Support procurement and regulated buyers | Certifications, policies, security, governance |
| Contact / conversion | Make next step low-friction | Clear form, routing, response expectations |
| Careers / culture | Support recruitment | Role clarity, values, team proof, process |

## UX Standards

- Navigation must support role-based discovery without hiding important pages in deep menus.
- Search is required when content volume makes browsing slow.
- Mega menus are acceptable only when categories are stable, scannable, and not overloaded.
- Proof must appear near claims; do not isolate all evidence on one testimonial page.
- Forms must explain what happens after submission and how soon the user should expect response.
- Enterprise pages should support skimming: headings, summaries, proof blocks, tables, and clear CTAs.
- Performance budgets still apply; enterprise does not mean heavy.

## Uganda And Africa Calibration

- Prioritize mobile, low-bandwidth, and clear contact paths even for enterprise sites.
- Show local trust signals where relevant: physical location, registration, known clients,
  sector memberships, payment/contact channels, and responsible people.
- Avoid global-enterprise theatrics when the buyer needs practical credibility and proof.
- For public-sector, NGO, education, healthcare, finance, and donor-facing websites, specify
  accessibility, document downloads, governance pages, and evidence trail requirements.

## Acceptance Gate

Before launch, confirm:

- Each priority stakeholder has a clear path from entry to proof to next action.
- Claims have nearby evidence.
- Content ownership and update cadence are documented.
- Search, navigation, forms, analytics, accessibility, and performance are tested.
- The site can support sales/procurement conversations without the founder explaining everything manually.
