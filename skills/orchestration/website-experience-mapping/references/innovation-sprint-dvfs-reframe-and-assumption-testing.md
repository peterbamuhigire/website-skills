# Innovation Sprint: DVFS, Reframe and Assumption Testing

Parent skill: [website-experience-mapping](../SKILL.md). Read when a client engagement is really a new service, product or digital experience (not only a website rebuild), when an established organisation keeps killing good ideas late, or when a one- to two-week discovery-to-concept studio sprint is being planned.

Deeper method detail and templates: `C:/wamp64/www/design-system-skills/skills/05-ux-process-research-and-psychology/enterprise-ux-process/references/arrive-framework.md`.

## 1. Success test: four dimensions, not three

Judge every concept on:

| Dimension | Question |
|---|---|
| Desirability | Do the main users want it enough to change behaviour? |
| Viability | Can it make or save money on sustainable terms? |
| Feasibility | Can it be built and run with available technology and skills? |
| **Strategic suitability** | Does it fit the organisation's strategy, risk appetite, funding band, capabilities, time-to-launch expectations and executive sponsorship? |

The fourth dimension is the one most often missed: projects that are wanted, profitable and buildable still die when they do not fit the organisation.

## 2. The six stages

1. **Audit** — set the scene: scope, market, technology and the organisation's strategic position.
2. **Research** — in-context user research: interviews, empathy maps, journey maps.
3. **Reframe** — synthesise research into a new perspective and scope. Keep this separate from Research; without it, research becomes a report nobody acts on.
4. **Ideate** — diverge widely, then converge on one or two concepts with their assumptions written down.
5. **Validate** — turn the concept into a business proposal and de-risk its critical assumptions with cheap tests.
6. **Execute** — prove, at limited scale, the capacity to earn revenue before full launch.

Skipping a stage is permitted but raises risk; say so explicitly to the client when it happens.

## 3. Load-bearing artefacts

| Artefact | Stage | Contents |
|---|---|---|
| Initial project outline | Audit | Problem, scope, constraints, sponsor |
| Strategic innovation profile of the organisation | Audit | Strategic intent, risk appetite, investment band, capabilities, champions, time-to-deployment. Keep it visible throughout. |
| Persona needs matrix | Reframe | Main persona(s) against needs, pains and current workarounds |
| Reframed project vision | Reframe | The new frame and scope; the client signs it off |
| Concept board | Ideate / Execute | Deliberately low-fidelity summary of the concept: user, need, idea, value, assumptions |
| Lean canvas | Validate | Business model on one page |
| Assumption map | Validate | Every assumption plotted by importance against evidence |
| Business case dashboard and test plan | Execute | Metrics, experiments and next 30/60/90-day steps for the client's delivery team |

## 4. Decision rules

1. **Frame for the main user.** An innovation cannot serve everyone; choose the frame that gives the main user a compelling solution, not everyone an adequate one.
2. **Keep concept boards rough.** Near-finished visuals make people judge polish instead of the idea.
3. **Test the riskiest assumptions first:** high importance, low evidence.
4. **Run a DVFS sanity check at least monthly during ideation.** Desirability leads, but any obvious viability, feasibility or strategic-fit deal-breaker ends the concept.
5. **Validation is not execution.** Validation reduces assumption risk; execution proves earning capacity. Merging them produces under-tested launches.
6. **Treat the process as risk control, not ritual.**

## 5. Two-week studio sprint template

**Week 0 (client homework and desk research):** client completes the project outline and strategic innovation profile; team drafts a PESTEL scan and a five-forces view.

**Week 1 — Audit, Research, Reframe**

| Day | Work |
|---|---|
| Mon | Audit workshop: confirm outline, profile and market scan; recruit five or six users |
| Tue–Wed | In-context interviews; build empathy and journey maps live; daily insight capture |
| Thu | Affinity mapping → personas → persona needs matrix → draft reframed vision |
| Fri | Client review and sign-off of the reframed vision (the main decision gate) |

**Week 2 — Ideate, Validate, Execute-lite**

| Day | Work |
|---|---|
| Mon | Diverge on many ideas; converge on two concepts; first DVFS sanity check |
| Tue | Produce two low-fidelity concept boards |
| Wed | Lean canvas, pre-mortem, assumption map |
| Thu | Wizard-of-Oz, paper-prototype or role-play tests with four or five users; update canvas |
| Fri | Final concept board, business case dashboard, two-page execute test plan; hand over |

Never cut, even when compressed: the strategic innovation profile, the reframe sign-off gate, the assumption map and the DVFS check.

## 6. Hand-off

Write the execute test plan so the client's delivery team can pick it up in their normal agile or lean cadence, then route website work to `page-builder` via this skill's map-to-sitemap procedure.
