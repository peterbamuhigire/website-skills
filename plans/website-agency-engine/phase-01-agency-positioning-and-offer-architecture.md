# Phase 1 - Agency Positioning and Offer Architecture

## Objective

Define exactly what kind of agency this repository is powering, who it serves, what outcomes it
sells, and why a client should pay $10k or more instead of hiring a cheaper designer or developer.

Without this phase, the rest of the work becomes generic production capability. Generic capability
does not create premium pricing.

## Why This Phase Is First

The analysis shows the repository already has broad capability. The real commercial risk is not
"can we make pages?" It is "can we package trust, strategy, differentiation, and business value
well enough that the work is bought as a premium offer?"

Premium websites are not sold as pages. They are sold as:

- revenue systems
- trust systems
- positioning systems
- sales enablement systems
- operational leverage for the client

## Core Outcomes

- choose 2-3 primary service niches where this repository has natural advantage
- define the premium website offer stack
- define what makes the agency different from template shops
- create pricing logic that supports $10k+ projects confidently

## Recommended Market Focus

Start with sectors where this repository already has a strong edge:

- professional services
- healthcare and trust-heavy businesses
- education and institutional organisations
- legal and consulting firms
- premium local businesses that need authority, not just traffic

These sectors buy trust, clarity, lead quality, and reputation. They are much easier to sell at
$10k+ than low-trust commodity brochure sites.

## Workstreams

### 1. Define the agency category

Decide the exact position:

- not "web design agency"
- not "developer for hire"
- but something closer to "conversion-focused website systems studio" or
  "authority website development for high-trust businesses"

The wording can change, but the commercial logic must stay the same:

- strategy first
- premium execution
- measurable outcomes
- modern technical quality
- SEO and conversion built in

### 2. Build the offer ladder

Create three tiers:

- Foundation Website Offer
- Growth Website Offer
- Authority Website System

The top-tier offer should naturally land above $10k because it includes:

- messaging strategy
- content architecture
- custom design direction
- premium page composition
- SEO foundation
- analytics and event setup
- launch operations
- post-launch optimisation window

### 3. Define scope boundaries

Premium projects stay premium when scope is controlled.

Write explicit rules for:

- page count assumptions
- copywriting depth
- revision rounds
- timeline structure
- handoff boundaries
- hosting and maintenance responsibility

### 4. Build pricing logic

Create a value-based pricing model with a baseline minimum project fee.

Pricing should be based on:

- commercial importance of the website
- decision complexity
- content complexity
- trust burden
- number of templates and bespoke sections
- SEO and conversion depth
- timeline pressure

### 5. Create the agency narrative

Write the internal one-paragraph explanation:

"We build high-trust, conversion-ready websites for businesses that cannot afford to look generic.
Every project combines strategy, messaging, premium design systems, SEO foundations, and rigorous
delivery standards so the website becomes a business asset, not just a digital brochure."

That statement should guide every later artifact.

## Repository Outputs Required

- `agency-positioning/` expanded with a premium website offer playbook if needed
- a standard pricing calculator or pricing rules reference
- a proposal positioning document for $10k+ projects
- a "who we serve / who we do not serve" qualification guide
- a project scope matrix by offer tier

## Success Criteria

- you can explain the agency in one sentence without sounding generic
- you can justify a $10k minimum with structure, not emotion
- each target sector maps cleanly to trust, SEO, and conversion needs
- low-fit leads can be rejected quickly
- all downstream skills can point back to a clear commercial promise

## Risks to Avoid

- targeting everybody
- selling code instead of outcomes
- underpricing because the offer is still vague
- bundling too much custom work into a fixed fee
- copying SaaS-agency positioning when your real edge is trust-heavy websites

## Reading Material To Buy And Provide At This Stage

- *Built to Sell* by John Warrillow: productizing services and removing custom chaos
- *The Business of Expertise* by David C. Baker: positioning and premium service design
- *The Win Without Pitching Manifesto* by Blair Enns: pricing power and authority posture
- *Value-Based Fees* by Alan Weiss: practical foundation for charging beyond hourly logic
