# Phase 3 - Strategy and Research Engine

## Objective

Turn the repository into a real strategy engine, not just a build engine. The agency must be able
to convert raw client information into positioning, page architecture, trust strategy, design
direction, and conversion logic before visual production begins.

## Why This Phase Matters

$10k+ websites are bought because clients believe the team understands:

- their market
- their customer
- their credibility problem
- their offer hierarchy
- their trust gaps
- their decision journey

This phase is where that belief is created.

## Core Outcomes

- create a standard website strategy method
- formalize how competitor and reference research is synthesized
- turn sector strategy into actionable page and messaging decisions
- build a reusable planning artifact that downstream skills can consume

## Workstreams

### 1. Website strategy brief

Create a single canonical strategy document per client containing:

- business objective
- primary audience segments
- decision criteria and objections
- trust requirements
- offer ladder
- CTA hierarchy
- positioning statement
- page architecture
- SEO topic and intent map
- visual direction constraints

This document becomes the source of truth for the whole project.

### 2. Competitive and reference synthesis

Use existing strengths in:

- `design-reference`
- `sector-strategies`
- `brand-alignment`
- `sales-copywriting`
- `ux-psychology`

But add a standardized synthesis method:

- what competitors say
- what they prove
- what they fail to prove
- where their UX creates friction
- where their trust story is weak
- where visual sameness creates an opening

### 3. Trust architecture

For premium websites, trust must be designed intentionally.

Create a trust inventory method for:

- credentials
- certifications
- case studies
- testimonials
- process transparency
- guarantees
- team proof
- partner logos
- compliance signals
- locality and legitimacy cues

This is critical for legal, consulting, healthcare, education, and premium service sectors.

### 4. Page goal mapping

Every page should have:

- one primary audience
- one primary conversion goal
- one proof burden
- one CTA hierarchy
- one SEO intent

This prevents bloated generic pages.

### 5. Research-to-design handoff

Create explicit outputs that `design-system` and `page-builder` can consume without ambiguity:

- mood and tone directives
- brand posture
- proof hierarchy
- content structure priorities
- required modules and components

## Repository Outputs Required

- a strategy brief template
- competitor analysis worksheet
- trust architecture checklist
- page goal matrix
- research synthesis template for reference sites and sector analysis

## Success Criteria

- every project has a strategy brief before design work begins
- page decisions can be traced back to business logic
- trust proof is collected deliberately, not patched in late
- reference analysis produces differentiated design direction, not imitation
- copy, design, SEO, and UX are aligned around one plan

## Risks to Avoid

- confusing inspiration with strategy
- letting aesthetics outrun positioning
- skipping trust design for high-stakes sectors
- treating SEO as keywords instead of search intent plus page purpose
- producing strategy artifacts too vague for execution

## Reading Material To Buy And Provide At This Stage

- *Obviously Awesome* by April Dunford: positioning clarity and competitive differentiation
- *They Ask, You Answer* by Marcus Sheridan: decision-stage content and trust-led sales
- *Building a StoryBrand* by Donald Miller: message hierarchy and clarity
- *Made to Stick* by Chip Heath and Dan Heath: sharper messaging and memorability
